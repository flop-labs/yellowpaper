## D-0401 — Consensus = stake-BFT-DAG (AlephBFT); PoW-GHOSTDAG deleted

**Decision Q1 = D2.** Status `[PARTIAL]` (devnet-wired; mainnet default stays BABE + GRANDPA).

**Context.** FLOP inherited an unintegrated PoW-GHOSTDAG fork (`consensus/`, a Kaspa subtree) whose safety
theorem assumes honest-majority **hashpower**. But PoUI gates block production on **stake + verified useful
work**, not hashpower — so the inherited Nakamoto safety proof is *void* and cannot be cited. A consensus
engine had to be chosen that is (a) consistent with PoUI's stake basis, (b) deterministic-final (M2M
settlement wants no reorg-aware pallet hacks), and (c) compatible with the live Substrate/FRAME execution
layer.

**Decision.** Adopt a **stake-based BFT-DAG of the AlephBFT class** under the FRAME runtime: a stake-weighted,
PoUI-gated committee produces a DAG of units ordered with deterministic, sub-second BFT finality. FRAME stays
the execution/state layer; only the layer beneath it (GRANDPA → `finality-aleph`) changes. The engine is
**adopted, not invented** — `aleph-bft` (peer-reviewed, AFT 2019) as a crates.io dependency, with a thin
in-repo `flop-finality-aleph` gadget driving it. The PoW-GHOSTDAG fork (~1238 files) is **deleted** and
de-referenced everywhere.

**Ratified constraints.**
- BABE + GRANDPA remain the **running mainnet default**; `pallet_aleph` is **inert** (`Enabled=false`) at
  index 104 until a *separate* mainnet-flip decision. This section stays `[PARTIAL]` until the four devnet
  proofs are captured.
- Committee = the PoUI gate: `stake ≥ min_stake` **AND** recent verified work, ranked by stake
  (`hp-consensus::select_committee`); snapshot per session by `pallet_aleph`.
- Finality-assuming pallets read a **finalized prefix** via `hp-consensus::FinalizedPrefix`, never the tip;
  a runtime `AlephFinalizedPrefix` returns the tip while disabled (today's behavior) and tightens to the BFT
  commit head once enabled — so the engine swaps by runtime upgrade *without* re-touching pallets. All
  finality-gated read-sites (vesting, HTLC refund, staking claim, compute-channel) are migrated as no-ops.
- Safety decomposes into **three independent sub-proofs** (§2.3): (1) Sybil cost, (2) BFT ordering safety
  under honest stake, (3) useful-work integrity. The machine-checked composition proof is **forthcoming**.
- Devnet (`flop-devnet` preset, 4 validators, AlephBFT enabled at genesis) is the validation vehicle; the
  four acceptance proofs (sub-second finality tracks tip; no reorg below finalized; committee tracks
  stake+work; safety under ≤¼ Byzantine stake) ship as a runbook, **not yet captured on infra**.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *(A) Generic DAG-native FRAME without a finality choice* — leaves the safety model undecided.
- *(B) GHOSTDAG-as-based-rollup sequencer* — a PoW L1 still contradicts PoUI; adds a bridge, two-domain
  state, ~3-day pruning, KIP-9 mass tax.
- *(C) Forking Substrate consensus* — perpetual fork tax.
- *Keeping PoW-GHOSTDAG* — safety proof void under stake-gating; never integrated with the state machine.

**Links.** Research: alephbft-integration-plan.md,
consensus-runtime-composition.md. PRs:
#44 (delete fork + on-chain plumbing + `hp-consensus`), #49 (compute-channel `FinalizedPrefix` migration),
#51 (node voter end-to-end + devnet harness). YP §§: §2, §2.3, §2.5, Appendix A.

## D-0402 — Sovereign FLOP-denominated validator DA

**Decision Q16.** Status `[PARTIAL]` (on-chain LIVE; validator service built, devnet e2e pending).

**Context.** Model weights (GBs), TEE quotes (5–10 KB), proofs, and I/O cannot live on-chain. The data must
live off-chain with a retrievability guarantee (data availability). R1 recommended *renting* tiered DA
(Arweave + EigenDA/Celestia). Two binding team constraints **amend** R1: the DA fee must be **FLOP-denominated
with no external currency/token/network dependency**, and **validators must be the storage nodes**. Separately,
today's TEE quote is served from the **miner's own HTTP server** — a withholding single-point-of-failure.

**Decision.** Build a **sovereign, validator-hosted, FLOP-denominated DA layer** as `pallet_da_registry`. The
chain stores only a `DataRef = (commitment, provider_id, retention_class)` per blob plus the assigned validator
subset and audit bookkeeping; the erasure-coded bytes live off-chain on a stake-weighted deterministic
validator subset (`hash(commitment) → subset`). Retrievability is enforced by **serve-or-slash** audits
(Filecoin-PoSt economics over FLOP's *own* bond). R1's *rent-don't-build* recommendation is superseded; its
byte/cost model and retention taxonomy are retained.

**Ratified constraints.**
- **No external token/network on the critical path** (no EigenDA/Celestia/Arweave). Fee
  `da_fee = f(bytes, retention_class)` is FLOP-denominated, reserved up-front, and routed to the storing
  subset (a new validator income stream; conservation unit-tested — no mint/burn in the DA path).
- **Reed–Solomon rate ½**, `R = 6` (k=3 data + 3 parity; any 3 of 6 reconstruct). `register_blob` rejects
  below an on-chain validator-count floor of `R`.
- **Serve-or-slash**: known-answer retrievability audits (Ghost-Task pattern) slash a **bounded** amount of a
  non-serving validator's bond via the `Liveness` path (bounded 1%, no eject/blacklist) — a transient miss is
  *not* conflated with fraud.
- **Two retention classes**: `Ephemeral` (≥ challenge window `W`, then permissionlessly prunable;
  dispute-conditional under sessions) for quotes/proofs/transcripts/I-O; `Persistent` (one-time deposit,
  re-replicated on validator churn via `reassign_blob`) for weights + dm-verity images. `W = 14 d` initially.
- **Quote delivery moves off the miner box** into neutral DA (`TaskQuoteRef`); admissibility gates on
  `DaRegistry::is_available(commitment)`, closing the withholding hole.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Rent EigenDA/Celestia/Arweave (R1's recommendation)* — re-introduces an external token, a bridge, and a
  third-party liveness dependency on the critical path; superseded by the two binding constraints.
- *DA fee priced in TIA/ETH-restaked via a volatility oracle* — external price oracle + token volatility;
  rejected for FLOP-denominated pricing.
- *Storing bytes on-chain* — buys nothing, costs state; the commitment is sufficient to bind a provider and
  fetch-and-check.
- *Accepted cost*: this is **more engineering than renting** — the explicit, accepted price of sovereignty.

**Links.** Research: da-sovereign-validator-design.md
(consolidated; amends R1, preserved in Appendix B). PRs: #41 (`pallet_da_registry` + runtime wiring +
consumer `DataRef`), #45 (off-chain `services/da-validator/` + oracle fetch-from-DA). YP §§: §5.1, §5.3,
Appendix A.

## D-0403 — Sessions + capacity-reservation settlement

**Decision Q17 (RATIFIED) + Q8.** Status `[PARTIAL]` (escrow primitive landed; bisection + SDK follow-ups).

**Context.** The per-task path is **4 consensus-ordered extrinsics** per inference, byte-bound at ~160 tasks/s
and serialising miner↔task matching through consensus — 10–100× under the agent-load target. Separately (Q8),
a paid task today acts as a *price*, not a *cap*: no refund on under-use, no top-up/abort on over-use, no
per-identity reservation cap.

**Decision.** Collapse a whole session to **2 inclusions** (`open_channel` + `settle`) via
`pallet_compute_channel`; inference streams off-chain over the existing RA-TLS attested transport. On-chain cost
→ **O(1)/session**; throughput then binds on GPU capacity. Settlement is a **capacity reservation**: escrow at
`open_channel` *is* the payment for reserved capacity. This is also the **Q10 escrow primitive** landing
(§6.4), kept generalizable.

**Ratified constraints.**
- **No refund on under-use or dissatisfaction.** `settle` pays the reserved amount minus a protocol burn;
  the only refunds are a **non-delivery timeout** or an **upheld fraud dispute**. (Supersedes the §12.4
  under-use row *for sessions*.)
- **Over-use** → abort or in-place **`top_up_escrow`** (Lightning splice-in; the aggregate-`G_n` accumulator
  already grows unbounded, only the escrow cap is raised).
- **Disputes = protocol fraud only** — wrong measured root, inflated `G_n` vs transcript, forged signatures,
  non-delivery. **Never output quality / dissatisfaction.** Resolution is optimistic settlement + bisection
  over the per-turn Merkle transcript root; non-response defaults the fraud verdict → slash via the existing
  `miner-slashing` forgery path + agent refund.
- **Challenge window ≤ DA retention `W`** (enforced in `integrity_test`: `ChannelDisputeWindow ≤
  DaEphemeralRetention`) so disputed leaves stay fetchable from neutral DA. Window = 7 d ≤ `W` = 14 d.
- Per-turn leaf `blake2_256(channel_id ‖ turn_index ‖ H(input) ‖ H(output) ‖ G_n)` signed by the enclave;
  advance input-hashing is dropped (it breaks multi-turn). Uniqueness = `session_id‖turn_index`; replay =
  monotonic counter.
- Measured-root binding (D-0404) verified at `open_channel` and `settle`. Per-turn `G_n` is **CLAIMED** until
  in-enclave measured `G_n` (D-0405) lands; the leaf already carries the field (drops in with no format change).

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Keep per-task 4-extrinsic RPC* — caps the network 10–100× under the agent-load target.
- *Metered-refund (gas semantics) for sessions* — explicitly **not** adopted for sessions: escrow is reserved
  capacity, not a metered price. (Gas-semantics reconciliation for the *per-task* path remains an open §12.4
  item, not ratified here.)
- *Adopt Tempo/an external L1 as the settlement rail* — informative, not a rail; re-introduces an external L1
  + bridge for the on-chain leg FLOP must own (escrow, `G_n`-vesting, slashing).
- *MPP as a literal Lightning import* — adopted only as a Phase-2 design pattern (fan a session across miners).

**Links.** Research: r2-payment-channel-sessions.md. PRs:
#46 (`pallet_compute_channel` + runtime hooks + measured-root gate), #48 (off-chain Rust/TS SDK: transcript,
crypto, miner host, agent client, dispute tooling). YP §§: §6.4, §12.4, §12.5, Appendix A. Conservation +
no-double-settle proven in `formal-specs/channel/channel-settlement.qnt`.

## D-0404 — Measured `model_hash`

**Decision Q2.** Status `[PARTIAL]` (measured-root pipeline LIVE; CVM producer side + on-TDX e2e pending).

**Context.** Today `model_hash` is a miner-passed value, so a miner can attest a genuine TEE while running a
*cheaper* model and *claiming* the expensive one's hash. A static file checksum proves a file *exists*, not
that the TEE *loaded* it. The PoUI claim is hollow until `model_hash` is a measured commitment to the loaded
weights.

**Decision.** Make `model_hash` a **measured** commitment via the open-weight-first pipeline **dm-verity root
→ RTMR3 → registry, fail-closed**: pack weights into a read-only EROFS image with a dm-verity Merkle tree
(4 KB blocks); the root is the commitment, verified by the kernel on every block read. Extend the root into
RTMR3 so it appears in the signed quote and flows into `report_data`. The chain stores expected roots per
`(model, precision)` and gates proof acceptance on `measured root ∈ registry`.

**Ratified constraints.**
- **Open-weight-first**: the root is *reproducible* — anyone re-packs the public weights and reproduces it, so
  it is a *public* commitment. Quantization changes the bytes → changes the root → each precision is a distinct
  commitment.
- **Fail-closed**: the dstack verifier parses the TDX event log (`no_std` JSON), **replays it to the quote's
  RTMR3** (binding events to the DCAP-signed quote), and **requires** the registry root among RTMR3 events
  (`Vk::V3`); a missing/unparseable log is **rejected**.
- **Registry enforcement default-on** (`EnforceMeasuredRoot`): registered roots always checked; unregistered →
  required when enforcing, grandfathered when off (rollout escape hatch).
- **Deferred** (`[PLANNED]`, hardware-gated): the miner CVM **extending the dm-verity root into RTMR3 at weight
  load** (the producing side of the event the verifier already checks) and the on-TDX e2e matrix. Until then
  `model_hash` in `report_data` remains a passed-in value.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Full ZK-of-inference* — prover cost scales 10³–10⁶× with proven FLOPs; the largest demonstrated LLM proof
  is a research artifact. Prove a *cheap* check, never the forward pass.
- *Static file checksum* — proves a file exists, not that the TEE loaded it.
- *Trust the miner-passed value* — the exact gap being closed.

**Links.** Research: verification-architecture.md; pattern: Tinfoil
Modelpack, Laminator ([arXiv:2406.17548](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.17548)). PR: #43 (`tools/modelpack`
reproducible packer + `pallet_model_registry` MeasuredRoots gate + dstack `Vk::V3` RTMR3 replay + producer
build script). YP §§: §3.2 tier table, §3.3, Appendix A.

## D-0405 — Measured `G_n` + reject-only throughput tripwire

**Decision Q4.** Status `[PARTIAL]` (op-count engine + tripwire LIVE; HW counter reads PLANNED).

**Context.** Billing on the parameter heuristic `2·P·N` is only ~2–3× accurate (omits the quadratic attention
term, embedding, MoE active-param routing) and is *claimed*, not measured — reward inflation at session scale.
Work must be **measured from the actual execution**.

**Decision.** Make `F_eff` (`G_n = F_eff/10⁹`) a **measured** op-count from the real execution, emitted by the
attested runtime and bounded by the chain from the **same shared, deterministic, `no_std` primitive**
(`hp_poui::flop_meter`, byte-identical Rust/Python/TS). Add a **reject-only throughput tripwire**: the on-chain
submit path rejects any claim whose implied throughput exceeds a network-peak ceiling.

**Ratified constraints.**
- **The tripwire never rewrites the billed amount.** `check_gn_tripwire` rejects if `gn·1000/latency_ms >
  GnThroughputCeilingGflopsPerSec` (default 2,000,000 GFLOPS/s) — **reject-only**, fail-closed,
  governance-togglable (`GnTripwireEnforced` default-on, root toggle). It never clamps `gn`.
- `effective_flops_exact` is **KAT-locked** against the analytic count (Llama-3-8B → 16, Llama-3-70B → 140
  `G_n`/token) with cross-language parity; the heuristic is a **sanity floor only**.
- Runtime emission into `report_data` is LIVE (attestor folds measured `g_n` into the TDX binding).
- **Deferred** (`[PLANNED]`/`[PARTIAL]`, hardware-gated): in-enclave CUPTI/DCGM/nvml counter reads; per-GPU
  MLPerf ceiling + under-report floor (need attested GPU-id / token counts via the transcript-leaf `g_n`);
  Tier-3 re-execution recompute.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Bill on the `2·P·N` heuristic* — ~2–3× inaccurate; demoted to a sanity check only.
- *Trust the claimed `G_n`* — reward inflation; replaced by measured emission + reject-only bound.
- *Clamp implausible claims to the ceiling* — rejected; a silent rewrite of billed work is itself an attack
  surface. Reject-only keeps the billed amount honest.

**Links.** Research: verification-architecture.md,
r3-throughput-ceiling.md. PR: #47 (`hp_poui::flop_meter` + Python
mirror + on-chain tripwire + `docs/hardware/G_N_MEASUREMENT_TEST_MATRIX.md`). YP §§: §4.2, §4.3, Appendix A.

## D-0406 — M-of-N oracle attestation threshold

**Decision Q7 / G-02.** Status `[LIVE]`.

**Context.** A single off-chain oracle checks the TEE attestation and signs it for the chain — the only
*currently live* verification tier. A single (or unanimous-of-a-small-set) oracle is a trust/censorship/
collusion bottleneck and a single point of capture.

**Decision.** Require an **M-of-N quorum** of distinct registered oracles to agree on task/verdict/values
before crediting a proof, via a new `submit_oracle_attestations` bundle extrinsic. The threshold is a
**governable** runtime parameter, default **2-of-3**.

**Ratified constraints.**
- `DefaultOracleThreshold` = 2 (of 3); `OracleThreshold` storage is root-governable via `set_oracle_threshold`.
- `check_threshold()` validates ≥M **distinct** registered oracles agree on the same task/verdict/values;
  duplicate-oracle and disagreement bundles are rejected (`OracleThresholdNotMet`,
  `OracleAttestationMismatch`).
- The single-attestation path credits **only** when threshold = 1 (backward-compatible default for unit mocks).
- The threshold sits on top of the existing ~213 B attestation (many ∧ small — decentralizes without inflating
  the per-task footprint).

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Keep a single-oracle (or unanimous small-set) path in production* — single point of capture; the gap being
  closed.
- *Full oracle-set decentralization / threshold-BLS aggregation now* — deferred; the governable M-of-N quorum
  is the shipped step, with broadening the set a later decentralization move.

## D-0407 — Leased model storage (supersedes "Persistent = stored forever")

**Decision (ratified).** Status `[LIVE]` (pallet + runtime + service + Quint landed). **Supersedes** the
`Persistent`-tier (never-expire, one-time deposit) aspect of [D-0402](#d-0402--sovereign-flop-denominated-validator-da);
all other D-0402 constraints stand unchanged.

**Context.** D-0402 gave model weights a `Persistent` retention class: a one-time deposit, stored **forever**.
Model relevance is only 6–12 months, so a never-expire rule makes the validator set's weights working set grow
monotonically — dead GB-scale weights accumulate indefinitely (design §6.1: ~25 GB/validator at N=800 for the
weights tier, and only ever upward).

**Decision.** Replace `RetentionClass::Persistent` with **`Leased`** (clean rename; no live network to migrate,
no back-compat). A model's registry deposit buys a **fixed storage term**; **anyone** may top up to extend it;
an expired lease is pruned after a grace period — but **never while the model is in use**; and pruning is
**recoverable** because the dm-verity root is deterministic (re-upload ⇒ same commitment ⇒ same identity).

**Ratified parameters (governance-adjustable).**
- **Default lease term 180 d**; deposit = `bytes × (term / LeaseTermUnit) × LeasedPricePerBytePerTerm`, paid in
  FLOP, routed to the storing subset as today; **not refundable** on early abandonment (capacity-payment model).
- **Top-up granularity / term unit 30 d** (`top_up_lease(commitment, additional_term)` — callable by **anyone**).
- **Expiry warning 30 d** before `lease_end`, emitted **once**, **lazily** (permissionless `poke_lease` + the
  `pin` access path) — not `on_idle`, to avoid `O(N)`-per-block scanning of a growing registry.
- **Grace period 14 d** after expiry (= the DA challenge window `W`) before a lease is prunable.

**Usage guard.** A refcount (`pin`/`unpin`) on `da-registry`: `compute-channel` pins the model's weights
`DataRef` at `open_channel` and unpins on every terminal path (settle / finalize / timeout / fraud).
**Prune eligibility = `lease_end + grace` elapsed AND `pin_count == 0`.** Opening a session against an expired
(or in-grace) weights lease **fails** (`ModelWeightsLeaseExpired`) — top up first. An expired-but-pinned blob
emits `LeaseExpiredButPinned` and stays until unpinned; storage validators keep serving and keep the original
fee, and the agent is **not** slashed for the lease debt. Identity (`ModelWeightsRef` + `MeasuredRoots`)
survives a prune, so `reactivate_blob` restores the bytes at the **same** commitment without re-registering
metadata.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Keep "Persistent = forever"* — unbounded, monotonically-growing validator storage for models no one uses.
- *Refund on early abandonment* — inconsistent with the reserved-capacity/no-refund payment model (D-0403).
- *`on_idle` expiry sweep* — `O(N)` per block over all leased blobs; the permissionless lazy `poke_lease`
  (called by a publisher / patron / keeper, with the DA service's `da_leases_expiring` metric flagging when)
  is `O(1)` per touch.
- *Lease auctions / pricing curves* — out of scope; flat per-byte-per-term price for now (economics agent owns
  pricing).

**Links.** Research: da-sovereign-validator-design.md §9.
Formal: `formal-specs/da/da-retrievability.qnt` (invariants: pinned-never-pruned, no-prune-in-grace,
extend-before-prune). Code: `pallet_da_registry` (lease lifecycle + `pin`/`unpin`), `pallet_compute_channel`
(open gate + pin/unpin), `pallet_model_registry` (`Leased` weights + `weights_commitment`), `services/da-validator`
(prune worker + lease metrics). YP §§: §5, Appendix A.

## D-0408 — Single validator-staking system (`pallet_validators` is the authority source; `pallet_staking` removed)

**Decision (ratified).** Status `[PARTIAL]` (no-back-compat ratified — devnet recreated from scratch).
**Supersedes** the runtime's use of Substrate `pallet_staking` + Phragmén election + eras as the `SessionManager`.

**Context.** FLOP ran **two disjoint definitions of "validator"**. Substrate `pallet_staking` (Phragmén, 28-day
bond, nominators, eras) was the `SessionManager` selecting today's BABE/GRANDPA authorities, while the custom
`pallet_validators` owned everything FLOP-specific: registration, the 305,505 FLOP min stake compounding 9%/yr,
slashing, SPEC-021 rotation, the PoUI committee (`poui_committee`), DA assignment (`DaValidatorPool`), and what
`pallet_aleph` reads. An account could be a session authority **without** being a registered FLOP validator, and
vice-versa — so slashing, DA assignment, and the PoUI gate all read a set the consensus layer ignored.

**Decision.** Make `pallet_validators::ActiveValidators` the **single source of truth** for the validator /
authority set. **Remove `pallet_staking` from the runtime** — and with it the on-chain Phragmén election
(`OnChainExecution`/`SequentialPhragmen`), the eras machinery, the bags-list voter list (`VoterList`), and the
election-provider support. A thin runtime adapter (`ValidatorSessionManager`) feeds the `pallet_validators`
active set into `pallet_session::historical` each session. Native validator **delegation** (cap vs self-stake,
commission, proportional rewards) stays on `pallet_validators` (it predates the removal — the whitepaper promise
survives). Equivocation offences route to `pallet_validators::slash`. No back-compat: devnet genesis recreated.

**Ratified constraints.**
- **Session authorities == `pallet_validators::ActiveValidators`.** `SessionManager::new_session` returns the
  active set; historical `FullIdentification` collapses to the account id (no `Exposure`/exposure pages).
- `pallet_staking`, `pallet_bags_list` (`VoterList`), the election-provider `OnChainExecution`/`SequentialPhragmen`,
  and the era-payout path are **deleted** from `construct_runtime!` and all `Config` impls. `SessionsPerEra`,
  `BondingDuration`, `SlashDeferDuration`, and the Phragmén bounds are removed.
- The 28-day era bond is replaced by `pallet_validators` freeze/thaw with `UnbondingPeriodBlocks` (no eras);
  delegation unbonding stays 21 d.
- `pallet_authorship::EventHandler` and `pallet_offences::OnOffenceHandler` **repoint** from `Staking` to
  `pallet_validators` adapters (block-author reward points → consensus split; offence → `slash`).
- `pallet_delegation_cap`'s `StakingInterface` (`validator_self_stake` / `validator_total_stake` /
  `is_validating`) repoints from `pallet_staking::{Ledger,ErasStakers,Validators}` to
  `pallet_validators::Validators` (`self_stake` / `total_stake` / `contains_key`).
- `ScoreProvider`/`VoterList` consumers and the Phragmén nominator quota are removed; **delegation** is the
  nomination replacement (the BFT-DAG committee, D-0401, ranks by stake + verified-work, not a Phragmén solve).
- `proxy::ProxyType::Staking` repoints to the `pallet_validators` + delegation call surface.
- The block **consensus reward** (90% pro-rata to active **and online** validators, 10% proposer bonus) is
  computed over the `pallet_validators` active set in `pallet_block_rewards` and **auto-compounded into locked
  stake** — consistent with the 9%/yr min-stake compounding and "rewards may not be unstaked while active".

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Keep `pallet_staking` as `SessionManager` and sync the two sets* — two sources of truth, perpetual drift; the
  exact bug being closed.
- *Keep Phragmén for authority selection over `pallet_validators` stake* — re-introduces eras / nominator
  machinery the BFT-DAG committee (D-0401) does not use; `poui_committee` already ranks by stake + verified-work.
- *A migration path* — none needed; no-back-compat ratified, devnet recreated from scratch.

**Links.** Code: `pallet_validators` (SessionManager source + delegation + `slash`), `runtime/zkverify`
(`ValidatorSessionManager`, authorship/offence adapters, `construct_runtime!`), `pallet_delegation_cap`
(`StakingInterface` repoint), `pallet_block_rewards` (consensus split). YP §§: §6.1, §11.

## D-0409 — Validator slashing-table reconciliation (code ↔ whitepaper draft)

**Decision (ratified).** Status `[PARTIAL]`. Reconciles the `pallet_validators` slashing table with the
whitepaper validator-section draft; **each divergence is resolved explicitly** (the table is now the single
authority, mirrored into the yellowpaper §6.1).

**Context.** The code's `SlashingReason` table and the whitepaper draft diverged on five points, and the draft
promised liveness mechanics (jail, extended-downtime kick) that **did not exist in code**.

**Decisions — divergence by divergence.**
1. **Liveness** keeps **1 %** (no eject) **and gains the two promised tiers** absent in code:
   - **Jail** — offline `> 300` blocks → **1 % slash + jailed** (removed from the active set, cannot author); a
     manual `un_jail` extrinsic is permitted only after **≥ 1 h** (`MinJailBlocks = 3 600`).
   - **Extended downtime** — offline `> 24 h` (`ExtendedDowntimeBlocks = 86 400`) → **5 % slash + kicked** from
     the active set; **re-entry requires a full stake top-up** to the current minimum, not just an un-jail.
2. **Collusion** stays **100 % burn + eject + blacklist** (code), **not** the draft's *50 % with half returned
   after 180 d*. Signing a fake `G_n` proof is **evidence-backed fraud**, the same class as `EvidenceForgery` —
   not a recoverable liveness lapse. **The whitepaper draft must change to 100 %.** This is the one judgment call
   (surfaced in the PR); implemented at **100 %** per the ratified default.
3. **Equivocation** stays **100 % burn + eject** (code) and is now **automatically reachable**: BABE/GRANDPA
   equivocation offence reports route to `pallet_validators::slash(Equivocation)` through the runtime offence
   handler (previously the slash was **call-only** — no automated path).
4. **`EvidenceForgery`** (100 % burn + blacklist) and **`TeeAttestationFailure`** (100 % burn + blacklist) —
   code-only tiers — are **kept** and **added to the whitepaper draft's table** (the security model rests on
   them), *not* deleted to match the draft.
5. **SPEC-021 rotation** now **executes on-chain**: `RotationIntervalBlocks` is **consumed in `on_initialize`**
   (was config-only); the bottom-N are ranked by a **performance metric = verified-work + liveness, not stake
   size**, ejected, and the top-N from the queue promoted.

**Ratified constants.** `JailDowntimeBlocks = 300`, `MinJailBlocks = 3 600` (1 h), `ExtendedDowntimeBlocks =
86 400` (24 h), `ExtendedDowntimeSlash = 5 %`, `Liveness = 1 %`,
`Collusion = Equivocation = EvidenceForgery = TeeAttestationFailure = 100 %`.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *The draft's Collusion 50 % / half-returned* — under-deters provable proof-forgery; treats fraud as a liveness
  lapse.
- *Deleting the `EvidenceForgery` / `TeeAttestationFailure` tiers to match the draft* — removes the only on-chain
  penalty for the two TEE / verification-fraud classes FLOP's security rests on.
- *Stake-size rotation metric* — rewards capital, not useful work; rotation ranks on verified-work + liveness.

**Links.** Code: `pallet_validators` (`jail`/`un_jail`/`kick` lifecycle, extended-downtime tier, rotation

## D-0410 — FLOP identity: spot money + opt-in bonded reservations, with a state-dependent survival governor

**Decision (ratified).** Status `[ACCEPTED]` (2026-06-11).

**Deciders:** Economics/protocol track (ratifies team-discussion §F1/§F2; depends on Q1=D2 consensus, ratified)

**Context docs:** F2 threat model · F1/F2 solution research · team discussion §F1–F3 · miner-validator-economics

**Context**

Two coupled fundamental questions were left `[OPEN — needs a position]`:

- **F1.** What does a *held* FLOP guarantee about future compute? Compute is a **non-storable flow**
  (like electricity) — a token is money in a compute economy, **not** a warehouse receipt. "Hold for
  advanced intelligence later" is therefore either a market bet or an enforceable contract — we must say
  which.
- **F2.** What causes a network exodus/collapse, and what dampens it? FLOP is a two-sided reflexive market;
  the death spiral (price ↓ → miner margin ↓ → exit → demand ↓ → price ↓) is the existential risk and was
  **not written down**.

The solution research showed both questions share **one load-bearing primitive** — a **bonded
capacity-reservation** — and that the parts already exist in-repo (`pallet_miner_staking`,
`pallet_compute_channel` escrow #46, Synthetic/Ghost Tasks, `pallet_miner_slashing`, hw-calibration `B_p`).

**Decision**

**1. FLOP identity = option (c), "both, layered."**
FLOP is **spot money by default** (liquid store of value; future worth = liveness + demand + the
intelligence-appreciation axis), **plus an opt-in, bonded, redundant, liveness-proven capacity-reservation**
instrument for parties who need *enforceable* future delivery. The token never carries delivery semantics;
a separate reservation object does (separation between levels + in time).

**2. F1 reservation = decision F1(c) "both, layered" — build as integration, not greenfield.**
A standing reservation = **pre-funded `compute_channel` escrow + a miner availability bond**, with
**Ghost-Task liveness** as the continuous delivery proof (Filecoin-PoSt analogue) and **slash-on-
failure-to-serve** reusing `pallet_miner_slashing`. Bounded by **duration caps + an over-collateralization
ratio** (counterparty-risk bounds from the F1 doc). This is the *committed minimum-viable-liveness core*
that also serves F2.

**3. F2 survival = a state-dependent governor, NOT permanent lock-in.**
Dampers engage only near collapse thresholds (preserving permissionless exit). Commit, **default OFF,
governance-settable**:
- **utilization-indexed emission damping** (lift miner reward when utilization is low — "missing money"
  fix; indexed on utilization, **never price** — anti-Terra), and
- **dynamic burn schedule** (10% → 15% → 25% by era).
- Plus the **committed core** (the F1 reservation) and a **mass-slashing circuit-breaker**.

**4. Anti-Terra invariant.** No mechanism may let a price drop mechanically expand supply. The governor
keys on margin/utilization/churn only.

**5. Early-warning metrics** (F2 threat model §3)
are promoted to indexer/monitoring and arm the governor via a composite exodus index.

**Status of implementation (this change set)**

| Item | State |
|------|-------|
| F2 threat model (unified failure chains + metrics + go/no-go) | **Done** — `whitepaper/research/f2-exodus-threat-model.md` |
| Governor params (emission damping + dynamic burn), default OFF | **Done** — `params/flop-protocol-params.yaml::governor`, mirrored in sim params + `check_params.py` |
| Governor modeled in simulator + 4 stress scenarios + identity test | **Done** — `ai-agent-simulator/src/model/{index,parameters,types,stress-scenarios}.ts`, `tests/unit/governor.spec.ts`, `just run-simulator-stress` |
| F1 bonded reservation **pallet** (escrow + bond + Ghost-Task proof + slash) | **Follow-on** — design ratified here; reuses compute-channel/staking/slashing |
| Governor **Rust runtime hook** (emission-pallet damping + governance origin) | **Follow-on** — params surface defined, default OFF |
| Indexer metrics + composite exodus index | **Follow-on (Q12 task)** |

**Consequences**

- **Positive:** a credible, differentiated "store compute for later" product (vs cash-settled TradFi
  futures); the existential failure mode is now written, monitored, and has armed dampers; the build is
  mostly integration of shipped pallets.
- **Cost / risk:** reservations add over-collateralization/oracle complexity + a default-risk surface
  (bounded by duration caps + MtM vs the external spot index — F3 interface, noted not built); governor
  adds emission-path complexity (mitigated: default OFF, identity-verified).
- **Open, surfaced not decided here:**
  - **Validator profitability vs the 90/0/10 fee split.** params route task fees 90% miner / 0% validator /
    10% burn; the economics doc shows validators unprofitable on block rewards alone if authoritative.
    **Flagged for governance** (80/10/10, or raise attestation fee) — *not changed by this ADR.*
  - **Option-A vs Option-B** perpetual idle-miner availability pool (who funds the guarantee). Interim =
    synthetic-tasks demand floor (#26).
  - **F3** price-oracle vs external GPU spot index — interface noted, not built.
  - **Q13** VCG auction — follow-on after F1 ships (out of scope here).

**Alternatives considered**

- **(a) Pure spot money** — simplest/honest but under-delivers the "hold for later" pitch; no enforcement.
- **(b) Spot + bonded forwards only** — enforceable but heavier (oracle + duration caps) without the
  layered default. (c) dominates (a) and (b) by keeping spot as the default and reservations opt-in.
- **Permanent exodus lock-in (ve-style)** — rejected: kills permissionlessness (PC-A) and risks the
  illiquidity trap; the governor achieves stability without removing exit.

## D-0412 — DA is a validator duty (no per-byte DA fee); supersedes the fee provisions of D-0402/D-0407

**Decision (ratified).** Status `[LIVE]` (pallet + runtime + service/indexer consume-side + Quint landed).
**Supersedes the fee provisions of** [D-0402](#d-0402--sovereign-flop-denominated-validator-da) (the
FLOP-denominated `da_fee` reserved up-front and routed to the storing subset) **and**
[D-0407](#d-0407--leased-model-storage-supersedes-persistent--stored-forever) (the leased deposit =
`bytes × term × per-byte-per-term`, routed to storers, non-refundable). **All non-fee provisions of both
decisions stand unchanged** — sovereignty (no external token), RS rate-½ `R = 6`, serve-or-slash audits,
retention classes, assignment, re-replication, lease term/warning/grace/pin/reactivation lifecycle.

**Context.** D-0402 modeled DA as a *separately charged resource*: `da_fee = f(bytes, retention_class)`
reserved from the publisher/agent and routed to the storing validator subset as a "new validator income
stream"; D-0407 priced the leased tier the same way (`bytes × term × per-byte-per-term`, non-refundable).
But storing and serving DA shards is **part of what a validator is already paid to do** — like authoring
blocks and casting finality votes, all of which are compensated by the validator share of block rewards +
transaction fees, not by metering each action. Charging per byte on top double-counts the duty, prices a
public good the protocol *needs* used (quotes off the miner box, transcripts in neutral DA), and adds an
income stream whose conservation/rounding is pure surface area. The enforcement that the duty is actually
performed already exists and is **independent of any fee**: serve-or-slash.

**Decision.** Treat DA as a **validator duty, not a charged resource.** Remove per-byte DA fee charging and
DA-fee routing entirely. Compensation is the **validator share of block rewards + transaction fees** (no
reward-split change is made here — see the economics flag below). Spam is bounded structurally, per class:

1. **Ephemeral** (quotes / transcripts / per-session I-O): **no charge of any kind.** Spam control is that
   they are creatable **only** through fee-bearing protocol flows (task submission, session open incl.
   `force_open`'s escrowed path) — `Pallet::store_ephemeral` is an **internal API** (origin/caller-trait
   gated), reachable from composing pallets, **not** a free-standing public extrinsic.
2. **Leased** model weights: **no storage fee**, but a **refundable anti-spam deposit** — *not* validator
   revenue. `deposit = bytes × DepositPerByte`, **held** at registration under a dedicated `HoldReason`
   (`LeaseDeposit`) and **returned in full** on prune (expiry + grace, unpinned), on the service-driven
   permissionless prune, or on a publisher's **voluntary withdrawal** (`withdraw_lease`). `top_up_lease`
   becomes a **free** term extension (transaction fee only); `reactivate_blob` re-locks the deposit. The
   deposit is **term-independent** (a held bond, not a capacity price), so extensions cost nothing.
3. **Validator compensation.** `route_fee` / `ProviderEarnings` / `TotalDaFees` and the per-byte price
   constants are **deleted** (the leased per-byte constant is *repurposed* as deposit sizing,
   `DepositPerByte`). Serve-or-slash (unchanged) is the enforcement that the already-paid-for duty is
   performed — the **same model as liveness slashing for block production**.
4. **Unchanged:** serve-or-slash audits, stake-weighted assignment, re-replication on churn, retention
   classes, and the full D-0407 lease lifecycle (expiry, warning, grace, pin/unpin, reactivation).

share**. The validator-profitability analysis in
`miner-validator-economics.md` already flags the 90/10
miner/validator split as thin; deleting the DA income stream removes a (small, prospective) offset. This
decision does **not** change reward splits — "**validator share sufficiency**" is recorded here as an

and therefore **not** a substitute income stream; it is anti-spam collateral only.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Keep the per-byte `da_fee` (D-0402) / leased per-byte-per-term price (D-0407)* — double-charges a duty

## D-0413 — C5 value-coupled security via the stake floor (not reward)

**Decision (ratified).** Status `[PARTIAL]` (principle ratified here; the `V_booked` retarget wiring + the
Lean C5 theorem land in **ENG-31** parts 2–3, gated on this). Resolves **C5** of the network-value
capstones (network-valuation §4).

**Context.** C5 requires the security budget to scale with the network's **realized effective value**
`V_booked = a·n_eff + b·n_eff² + c·v̄·D₂` (`NetworkValue.lean`) —
`n_eff`/`D₂`, *not* headcount or Reed's 2ⁿ, so Sybils (`nEff_sybil_collapse`) and concentration cannot
inflate it. `CoCVoC` (`Security.lean`) proves deterrence
is a **stake** condition: `coc_ge_voc_iff_stake_ge` holds iff per-validator stake `S ≥ requiredStake`, with
`requiredStake = m·VoC/(θ·N·σ·δ)`. VoC has two parts — a **reward-capture** term `mult·θ·α·B·R` (∝ block
reward `R`) and a **value-extraction** term `mevF` (MEV / ransom / theft ∝ the value the network secures =
`V_booked`). Because `R` is a fixed emission schedule (SPEC-018 halvings), the reward-capture term does not
track value; at bootstrap it *dominates* VoC (numeric anchor `stake_at_current_params`: at `R = 100`,
reward-capture ≈ 322M FLOP/yr ≫ `mevF` ≈ 1.2M ⇒ `requiredStake` ≈ 1.2M FLOP), but **as the network matures
the value-extraction term `mevF ∝ V_booked` overtakes the fixed reward-capture term** — a high-value network
is a high-value target — so for a grown network `VoC ∝ V_booked` and hence `requiredStake ∝ V_booked`. The
gap: nothing couples `requiredStake` to `V_booked`; `validator_min_stake` is pegged to `R` (and is the stale
static 305,505, acknowledged ~4× low at `R = 100`).

**Decision.** Scale the security budget with value through the **stake requirement**, coupled **directly** to
`V_booked` — **not** by issuing or routing more reward. As realized effective value grows, the protocol raises
the validator stake floor (and committee-admission bar) so committee stake-at-risk tracks `V_booked`;
validators post additional FLOP to remain in the committee. This is the literal reading of C5 — *"validator
stake must grow with the size of the network it secures."* The reward intermediary the prep candidates
assumed is unnecessary: `requiredStake ∝ VoC`, and `VoC ∝ V_booked` *directly* via the value-extraction term.

**Ratified constraints.**
- **Coupling is on the stake side, exogenous to emission and fees.** `required_stake = f(V_booked)`, `f`
  monotone non-decreasing. **No change** to the SPEC-018 emission schedule, the 90/10 block-reward split, or
  fee routing. Therefore **non-inflationary** (mints nothing) and **lockup-positive** (more FLOP staked ⇒ less
  circulating) — the opposite sign to an emission bonus, and on the safe side of the anti-Terra invariant.
- **Ratchet, not float.** The live floor is `max(baseline_floor, k·V_booked)`, where `baseline_floor` is the
  SPEC-018-`R`-derived requirement (the reward-capture term). It **never decreases below baseline** — baseline
  binds at bootstrap (reward-capture-dominated, `V_booked` small); the value term binds at maturity. The
  ratchet also blocks a "suppress measured `V_booked` ⇒ lower the bar ⇒ attack cheaply" move and **composes
  with the C6 thermostat** (`thermostat_floor_invariant`, `NetworkValue.lean`).
- **`V_booked` is a slow-cadence admission gate, not a per-block consensus input.** The floor retargets on an
  epoch/governance cadence from the `D₂`/`n_eff` metrics (ENG-31 part 1), tolerating a non-consensus-critical
  estimator and measurement lag; `nEff_sybil_collapse` + the `D₂` concentration bound keep Sybils/concentration
  from lowering the bar.
- **Participation/incentive is DEC-5's concern, not this decision's.** Raising required stake under fixed
  reward lowers FLOP yield-%; validators are compensated by FLOP **appreciation** as `V_booked` grows (the
  `price_independent` law: the stake↔value relation holds in FLOP at any token price) plus the existing reward
  stream. *Whether that stream suffices* to attract the floor stake is the validator-sufficiency question owned
  by **DEC-5** (fee/reward split). This decision deliberately does not touch reward — it **removes**, rather
  than creates, the DEC-5 entanglement the reward-funded alternatives carried.
- **Unblocks ENG-31 parts 2–3, retargeted to the stake side.** Part 2 wires `required_stake = f(V_booked)`
  (retarget rule + cadence + `k` calibration; safety zone `economic_parameters`; `check_params`). Part 3
  formalizes C5 in Lean as a thin wrapper on `coc_ge_voc_iff_stake_ge` (instantiate `VoC ∝ V_booked`, `f`
  monotone ⇒ `CoC(S = f(V)) ≥ VoC(V)` above threshold) + the `economic-security-proofs.md` catalog.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- **(A) Fee-funded reward coupling** *(the prep recommendation)* — route a value-tracking fee share to
  validators so an effective reward `∝ V_booked` lifts `requiredStake` via the reward-capture term. Works, but
  *indirect* (value→reward→stake instead of value→stake), **entangles DEC-6 with the DEC-5 fee split**
  (validators get 0% of inference fees today), and depends on fee volume materializing. (D) reaches the same
  `requiredStake ∝ V_booked` directly, with no emission/fee change.
- **(B) Value-coupled emission bonus** — base schedule + a bonus term in `V_booked`. **Inflationary by
  construction** (fails the "track value *without over-issuing*" test), needs a hard cap, collides with the
  day-730 halving cliff (F2 C5), and puts `V_booked` on the critical mint path (manipulation-resistant on-chain
  `n_eff` is a high bar). (D) is the opposite sign — it *locks* supply.
- **(C) Governor-retarget** — point the F2 supply-side governor at `V_booked`. The governor is a **default-OFF
  survival damper** (utilization-indexed boost + dynamic burn); retargeting conflates survival insurance with
  the primary coupling, and off-by-default yields no baseline coupling.
- **Couple via `R` only (the framing the prep inherited)** — treats `R` as the sole lever on `requiredStake`;
  but `requiredStake ∝ VoC` and `VoC ∝ V_booked` directly, so the reward intermediary is redundant.

**Links.** Research: network-valuation.md §4 (C5),
verification-economics-bounds §5e,
pouw-landscape §3 (demand-coupled-rewards invariant). Formal:
`FlopSpecs/Security.lean` (`CoCVoC.coc_ge_voc_iff_stake_ge`, `required_stake_linear_in_reward`,
`price_independent`), `FlopSpecs/NetworkValue.lean` (`Vbooked`, `nEff_sybil_collapse`, C6
`thermostat_floor_invariant`). Params: `validator_min_stake` note retargeted `R` → `V_booked`. Wiring: ENG-31
parts 2–3. Cluster: DEC-5 (validator sufficiency / fee split). YP §§: §6.1, §9, §11.

## D-0414 — Validator-native attestation; supersedes M-of-N oracle set

**Decision (ratified).** Status `[LIVE]` (pallet shipped, tests green). Resolves **DEC-1**. Supersedes **D-0406** (M-of-N oracle attestation threshold).

**Context.** D-0406 introduced a separate oracle registry (`RegisteredOracles`, `OracleCount`, `OracleThreshold`) managed by root-governance. Oracles were distinct accounts from validators. This created an additional trust party: the oracle set could be compromised independently of the BFT committee, and governance had to maintain two authority lists. The TEE quote verification logic (DCAP/dstack) was already running off-chain on the tee-bridge; the only question was *which accounts* were trusted to submit the result on-chain.

**Decision.** Validators — the accounts already staking, rotating, and subject to BFT slashing — run TEE quote verification off-chain (same DCAP/dstack tee-bridge logic) and submit signed `ValidatorAttestation` bundles. The on-chain check verifies:
1. Each signer is an active validator (`pallet_validators::ActiveValidators`).
2. The bundle carries at least `ceil(active_count × threshold_fraction)` **distinct** active-validator signatures agreeing on the same `(task_hash, gn_weight, model_hash, output_hash, is_valid)` tuple.
3. The per-attestation sr25519 signature is valid over the SCALE-encoded signable payload.

The threshold fraction is a `Perbill` stored in `ValidatorAttestationThreshold` (governable, default `Perbill::from_rational(2, 3)` ≈ 2/3, matching the BFT honest-majority assumption). A minimum of 1 is enforced via `.max(1)`.

**Ratified constraints.**
- **Oracle registry removed.** `RegisteredOracles`, `OracleCount`, `OracleThreshold`, `register_oracle`, `unregister_oracle` are gone. Call indices 3 and 4 are tombstoned (return `VerificationFailed`) to preserve the extrinsic namespace.
- **`ValidatorAttestation` replaces `OracleAttestation`.** Wire-format compatible (same fields; `validator_id` replaces `oracle_id`); `hp-poui` exposes both for a migration window.
- **BFT quorum, not fixed M.** `required = ceil(active_count × threshold_fraction).max(1)`. Scales automatically as the validator set grows; no governance action needed on rotation.
- **Trust model collapses to one party.** Validators secure consensus and attest proofs; no separate trusted oracle class. A validator that lies about a TEE result is subject to the same slashing and blacklist logic as any other misbehaviour.
- **Off-chain TEE verification unchanged.** Validators run the existing tee-bridge (DCAP/dstack) off-chain and sign the result; the on-chain path only aggregates and threshold-checks signatures.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- **(A) Keep the oracle registry (D-0406 status quo)** — separate oracle accounts, root-managed registry, fixed M-of-N. Requires maintaining a second authority list independent of the BFT committee; oracle accounts are not staked, so the economic deterrence is weaker than for validators. Adds operational surface (registry management extrinsics) for no benefit over (D).
- **(B) ZK-verified TEE quotes on-chain** — submit the raw DCAP quote and verify it in a RISC0/SP1 SNARK inside the pallet. Fully trustless but adds ~500 ms of proving time per proof and a heavy verifier pallet; complexity grossly exceeds the threat model at current network scale. Left as a long-term upgrade path.
- **(C) Off-chain workers** — each validator runs an OCW that submits attestations autonomously. OCWs have the same validator-account trust model as (D) but require the tee-bridge to run inside the node process and add liveness complexity (OCW scheduling, unsigned transaction priority races). (D) is simpler: validators submit at-will, quorum aggregation happens in a single extrinsic.

**Links.** Task: DEC-1. Pallet: `pallets/pallets/flop-poui/` (`oracle.rs`, `lib.rs`). Primitive: `hp-poui` (`ValidatorAttestation`). Runtime: `FlopValidatorSet` in `pallets/runtime/zkverify/src/lib.rs`. YP §§: §3.6, §13.3.

## D-0416 — Validator rotation orders by stake, gated by a minimum-performance floor

**Decision (ratified).** Status `[PARTIAL]`. **Supersedes
[D-0409](#d-0409--validator-slashing-table-reconciliation-code--whitepaper-draft) point 5** ("bottom-N ranked by
a **performance metric = verified-work + liveness, not stake size**") and its rejected "stake-size rotation
metric" alternative. **Reconciles SPEC-021 rotation with
[D-0401](#d-0401--consensus--stake-bft-dag-alephbft-pow-ghostdag-deleted)**, which already ranks the *committee*
by stake.

**Context.** D-0401 selects the BFT committee by **stake** (subject to a verified-work gate); D-0409 pt 5 had the
SPEC-021 active-set rotation eject/promote by a **performance ranking** (verified-work + liveness), *not* stake —
two different orderings for one validator set. The performance ranking also made §13.2 **V9** a `[GAP]`: the
ranking metric *is* the gameable quantity, so a validator could **wash verified-work to climb the bottom-50 cut**.

**Decision.** Rotation **orders by stake**, **subject to a minimum-performance requirement** (verified-work +
liveness as a **floor/gate**, not the ranking key). Each interval, validators **below the performance floor** are
ejected; the remainder is **stake-ordered**, the bottom-N of those rotated out, and the top-N from the queue
promoted — the same stake-ranked basis as committee selection.

**Ratified constraints.**
- **Performance gates, stake orders.** A validator must clear the verified-work + liveness floor to stay/be
  promoted; among those that clear it, position is by stake (consistent with `hp-consensus::select_committee`).
- **Closes the V9 ranking-gaming vector.** Wash-work no longer improves rank (only stake does), so the "fake work
  to survive the cut" incentive collapses; the residual is the bounded "do *just enough* to clear the floor", not
  an open-ended ranking race.
- **Not "capital over work".** The floor still requires real verified work + liveness; the value-coupled stake
  floor ([D-0413](#d-0413--c5-value-coupled-security-via-the-stake-floor-not-reward)) sets how much stake is
  required.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Performance-metric ranking (D-0409 pt 5)* — makes the gameable quantity the ranking key (V9); inconsistent
  with the stake-ranked committee (D-0401).
- *Pure stake ordering, no performance floor* — D-0409's original objection ("rewards capital, not useful work");
  the minimum-performance gate answers it.

**Links.** Failure matrix: §13.2 V9. Supersedes D-0409 pt 5; consistent with D-0401 + D-0413. Code:
`pallet_validators` rotation `on_initialize` (order-by-stake + performance-floor gate — follow-up). YP §§: §6.1,
§13.2.

  non-Turing-complete, conservation-safe (the SOTA answer §6.4 already names).
- **The "Bitcoin opcodes" claim is corrected** to the spend-condition-layer framing — no claim of

- A **UTXO payment side-rail** is retained as a *future option* for high-frequency micropayments only — not a

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *(A) UTXO payment side-rail with a script subset (now)* — adds a second state model + bridge for marginal
  benefit; kept only as a future micropayment-lane option.
- *(C) Drop the claim, list raw primitives only* — loses the unifying composability story; the declarative layer

- *Claim Bitcoin-opcode compatibility* — false on an account model; the original v2-draft error.

**Links.** Research: agent-composition-primitives.md (the
`Condition` Miniscript-for-FRAME spec + survey). Consistent with D-0401 (account model). Code: `pallet_multisig`,

## D-0418 — Capacity-proportional miner self-stake (linear, no cap, keyed to calibrated B_p)

**Decision (ratified).** Status `[PARTIAL]` (devnet-wired end-to-end: capacity curve + `top_up_self_stake` +
`is_capacity_bonded`, the B_p binding (`hw-calibration` → `/86_400` → G_n/s), and the fail-closed PoUI credit gate
all landed & tested; coefficient `0.01` confirmed by sim calibration). Implements the stake half of **ENG-8**. Supersedes the
**flat-only** miner self-stake and the v2-draft's "10,000 + 1 FLOP / 10 GFLOP" formula.

**Context.** Miner self-stake was a flat 10,000 FLOP regardless of capacity, so a large miner (huge fraud blast
radius) risked the same as a single-GPU miner — fraud skin-in-game did not scale. The v2 whitepaper draft proposed
"+1 FLOP / 10 GFLOP capacity", which ENG-8 flagged as ~10× too high (≈ +100,000 FLOP for one H100-class card at
~1M G_n/s) and keyed to a gameable *declared* capacity. Separately, the recurring "can a very large miner take
over the network?" question: **miners are not validators** — a miner casts no consensus vote, so a large miner
**cannot** take over consensus by mining. Consensus capture requires validator *stake* and is bounded on the
validator side (BFT safety needs < ¼ of committee stake hostile, the D-0413 value-coupled floor, 1,000 slots,
rotation). The capacity-stake curve's job is therefore **fraud deterrence**, not consensus security.

**Decision.** Required miner self-stake scales **linearly with calibrated capacity, with no cap**:
`required_self_stake(B_p) = MinMinerSelfStake + B_p · CapacityStakePerGflop`, where `B_p` is the **calibrated**
sustained throughput (G_n/s) from `hw-calibration` (not declared/peak — anti-gaming), and `CapacityStakePerGflop`
is governance-set.
- **Linear, no cap — deliberately.** Fraud profit grows ~linearly with capacity (more capacity ⇒ more inflatable
  work before detection), so the bond must grow at least linearly to keep fraud −EV at every scale. **A cap (or
  sublinear curve) would make the largest miners' fraud +EV** — the opposite of the intent ("larger miners risk
  more"). This is the security answer to the "large-miner takeover" question on the *mining* side.
- **Starting value `CapacityStakePerGflop = 0.01 FLOP per GFLOP/s` (`VFY/100`)** ⇒ an H100-class miner
  (~1,000,000 G_n/s) posts **≈ +10,000 FLOP** (10× below the draft). Linear: ~10 cards ⇒ +100k; ~100 cards ⇒ +1M.
- **Keyed to calibrated B_p**, sourced via a `CapacityProvider` trait (bound to `hw-calibration` in the runtime;
  `()`→0 until wired, so the term is inert and **fail-safe** — never locks out a miner — until deliberately
  switched on).

**Ratified constraints.**
- Curve shape = **linear, uncapped** (the security invariant above). Any future cap/taper must re-open this
  decision with a fraud-profit bound that still holds at the top end.
- Capacity = **calibrated B_p** only; never miner-declared/peak.
- Enforcement `[LIVE]`: `pallet_miner_staking` exposes `is_capacity_bonded(miner)`; activation
  (`set_miner_status(active)`) is gated on it, and the **PoUI credit path** gates on it — at the shared oracle
  credit point (`verify_and_process_attestations`, covering single + M-of-N) and the simulator path; an
  under-bonded miner's verified proof is **dropped, not credited**. `top_up_self_stake` lets a miner meet a raised
  requirement after B_p finalizes.
- Composes with **D-0419**: the larger capacity bond is slash-locked while disputes/sessions are open.
- **Value `[CALIBRATED]` — 0.01 confirmed** (miner-capacity-stake-calibration.md). `CapacityStakePerGflop` was calibrated in the tokenomics sim against
  *fraud-profit-over-the-dispute-window* (and weighed against miner capital opportunity cost — self-stake earns no
  yield today). Governance-adjustable.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Flat self-stake (status quo)* — large miners under-bonded relative to fraud blast radius.
- *Draft's "1 FLOP / 10 GFLOP"* — ~10× too high (+100k for one H100); deters legitimate supply.
- *Linear + max-stake cap* — reintroduces sublinear stake/capacity above the cap ⇒ the largest miners' fraud
  becomes +EV; a security hole. Rejected.
- *Key to declared/peak capacity* — gameable (declare low, serve high); must be calibrated B_p.

**Links.** Task: ENG-8. Code: `pallet_miner_staking` (`required_self_stake` / `is_capacity_bonded` /
`top_up_self_stake` / `CapacityProvider`; activation gate), `runtime/zkverify` (`CapacityStakePerGflop`, `Capacity`
provider). Sim: tokenomics-simulation (coefficient calibration). Related: D-0413 (validator value-coupled floor —
the *consensus*-side security), D-0419 (slash-lock). YP §§: §6.1.

## D-0419 — Unbonding slash-lock + unlock cooldown

**Decision (ratified).** Status `[PARTIAL]` (miner side `[LIVE]` via ENG-21 / PR #73; explicit validator-side
parity is a follow-up). Closes failure-matrix **M11** (§13.1, the §13.8 #1 gap) and ratifies the policy behind it.

**Context.** Slashing only bites while the bond is still locked. Miner `UNBONDING_PERIOD_BLOCKS` (604,800) was
set **equal** to `ChannelDisputeWindow`, so a miner could begin unbonding at unilateral settle and have stake
fully exit before a late-window dispute's *respond + resolve* steps finish — a fraud verdict would then land on
an already-emptied bond. The same race exists wherever unbonding can outrun an open liability (session, dispute,
audit/offence window).

**Decision.** Stake **cannot complete unbonding while the staker has any open liability** — an open session, an
unresolved dispute, or a pending audit/offence window. Unbonding is **frozen while liabilities are open**; the
**unlock cooldown** only runs once the last one closes, sized so the bond outlives the full dispute lifecycle
(window + max respond + resolve). Freeze-while-open is the exact mechanism (no hand-tuned margin): the bond is
provably present whenever a verdict can still land.

**Ratified constraints.**
- **Miner `[LIVE]`** (ENG-21 / PR #73): `pallet_miner_staking` tracks an open-session/dispute count; `unbond`
  rejects/queues while `> 0`; tests cover window-edge dispute → still slashable, unbond blocked while open,
  unbond proceeds after resolution.
- **Validator**: §13.2 V11's "21-day margin" (unbonding 21 d > retention `W` 14 d + dispute lifecycle) already
  satisfies the policy by margin; D-0408's `UnbondingPeriodBlocks` freeze/thaw is the hook. Making the freeze
  **explicit** (freeze-while-open, not margin-only) for parity with the miner side is a follow-up.
- Composes with **D-0420**: the returnable equivocation portion (50 % after 180 d) is itself slash-locked — it
  cannot be withdrawn while a higher penalty can still attach.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Fixed margin (unbond ≥ window + resolution)* — works for validators today but relies on a hand-tuned number;
  freeze-while-open is exact and self-adjusting.
- *Leave M11 open* — a 100 % penalty against an empty bond is no penalty; the highest-blast-radius §13.8 gap.

**Links.** Failure matrix: §13.1 M11, §13.2 V11, §13.8 #1. Task: ENG-21 (`done:#73`) + validator-parity
follow-up. Code: `pallet_miner_staking` (open-liability freeze), `pallet_validators` (`UnbondingPeriodBlocks`,
[D-0408](#d-0408--single-validator-staking-system-pallet_validators-is-the-authority-source-pallet_staking-removed)).
YP §§: §6.1, §13.

## D-0420 — Equivocation slashing — correlated (Eth2-style), lone fault recoverable

**Decision (ratified).** Status `[PARTIAL]` (principle ratified; pallet curve + Lean model + params are wiring
follow-ups). **Supersedes the flat-100 % Equivocation row of
[D-0409](#d-0409--validator-slashing-table-reconciliation-code--whitepaper-draft).** D-0409's other 100 % classes
— **Collusion** (fake `G_n` proof), **EvidenceForgery**, **TEE-attestation failure**, **DoubleSpending** — are
**unchanged**.

**Context.** D-0409 set Equivocation = flat **100 % burn + eject + blacklist**, the same class as proof-forgery.
But equivocation (signing two conflicting blocks) is the fault that is *most often an honest accident* — a
misconfigured failover or redundant signer double-signs with no intent to attack. A flat 100 % over-punishes the
lone operator, while only a **coordinated ≥⅓ cartel** can actually violate BFT safety yet pays the same rate as
one honest slip. Ethereum's correlated/anti-correlation penalty is built for exactly this: scale the penalty with
the *fraction of stake* equivocating in the same window.

**Decision.** Equivocation moves from flat-100 % to a **correlated penalty** keyed to the equivocating-stake
fraction in the slashing window:
- **Isolated / lone** (small fraction): **slashed & ejected, with 50 % of the bond returned after a 180-day
  lockup** (net 50 % loss; re-stakeable). Treats a one-off double-sign as a recoverable, plausibly-honest fault —
  not provable fraud.
- **Correlated cartel** (fraction → ⅓): scales to **100 % burn + eject + blacklist**, no return — a coordinated
  safety attack deterred at full strength.

Net: directly raises cost-of-corruption for cartels (the `CoCVoC` threat) while staying lenient on a lone mistake.

**Ratified constraints.**
- **Scope = Equivocation only.** Collusion / EvidenceForgery / TEE / DoubleSpending stay flat **100 % + eject +
  blacklist** — deliberate provable fraud with no honest "lone" reading, so D-0409's stance (and its rejection of
  "50 %/half-returned" *for collusion*) stands. The 50 %/180-day structure D-0409 rejected for collusion is
  adopted **here for equivocation** precisely because equivocation, unlike fake-proof signing, can be an honest
  failover bug.
- **The returned 50 % is slash-locked ([D-0419](#d-0419--unbonding-slash-lock--unlock-cooldown))** — not
  withdrawable while any dispute/offence window is open; the 180-day clock cannot let stake escape a pending
  higher (correlated) penalty.
- **Exact curve deferred to calibration** (safety-zone `economic_parameters`): lone/base rate, correlation
  multiplier, ⅓ saturation threshold, window length → a `pallet_validators` wiring task;
  `Security.lean::StakingSlashing` gains the recoverable + correlated equivocation case (today: full-burn-100 % +
  recoverable-1 %/5 % only).

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Keep flat 100 % for equivocation (D-0409)* — over-punishes honest failover double-signs at the cartel rate.
- *Flat 50 %/half-returned, no correlation* — loses the cartel deterrent (a ⅓ coordinated attack would pay the
  same as one operator).
- *Extend the curve to collusion/forgery/TEE* — deliberate fraud; correlation-leniency would under-deter a lone
  forger (scope = Equivocation only).

**Links.** Research: economic-security-proofs.md (`CoCVoC` cartel
cost-of-corruption); pattern: Ethereum correlated slashing. Formal:
`formal-specs/lean/FlopSpecs/Security.lean::StakingSlashing` (case to add). Code: `pallet_validators` slash curve
(follow-up). YP §§: §11, §13.2 (V2).

## D-0422 — Refundable failed sessions via an unused-proportional penalty (amends D-0403)

**Decision.** Status `[LIVE]` for the static split: `φ = 20 %` wired in
[#598](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/pull/598) (`ChannelRefundPenalty = Perbill::from_percent(20)`,
`ChannelMaxSettlementTurns = 1024`, `ChannelMaxMerklePathLen = 64`;
`refund_penalty_phi_percent` enforced), dispute-standing gates in
#704. The dynamic `φ = clamp(ρ, φmin, φmax)` remains designed-but-not-wired (see sizing below). **Supersedes D-0403 ratified constraint #1 ("No refund on under-use") for the FAILED /
EARLY-TERMINATED case only.** Under-use of a *delivered, cooperatively-settled* session stays
non-refundable (D-0403 unchanged there); output quality / dissatisfaction is still never a refund trigger.

**Context.** D-0403 made escrow a non-refundable capacity payment because a clean refund of unconsumed
escrow is a free-option / wash primitive for a colluding agent+miner pair (finding #455): open a channel,
do little/no verified work, "fail", reclaim escrow at ~zero cost. That blanket no-refund also zero-refunds
sessions that fail through no fault of the agent (miner crash mid-session, M5) beyond the existing
non-delivery-timeout path. The gap: no way to return the *unused* remainder of an early-terminated session
without reopening the collusion vector.

**Decision.** On a failed / early-terminated session, split the escrow `E` at settle into three conserved
buckets: **miner** ← value of VERIFIED per-turn work `v` (`Sessions.minerCredit`; transcript-verified, never
a claimed aggregate); **agent refund** ← `(1−φ)·(E−v)`; **penalty** ← `φ·(E−v)`, routed RECOVERY-PROOF —
burned or slashed to the FLOP Foundation (slashing-proceeds decision), **never to the miner**. The penalty
is proportional to the UNUSED reservation (a single knob `φ`), not a fixed floor: this one knob (a) prices
reservation griefing — an abandoned reservation (`v=0`) costs `φ·E`; (b) refunds a genuinely-failed
session's unused remainder; (c) charges nothing on an honest fully-used session (`v=E ⇒ penalty=0`;
Pigouvian).

**Ratified constraints.**
- **Routing recovery-proof.** The penalty (or at least its collusion-deterring part `φf`) is burned or
  routed to the Foundation; a penalty paid to the counterparty is recovered by a colluding pair and deters
  nothing (`RefundPenalty.penalty_to_coalition_nullified`). An optional miner-compensation slice `φm`
  (coalition-recoverable) may make an honest griefed miner whole but does not count toward collusion
  deterrence.
- **Fault split.** Miner non-delivery (M5 crash, missed ack) ⇒ `φ=0`, full refund of unconsumed escrow +
  `FailedAcks` (already `[LIVE]`) — the agent is not penalized for the miner's failure. Agent-initiated /
  ambiguous early close ⇒ `φ` as sized. Upheld fraud ⇒ 100% miner slash + full agent refund (unchanged).
- **Reward follows verified work.** `v` is the transcript-verified per-turn `G_n` (per-turn metered), never
  an agent-signed aggregate (closes the #455 crediting gap); with full per-turn metering the consumption
  remainder is charged per turn and `φ` applies only to any un-metered lump.
- **Sizing / range.** `φ ∈ [max(ρ, channel_protocol_fee), 1]`, governance-set, ideally dynamic
  `φ = clamp(ρ, channel_protocol_fee, φ_max)` where `ρ` = occupancy (reserved / calibrated-`B_p` capacity
  over the reserved window). `φ = 1` recovers D-0403 (no refund); `φ = channel_protocol_fee` is maximum
  refund; `φ ≥ ρ` makes reservation griefing non-+EV (`RefundPenalty.griefing_deterred_by_floor`);
  `φ ≥ channel_protocol_fee` keeps the refund from being a cheaper wash than settle
  (`RefundPenalty.refund_not_cheaper_than_settle`). A **dynamic** `φ = clamp(ρ, φmin, φmax)` makes the
  measured occupancy `ρ` an on-chain input; the clamp bounds a misreported `ρ` into `[φmin, φmax]`,
  and with `φmin = channel_protocol_fee` the anti-wash floor is `ρ`-manipulation-proof for ANY `ρ`
  (`RefundPenalty.clampPhi_lb` + `RefundPenalty.antiwash_robust_to_rho`) — only griefing-sizing
  `φ ≥ ρ_true` needs honest occupancy, which rides on the existing `B_p` calibration surface. **Ship
  static `φ` first (zero `ρ` surface); adopt the dynamic clamp only if utilization drift warrants**
  (see #560). Params: `refund_penalty_phi_percent = 20` is wired and enforced
  ([#598](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/pull/598)); `φmin`/`φmax` land only if the dynamic
  clamp is adopted. **Sizing note (post-#669):** the `channel_protocol_fee` referenced above as the
  anti-wash floor was removed by [#669](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/pull/669) (sessions
  settle with **no** protocol fee), so the anti-wash floor is carried by `φ` itself — the wired
  static `φ = 20 %` dominates the old 1 % floor by construction, and a future dynamic clamp must
  give `φmin` its own protocol param rather than reusing the removed fee.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Two-part tariff (separate non-refundable floor `κ·E` + penalty on the remainder)* — the
  unused-proportional penalty subsumes it (`RefundPenalty.griefing_penalty_is_floor`: at `v=0`, `φ·E` IS a
  floor) and is Pigouvian-superior (a fixed floor taxes honest full-use, `RefundPenalty.full_use_no_penalty`).
  The floor's only edge (work-verification independence) was covered by the 1%
  `ChannelProtocolFee` burn at the time of the analysis; that fee was since removed (#669), and the
  wired static `φ = 20 %` carries the floor instead (see sizing note above).
- *Minimum session size (`MinChannelEscrow`) as the griefing defense* — `φ`/`κ` are fractions and cannot
  floor a fixed per-session cost (`RefundPenalty.proportional_cannot_floor`); a minimum session size is a
  distinct ABSOLUTE mechanism, and is low-value here (cooperative-path miners decline dust off-chain;
  `force_open` dust is refunded on expire so a floor does not deter it — tracked separately).
- *Blanket no-refund (D-0403 as-is)* — correct against collusion but zero-refunds genuinely-failed sessions
  and gives no capacity-griefing price signal short of forfeiting the whole escrow.

**Formal backing.** `formal-specs/lean/FlopSpecs/RefundPenalty.lean` (native ℝ) + `RefundPenaltyInt`
(integer/`Perbill` mirror in `Computable.lean`, Plausible-tested, DRT-ready for the #560 wiring):
conservation (ℝ + ℕ), collusion deterrence under recovery-proof routing, the nullification theorem
(penalty→miner deters nothing), griefing deterrence `φ≥ρ`, the anti-wash floor, the
tariff-subsumption + Pigouvian pair (why one knob, not two), and `proportional_cannot_floor` (minimum
session size is a separate absolute mechanism).

**Links.** Amends D-0403; relates to #442 (miner-crash decision), #455 (settle crediting gap), and the
slashing-proceeds-to-Foundation decision. YP §§: §12.4, §12.5, §13.1 (M5).

## D-0423 — Dust force_open expiry does not increment FailedAcks

**Status.** `[LIVE]` guard for #561. Complements D-0422: miner-fault `force_open` expiry still gives the
agent a full refund (`φ=0`), but dust/spurious requests below
`min_force_open_escrow_for_failed_ack = 0.05 FLOP` do not increment the named miner's `FailedAcks`.

**Context.** `force_open` is permissionless and names a miner before any off-chain handshake. An attacker can
post tiny reservations against a target miner; if the miner rationally ignores them as dust, `expire_force_open`
refunds the attacker in full and, before this guard, incremented `FailedAcks` at roughly tx-fee cost. A minimum
session escrow does not deter this path because expiry refunds the escrow.

**Decision.** Choose option A: below a dedicated `MinForceOpenEscrowForFailedAck` threshold, an expired
`force_open` refunds normally but does not count as a missed-ack reputation event. At or above the threshold,
expiry still increments `FailedAcks` exactly as before. The knob is separate from `EscrowPerReservationSlot` so
reservation-cap tuning cannot silently move reputation semantics.

**Sizing rule.** Recompute the threshold as
`T = round_up(max(ExistentialDeposit, k * F_open))`, where `F_open` is the minimum-fee cost of a `force_open`
extrinsic and `k=20` by default. Also require `T` to stay at or below the smallest session escrow miners are
expected to service. Current rough inputs: `F_open ~= 0.002 FLOP`, `ExistentialDeposit = 0.01 FLOP`,
`20 * F_open ~= 0.04 FLOP`; round to `0.05 FLOP`.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *B: Non-refundable force_open open-fee forfeited on expire* — economically stronger against spam, but charges
  honest agents when the miner is actually unavailable and changes the D-0422 miner-fault refund split.
- *C: Weight or rate-limit FailedAcks in the future reputation layer* — still useful later, but leaves the live
  raw counter griefable until ENG-13 lands.
- *D: Per-agent force_open rate limits against each miner* — adds state and is Sybil-soft; it reduces volume but
  does not define when a miner may ignore dust for free.

## D-0424 — Dispute standing = session agent + validators

**Status.** `[LIVE]` via [#591](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/591).

**Decision.**
1. **The session agent may challenge only a transaction/session it participated in.** This preserves the
   privacy and skin-in-the-game benefits of agent-side challenge without granting the agent standing over
   unrelated sessions.
2. **Any active validator may challenge any transaction/session.** Validators are the independent fraud
   backstop for collusion/laziness and keep the challenge catch probability alive when the agent is offline,
   passive, or aligned with the miner.
3. **Arbitrary public challengers do not have standing.** The protocol keeps the challenger bond and
   loser-pays/frivolous-dispute deterrence, but non-participant non-validators are rejected before the bond
   is locked.
4. **Disputes remain fraud-only.** Standing does not expand disputes into output-quality/SLA arbitration:
   valid grounds remain wrong measured root, inflated `G_n` vs transcript, forged/replayed signatures,
   transcript withholding/non-response, or non-delivery.

**Current implementation note.** `pallet_compute_channel::open_dispute` gates standing before locking
`ChannelChallengerBond`: accept if origin is the channel agent **or**
`Config::ValidatorSet::is_active_validator` (runtime wires `FlopValidatorSet` →
`pallet_validators::ActiveValidators`); otherwise `Error::NoDisputeStanding`.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Agent-only* — weakens independent fraud detection and leaves miner/agent collusion or agent laziness
  unchecked.
- *Fully permissionless public challenges* — preserves detection but exposes the system to third-party
  griefing, unnecessary data exposure, and verifier's-dilemma under-provisioning.
- *Validator-only* — loses the natural counterparty remedy for the agent that already holds the transcript
  and suffered the alleged fraud.

**Links.** Tracker: [#449](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/449). Implementation:
[#591](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/591). Research:
`whitepaper/research/dispute-reexecution-tradeoffs.md`. YP §§: §12.5, §15, Appendix C.

## D-0426 — Weak-subjectivity checkpoints for light-client bootstrap

**Status.** Ratified; artifact schema + SDK verify + indexer publication + Quint T-C2 model landed in
[#594](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/594). The unbounded Lean discharge is now
`WeakSubjectivity.fresh_client_not_on_old_key_fork`; node-side auto-production and the SDK not-future guard
remain follow-ups.

**Context.** FLOP expects agent wallets and light clients to verify settlement/finality claims without running
archive/full nodes. A client that was offline beyond the economic accountability window cannot safely accept an
arbitrary peer's "latest finalized" header, because old validator keys may be used in a long-range/posterior
corruption story. The useful model should be easy to specify and easy for light clients to follow.

**Decision.**
1. **Publish weak-subjectivity checkpoints as compact finalized artifacts.** A checkpoint commits to
   `chain_id`/genesis hash, finalized block number and hash, state root, runtime `spec_version`, finality
   authority/committee-set hash, checkpoint index, and timestamp/block time.
2. **Proof = finality committee evidence over the checkpoint tuple.** The implementation may expose the
   native AlephBFT finality justification or a compact ≥2/3 committee signature/aggregate over the same tuple;
   the light-client rule is to verify the proof before trusting headers forward from that checkpoint.
3. **Cadence = every 86,400 finalized blocks** (daily at the 1 s block target), plus an extra checkpoint at
   runtime-upgrade and finality-authority-set transitions.
4. **Freshness rule = 21 days.** A light client may bootstrap from a checkpoint no older than the validator
   unbonding/slash-lock window. If it has been offline longer than 21 days, it must refresh a checkpoint from
   independent sources before signing settlements or accepting terminal economic claims.
5. **Publication is redundant, not a fork-choice override.** Foundation releases, validator/explorer/indexer
   endpoints, and DA/IPFS-style mirrors may all serve the same signed artifact. Consensus safety still comes
   from AlephBFT; the checkpoint is a weak-subjectivity bootstrap anchor, not an automatic social-finality
   mechanism.

**Current implementation note (tracker [#594](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/594)).**
- Schema: `docs/reference/weak-subjectivity-checkpoint.schema.json`
  (explorer mirror: `explorer/public/checkpoints/`).
- SDK verify: `sdk/typescript/src/weak-subjectivity.ts` (`verifyCheckpoint` rejects stale / wrong chain /
  wrong genesis / authority-set mismatch / invalid proof).
- Publication: indexer `GET /api/weak-subjectivity/latest`, Foundation
  `releases/checkpoints/ws-{chainId}-{index}.json`, validator `weak-subjectivity/latest.json`.

## D-0427 — Session throughput contract — adaptive session-control lane

**Status.** Ratified recommendation; lane/admission implementation follow-up open in
[#595](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/595).

**Context.** Sessions moved inference off-chain, so the block no longer carries turns, prompts, outputs, or
proof payloads on the happy path. The chain carries compact control traffic (`open_channel`, `settle`, forced
settlement/finalization, and disputes). The target is to maximize useful session throughput while preserving
the 1 s block target and the propagation-bound effective block budget.

**Decision.**
1. **Do not raise the block-time, block-weight, or block-length constants to get throughput.** Keep the 1 s
   block target, 0.75 s max block compute budget, 75 % normal dispatch ratio, and 5 MB technical length cap.
   Treat the Yellow Paper's ~125 KiB effective propagation budget as the practical control-plane budget.
2. **Adopt option C as a hybrid lane policy.** Session control, disputes/finality, and ordinary transfers share
   the same chain but have explicit admission/priority lanes. New opens are lowest priority inside the session
   lane; settlement, finalization, timeout, and dispute calls outrank new opens.
3. **Launch target = 96 session-control tx/block steady, 128 tx/block burst.** Since a happy-path session uses
   two inclusions, this advertises roughly 48 session lifecycles/s steady and 64/s burst before off-chain
   inference capacity, not L1 inclusion, becomes the bottleneck.
4. **Byte/weight guardrail.** Session-control traffic targets at most 75 KiB effective bytes/block or 60 % of
   normal-dispatch weight, whichever is lower. It may burst to 96 KiB or 75 % of normal-dispatch weight only
   while telemetry is green.
5. **Telemetry backpressure.** Green means the trailing 60-block average block time is ≤ 1.05 s, block
   propagation p95 is ≤ 500 ms, and finality lag remains below the warn threshold. If any condition fails,
   throttle new `open_channel`/`force_open` first and preserve settlement/dispute/finalization capacity.
6. **Settlement cadence is event-driven.** Do not force periodic on-chain settlement just to count work:
   active sessions settle on close, escrow exhaustion/top-up, timeout, or risk boundary. SDKs may recommend
   close/reopen when session duration, turns, escrow, or re-attestation cadence reaches the D-0403 bounds.

**Current implementation note.** Implement [#595](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/595): expose
lane params, mempool/admission ordering, telemetry thresholds, and load tests that show overload throttles new
opens before average block time drifts while settlement/dispute traffic still clears.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Raise block size/weight for launch throughput* — risks block propagation and directly conflicts with the
  1 s target.
- *Reserve capacity only for session settlement* — protects exits but leaves opens under-specified during
  product launches.
- *Best-effort shared mempool only* — lets session bursts crowd ordinary transfers and makes block-time drift
  a fee-market side effect rather than an explicit throttle.
- *Force frequent periodic settlement* — increases on-chain load and defeats the O(1)/session advantage.

**Links.** Tracker: [#439](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/439). Implementation:
[#595](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/595). Research:
`whitepaper/research/r2-payment-channel-sessions.md`,
`whitepaper/research/scaling-500k-agents.md`. YP §§: §12.5.

## D-0428 — Two-lane session capacity market

**Status.** `[PARTIAL]`: signed bilateral offers, SDK force-open parity, miner auto-ack, and indexed
discovery are implemented by [#635](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/635); the
condition-armed scarcity auction is salvageable from closed
[PR #626](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/pull/626)'s branch.

**Decision.** Use exactly two economic lanes:

1. **Abundant capacity → bilateral standing offer.** A miner signs the target chain genesis hash,
   model/precision, enclave key, minimum escrow, exact SLA, capacity hint, finalized-head expiry,
   and a miner-scoped nonce. The agent receipt key countersigns the target genesis hash and exact
   acceptance. `open_channel_with_offer` verifies both signatures, expiry, and nonce, then enters
   the existing `Open` state with the same registry, calibration, escrow, assurance, and settlement
   invariants as rendezvous `open_channel`.
2. **Measured scarcity → conditional auction.** D-0428's `ReservationGate`/VCG work engages only
   when measured scarcity warrants price discovery. It is not a permanent third happy-path lane.
   `force_open` remains a permissionless inclusion hatch, not a pricing or discovery mechanism.

**Ratified constraints.**

- Offer validity never comes from its venue. DA Ephemeral, indexers, caches, and gossip only
  replicate bytes; runtime checks are authoritative.
- Both signature preimages bind the chain genesis hash. Offers from a prior devnet generation or
  another network therefore fail closed even when operators reuse keys and the young chain has not
  reached the old offer's expiry height.
- A consumed `(miner, nonce)` leaves a permanent replay tombstone, matching the permanent channel
  record. Expiry bounds enclave-key freshness and makes an unconsumed stale offer unusable.
- Offer-backed non-delivery at or above D-0423's dust threshold increments `FailedOfferOpens`;
  smaller reservations still refund but do not increment it. Missed force acknowledgements remain
  separate `FailedAcks`. Neither is a slash.
- A no-settlement timeout cannot attribute miner failure versus agent abandonment.
  `FailedOfferOpens` is therefore weak corroborating evidence only: materializers expose the
  thresholded cumulative counter with an explicit weak-evidence label, and agents must not use it
  as a standalone ranking penalty.
- Free-to-sign offer volume is never a ranking signal. Agents rank by current on-chain stake,
  blacklist/slash state, settlement history, attributable timeouts, and `FailedAcks`; they may use
  `FailedOfferOpens` only as weak corroboration.
- Standing offers remove the live RA-TLS rendezvous from authorization, reducing the
  CVE-2026-33697 relay surface. They do not strengthen session transport confidentiality; privacy
  still inherits the deployed attested-TLS transport's caveats.

**Alternatives rejected.**

- *Auction every session* — adds cadence/commit-reveal latency when capacity is abundant.
- *Treat force-open as the growth path* — locks agent capital through the ack window and offers no
  discovery.
- *Trust the publication venue or rank by offer count* — creates a spam/Sybil authority outside
  the signed object and on-chain reputation.

## D-0430 — Degraded-state settlement policy matrix

**Status.** `[PARTIAL]` for #656: compute-channel DA/unrecoverable/stale-attestation runtime guards,
indexer surface, Quint model, and the unbounded Lean terminal-loss override are wired;
attestation-pause service health and the normal co-signed-prefix split remain follow-ups.

**Context.** The failure matrix named finality stalls, DA loss, attestation quorum outages, verifier service
outages, and governance gates separately, but settlement needed a single rule for what agents observe when
those components are degraded mid-window. The invariant is simple: a liveness repair can delay or unlock an
undisputed safe path, but it cannot turn unverifiable evidence into a miner payout.

**Decision.**
1. **Temporary objective outage ⇒ extend/pause, once per window class.** DA temporarily unavailable or finality
   stalled extends the challenge/response window instead of paying or slashing.
2. **Terminal DA loss ⇒ fail closed.** If DA repair marks the transcript unrecoverable before settlement can be
   safely challenged, runtime refunds escrow and returns any challenger bond without a miner fraud slash. In v1,
   the agent is made whole from escrow and the miner bears unrecoverable-transcript risk; storer slashes remain
   uncoupled da-registry audit deterrence until E.31 defines any coupled storer-compensation leg.
3. **Stale attestation ⇒ reject settlement.** A settlement whose channel attestation freshness window elapsed is
   not accepted; the channel must be re-attested or resolved through the applicable timeout/degraded path.
4. **Shared degraded vocabulary.** Attestation outcomes use the common reason set:
   `Unavailable`, `Unverifiable`, `Stale`, `Conflicting`, `Rejected`, `Escrowed`, `Paused`.
5. **Observability is required.** Indexers expose channel state `degraded` during an extension and
   `degraded_refunded` on terminal fail-closed refund.

**Policy matrix.**

| Component | Temporary / stale state | Terminal / conflicting state | Settlement policy |
|-----------|-------------------------|------------------------------|-------------------|
| Finality | finalized head stalled | checkpoint/hard-fork recovery required | freeze finality-clocked economic deadlines; no best-head slash/refund |
| DA | transcript unavailable during window | transcript unrecoverable after repair threshold | extend once; then fail-closed refund if terminal |
| Attestation | quote/quorum freshness elapsed | conflicting/rejected profile verdict | reject settlement; pause only attestation-dependent clocks when outage is objective |
| Verifier service | no quorum / service outage | conflicting verifier evidence | `Unavailable`/`Unverifiable` reason; no payout from missing evidence |
| Governance gate | track paused/timelocked | proposal rejected | preserve existing state; no implicit parameter fallback |

**Open stubs.** E.27 tracks re-attestation cadence and pause-service wiring; E.31 tracks the precise
normal co-signed-prefix receipt split. The terminal-loss proof does not wait on that split:
`DegradedSettlement.resolve` overrides an arbitrary normal outcome with the full-refund branch.

`formal-specs/channel/settlement-degraded-window.qnt` and
`formal-specs/lean/FlopSpecs/DegradedSettlement.lean`
(`unrecoverable_transcript_voids_payment`; AXLE `6424620b-2dc0-4295-bbdc-d35dd0112cb0`).

## D-0431 — TOPLOC required for all sessions (execution-integrity floor); answer quality stays market-layer

**Status.** `[DECIDED]` (resolves [#446](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/446)). Policy ratified;
implementation tracked by [#753](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/753) (assurance-class gate),
[#748](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/748) (per-cell activation calibration),
[#754](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/754) (escalation lifecycle).

**Context.** #446 asked where execution fraud ends and subjective output quality begins, and when a TOPLOC
commitment / re-execution should be mandatory. Options: **A** quality is market-layer only, protocol disputes
cover execution integrity; **B** require TOPLOC commitments for all sessions at launch with thresholded
re-execution; **C** require TOPLOC only for high-value / non-TEE (SOFT) sessions; **D** add optional quality
warranties/reputation outside protocol fraud. TOPLOC detects execution substitution (model / precision /
prompt / decode / KV-cache games — plausible-but-wrong outputs at a correct `model_hash`), not answer quality.

**Decision — Option B, with an explicit activation reading.**
1. **Commitment is a mandatory floor for every session.** There is no TOPLOC-less settlement lane. Every
   session pins an assurance profile at channel open whose minimum is TEE + TOPLOC commitment; a session that
   promises TOPLOC **cannot settle without the required committed evidence** (capture + DA publish). Missing or
   unavailable required evidence **freezes/rejects settlement** (fail-closed), it does not silently accept.
2. **Slash adjudication stays fail-closed per (model, precision, backend, GPU) cell.** "Required for all
   sessions" binds the *commitment*, not a band verdict on an uncalibrated cell. A band **Slash** fires only on
   a cell whose thresholds have passed the #748 activation gate (`ToplocResearch.productionActivated` /
   `cellMeets`); before that a cell is commitment-required but adjudication-inert (evidence retained for
   later/dispute). This is what makes B launch-compatible instead of either blocking every session or slashing
   on an unmeasured detector.
3. **Thresholded re-execution.** Suspicious commitments escalate per the derived accept/escalate/slash band;
   the **Escalate** middle verdict routes to the persistent escalation lifecycle (#754), never to silent accept.
4. **Answer quality is out of protocol fraud (Option A's valid kernel, retained as the boundary).** Execution
   substitution is fraud (slash); semantic/answer quality is a market/reputation remedy (Option D, complementary,
   not a substitute). The R4 normative boundary (`ToplocSession` / toploc-r1-r4-research §R4) is authoritative.
5. **Value-capped assurance classes.** Higher-value exposure requires stronger classes (TEE+TOPLOC → +sampled
   re-execution / work-cert); exposure is capped by the pinned class and its activation evidence (#753).

**Rejected.** **C** (TOPLOC only for high-value/SOFT) — rejected: it makes a low-assurance lane the default and
invites the price race-to-the-bottom in the register's §6 worked example (a SOFT miner undercuts on price, the
agent acts on degraded output before spot-checks catch it). **A alone** — rejected as the *whole* policy: it
leaves the base tier's execution integrity resting on the dispute lane only. **B/C both** depend on the
plausible-but-wrong surface being disputable at all, which is Tier-2's purpose. **D** kept, but as a
market-layer complement, never a protocol-fraud substitute.

**Prerequisite.** [#642](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/642) (`ToplocFraudUpheld` under-reports
slash amounts) must land before any policy or economics leans on that event data.

**Links.** Decision: [#446](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/446). Build: #753, #748, #754, #642.

## D-0432 — TEE attestation is optional, not a participation or settlement precondition

**Status.** `[DECIDED]` — amends the *framing* of [D-0414](#d-0414--validator-native-attestation-supersedes-m-of-n-oracle-set)
and [D-0431](#d-0431--toploc-required-for-all-sessions-execution-integrity-floor-answer-quality-stays-market-layer).
Neither prior decision is reversed; the non-TEE (SOFT) tier's end-to-end definition is tracked as
open item E.33 ([#650](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/650)) and must land alongside.

**Context.** D-0414 made active validators the on-chain attestation authority by verifying TEE quotes;
D-0431 pinned every session's assurance-profile minimum at "TEE + TOPLOC commitment." Read together,
these presented TEE attestation as a precondition for participation and settlement. But
`VerificationStack.sessionVerifier_survives_broken_tee` proves the composed session verifier retains
full TOPLOC soundness **even with a fully broken TEE** (ρ = 1: forged quotes pass —
Foreshadow/SGAxe/AEPIC/CrossTalk): the stacked verifier is still as sound as the standalone derived
TOPLOC band. So soundness does **not** rest on TEE. Requiring TEE for all participation (a) excludes the
SOFT (non-TEE) miner class the tiered stack was designed to admit, (b) concentrates trust in a single
hardware root of trust, contrary to the trust-domain principle (YP §0), and (c) is unnecessary for
soundness.

**Decision.**
1. **TEE attestation is an OPTIONAL HARD-tier strengthening.** It is not a precondition for participation
   or settlement.
2. **The mandatory execution-integrity floor is TEE-independent:** TOPLOC commitment (D-0431, unchanged as
   the floor) + optimistic sampled re-execution + slashing. Protocol soundness **MUST NOT** depend on TEE
   integrity.
3. **HARD vs SOFT.** Where present, a TEE attestation raises a session's assurance tier (HARD) and its
   permitted value cap. A non-TEE (SOFT) session is a first-class participant, value-capped and backed by
   spot-check re-execution.
4. **D-0414 is unchanged in substance** — validators remain the on-chain attestation authority via BFT
   quorum. What changes is framing: a TEE quote the quorum verifies is *one assurance input for the HARD
   tier*, not a universal settlement gate. The SOFT path's on-chain evidence (miner session-key turn
   signatures + agent receipt in place of enclave signatures; validator re-execution quorum for disputes)
   is specified by E.33.
5. **The `TeeAttestationFailure` slash class applies only to HARD-tier attestations** (YP §11.3).

**Rejected.** *Keep TEE mandatory* — rejected: it rests soundness on a single hardware RoT (contra §0),
excludes commodity / non-CC supply, and is provably unnecessary for soundness
(`sessionVerifier_survives_broken_tee`).

**Prerequisite.** The SOFT-tier end-to-end definition (E.33 / #650) lands alongside so the non-TEE lane
is fully specified; until then the *wired* settlement path remains TEE-backed while the spec admits SOFT
as first-class.

**Links.** Amends D-0414, D-0431. Formal:
`FlopSpecs/VerificationStack.lean::sessionVerifier_survives_broken_tee`. Research:
`research/verification-architecture.md`, `research/cheap-miner-hardware-profiles.md`,

## D-0433 — Calibration = quick verified entry and renewable rolling baseline

**Status.** `[LIVE]` — 10-minute verified entry, capacity deposit before activation, timed one-shot
calibration packs, seven-day lease, reboot/periodic renewal, expiry, identity invalidation, and
multi-GPU inventory are implemented. Statistical degradation/downshift and miner-wide overlapping
channel reservation remain E.22.

**Context.** The old runtime combined a six-hour/512-job provisional burst with a weak 90-day phase
machine. The phase machine waited without enforcing continuous work and had no deployed miner state.
It was removed rather than migrated. Permanent burst caps also left stale capacity after hardware or
operating-state changes.

**Decision.**

1. **Quick entry is the serving gate.** A new miner MAY serve after 64 verified jobs over at least 600
   finalized blocks; there is no background Dyno.
2. **Capacity is aggregate per host.** Attestation records bounded `(SKU,count)` inventory. HARD
   ceiling is 90% of the summed per-device ceilings, bounded by measured aggregate throughput.
3. **Revalidation is lifecycle-driven and automated.** A miner-side calibration supervisor detects
   local lifecycle changes; the Ghost-Task issuer supplies unpredictable known-answer work and verifies
   the result. Operators are not expected to manually replay extrinsics after each event.
4. **Same-hardware restart or reconnect takes the quick path.** After process/host restart or recovery
   from an offline interval, the supervisor passes eight fresh canaries and renews the lease.
5. **Hardware identity changes fail closed.** A changed attested inventory, device set, driver/firmware
   measurement, or calibration fingerprint MUST invalidate the prior cap and require a fresh verified
   benchmark burst.
6. **Caps are leases, not immortal facts.** The supervisor MUST renew them periodically in the
   background; missed canaries expire the cap. Existing sessions keep
   their open-time snapshot as an immutable upper bound, but the current cap may tighten settlement;
   new sessions use the latest `calibration_version`.
7. **Stake precedes activation.** The supervisor tops up capacity-proportional self-stake before
   acceptance; the pallet independently rejects an under-bonded proposed cap.
8. **Miner triggering does not mean miner selection.** Calibration credentials are issuer-selected,
   timing-bound, and one-shot. Initial replacement has a 600-block cooldown. Renewal cannot raise cap.

**Rejected.** *90-day entry gate* — unnecessary because the verified, stake-backed burst already bounds
serving. *Reuse a cap forever* — unsafe after reboot drift, long downtime, or hardware replacement.
*Keep the fixed dyno* — elapsed time without enforced coverage is not evidence. *Rerun initial
calibration after every reboot* — unnecessary when unchanged hardware passes fresh canaries.
*Miner-only self-reporting* — gameable; lifecycle detection is local, but acceptance remains bound to
independently verified Ghost Tasks.

**Links.** Issue [#1070](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/1070). Research:
`latency-gflop-calibration-gpu-benchmark.md`.
Code: `pallets/pallets/hw-calibration`, `tee-bridge/poui-oracle/src/issuer.rs`,
`sdk/miner-worker`. YP: §7, E.22, H.4.

## D-0434 — Capacity Reservation Units (CRU) = forward-dated bonded channels, not a new pallet

**Status.** `[ACCEPTED]` — design ratified 2026-07-28. Implementation `[PLANNED]` (#1085); parameter
values (κ, `availability_split_ppm`) still require simulator validation before they are pinned in
`params/`.

**Context.** [D-0410](#d-0410--flop-identity-spot-money--opt-in-bonded-reservations-with-a-state-dependent-survival-governor)
committed FLOP to "spot money + **opt-in bonded reservations**" but left the reservation instrument
unspecified — the load-bearing primitive for F1 (enforceable future delivery) and F2 (a committed
liveness core that cannot exit instantly). Separately, the compute market has no term structure: the
FRCR cash benchmark (v0, observational) measures spot only, and derivatives form on a term tape, not
on quotes. Miners also carry the "missing money" problem: readiness is unpaid, so idle-but-available
capacity is unprofitable.

**Decision.**

1. **The instrument.** A CRU is a miner-**bonded** commitment to serve a fixed rate (`G_n`/sec) over
   a future window at a fixed price. It enforces *delivery* (miner bonds, refusal/downtime is
   slashable), where a session enforces *payment*. It is a prepaid, expiring claim — **not equity,
   not a bonding curve**: no residual claim, no governance, supply capped by the issuer's calibrated
   `B_p`, worthless after expiry.
2. **No new pallet.** With unconstrained session shape params a reservation *is* a forward-dated
   session. Implement as `compute-channel` generalization: optional `start_block`/window, an optional
   miner-bond escrow leg composed from `has-station` `Policy` leaves (D-0417), and an earnout split
   in settlement. `bond = 0` MUST remain byte-for-byte today's session semantics. The reserved
   channel is itself the redemption vehicle — there is no separate draw mechanic for the bilateral
   case.
3. **Payment splits in two.** An **availability** share (protocol constant; **25% is a
   recommendation pending #1091 simulator validation, not a ratified value**) accrues
   against proven Ghost-Task liveness — the missing-money fix, TEE-free — and the usage remainder
   resolves per delivered `G_n`. Buyer non-use is **take-or-pay**: the right lapses, payment resolves
   to the provider, nothing is burned. Miner-caused undelivery returns unearned escrow plus damages.
4. **Bond sizing is derived, not asserted.** `bond = κ · quota · price`; **κ = 1.5 is a
   recommendation pending #1091**, bounded
   below by the 30-day spot-spike quantile (deterministic non-delivery detection ⇒ no `1/p`
   inflation) and above by carry vs margin and by D-0418 stake stacking. **Canonical values live
   only in `params/flop-protocol-params.yaml` + `check_params.py` once validated — this decision
   ratifies the mechanism and the derivation, never the numbers; if this text and `params/` ever
   disagree, `params/` wins.** Slash is **proportional to
   undelivered quota** with pro-rata bond release; 75% compensates the buyer, 25% funds
   backup promotion (Foundation fallback).
5. **Origination is posted and funded, never auctioned.** Miner standing offers and agent pooled bids
   are posted objects whose *posting* locks bond or escrow — that funding IS the anti-spam, so no
   free options exist on-chain. Fills are at posted price (no priced race for a proposer to
   arbitrate); a contested bid claim resolves by claim-window + draw whose entropy MUST NOT be
   knowable during registration (commit the registrant set first; `Babe::randomness()` is fixed one
   epoch ahead and is therefore unsuitable as-is).
6. **Rollout is parameterized, not versioned.** One object ships once; `lot_fraction` launches at
   100% (bilateral, one holder) and governance lowers it to unlock group buying. There is no
   bilateral-then-divisible migration. Tenors are whole UTC days, ≤ 30 days.
7. **Issuance is gated by calibration, not org form.** `reserved_rate ≤` the issuing account's
   on-chain calibrated `B_p`; a pool that calibrates as one entity qualifies, an uncalibrated
   umbrella account does not.

**Parameter-evidence amendment (#1091, 2026-07-28; recommendations only — no `params/` pin).**
The ratified mechanism above is unchanged. The robustness/formal pass updates its unratified launch
numbers and activation preconditions:

**Rejected.** *A dedicated CRU pallet* — duplicates channel machinery and forces a storage migration

## D-0435 — Four-way block-reward split and a halving developer subsidy

**Status.** `[PARTIAL]`. The split arithmetic and the subsidy schedule are `[LIVE]` in
`pallet_block_rewards` / `pallet_flop_subsidy` and pinned in `params/flop-protocol-params.yaml`. The
**distribution policy for the agent and staker legs is not ratified** — those legs mint into sovereign
pool accounts and are never paid out (see *Deferred*, below).

**Supersedes.** The **90/10 miner/validator block-reward split** carried by
[D-0408](#d-0408--single-validator-staking-system-pallet_validators-is-the-authority-source-pallet_staking-removed)
(and its changelog row), and the **subsidy half** of the 2026-06-24 flat-5-FLOP-per-recipient,
era-0-only decision (recorded in `params/` predating this log, per the header rule on D-007/D-008).
D-0408's *other* ratified constraints — `pallet_validators` as the single authority source, delegation,
the freeze/thaw unbonding model, and the auto-compounding of validator earnings into locked stake — are
**unchanged** by this entry.

**Context.** The economics source of record is now the tokenomics workbook
(`FLOP Tokenomics & Airdrop.xlsx`, rev 2026-08-20), mapped onto the repo in
ECON-008. The workbook keeps the emission
curve the repo already implements (96 FLOP/block, 6 halvings, 1.5 FLOP floor; the 10-year total
11,731,392,000 FLOP cross-checks exactly) but changes **how each block is divided** and **what is minted
alongside it**. Two actor classes the repo pays nothing — agents/brokers and plain FLOP stakers — carry
load in the workbook's demand and yield models, and the developer subsidy was re-scoped from a flat
two-year runway to a halving six-year one. The workbook is explicit that the miner leg is the
**residual**: "Any increase in those comes straight out of miner revenue"; the agent leg is "carved OUT
of the miner allocation, not added on top".

**Decision.**

1. **Four-way split, miner as residual.** Each block reward is divided
   `miner_share_ppt = 750` · `validator_share_ppt = 100` · `agent_share_ppt = 100` ·
   `staker_share_ppt = 50`, summing to 1000 ppt. At the era-0 reward of 96 FLOP/block that is
   72 · 9.6 · 9.6 · 4.8 FLOP. The agent (10%) and staker (5%) legs are **carved out of the miner leg**
   (900 → 750 ppt), never added on top.
2. **The validator pool is untouched.** `validator_share_ppt` stays at 100 ppt, so YP §9.1 **R9.5**
   ("the validator 10% pool MUST NOT be increased by an inference surcharge or a second mint") holds
   verbatim and is **not** weakened. R9.5 forbids *increasing* the validator pool; nothing here does.
3. **Agent and staker legs are parked, not paid.** Both legs mint per distribution period into
   protocol-derived `PalletId` sovereign accounts (one per leg) and emit an event. **No distribution
   from either pool is implemented, and none MUST occur until its policy is ratified** (YP §9.1 R9.12,
   Appendix E stub E.40). The staker leg's eligible set is defined — "all FLOP stakers other than miners
   and validators" (workbook, 2026-08-04) — but its basis, cadence, and liquidity are not; the agent
   leg's distribution basis is not specified by the workbook at all.
4. **Developer subsidy: 8 FLOP per recipient, halving, 3 eras.** `flop-subsidy` mints
   `subsidy_per_block_per_recipient = 8` FLOP to **each** of FLOP Labs and the FLOP Foundation
   (16 FLOP/block combined) in halving era 0, halving on the **same 63,072,000-block boundaries** as the
   block reward (8 → 4 → 2 per recipient) and going to **exactly 0 from era 3**:
   `subsidy_duration_blocks = 189_216_000` (~6 years). Unlike the block reward — which floors at 1.5
   FLOP/block in perpetuity — the subsidy **terminates**. Total = 63,072,000 × (16 + 8 + 4) =
   **1,766,016,000 FLOP**, matching the workbook's Tokenomics §5 line.
5. **Genesis reallocation.** The validator airdrop component is cut to the bond and nothing else —
   1,000 × 305,505 = `genesis_validator_airdrop = 305,505,000` FLOP, granted free and bonded at launch,
   with nothing liquid on day 1 (down from 397,353,600). The 91,848,600 freed moves to a **new enforced**
   `genesis_reserve = 588,540,600`, so the four genesis components sum to `genesis_supply`
   (2,483,460,000) under CI. `genesis_supply` itself does **not** change.

**Ratified constraints.**

- The four legs **MUST** sum to 1000 ppt; miner is the residual leg and absorbs every future leg addition
  unless a later decision says otherwise.
- `validator_share_ppt` **MUST** remain 100 ppt under this decision; YP §9.1 R9.5 is unweakened.
- The agent and staker pools **MUST NOT** distribute before E.40 ratifies. Fail-closed: an unratified
  policy means accrual only, never a default payout.
- The subsidy **MUST** halve in lockstep with the block reward and **MUST** be exactly 0 from era 3;
  it is a separate mint, never part of the block reward (YP §9.1 R9.3).
- Canonical values live **only** in `params/flop-protocol-params.yaml` under `check_params.py`. If this
  text and `params/` ever disagree, **`params/` wins**.
- The workbook is the source for actor-level splits and the airdrop schedule; it is **not** authoritative
  over the repo's formal security analysis (see *Deferred* C1).

**Deferred — recorded as open, NOT resolved by this decision.**

## D-0436 — Five halvings, a 3 FLOP perpetual floor, and a subsidy that runs the whole halving phase

**Status.** `[DECIDED]`. Live in `params/flop-protocol-params.yaml`, `RewardMaxHalvings`,
`pallet_flop_subsidy`, the Lean `Emission`/`HalvingBoundary` models, the Quint twins and the macro
simulator.

**Supersedes.** The **sixth-halving clause** of
[D-0421](#d-0421--remove-the-validator-and-laa-auctions-add-a-sixth-halving) — verbatim: "**6th halving
added** (floor 3 → **1.5** at the current 96 FLOP basis, era 6 / Day 4380)". That clause is reversed:
the floor returns to **3 FLOP/block** at era 5. D-0421's *other* ratified constraints — validator
auction and LAA removed, genesis is airdrops only at 2,483,460,000 FLOP, `pallet_genesis_auction`
deleted — are **unchanged** by this entry.

**Amends.** The **subsidy-schedule half** of
[D-0435](#d-0435--four-way-block-reward-split-and-a-halving-developer-subsidy): the 3-era cap becomes a
5-era one. D-0435's four-way 75/10/10/5 split, its miner-as-residual framing, its genesis-airdrop
resizing and its per-recipient era-0 rate of 8 FLOP are **unchanged**.

**Context.** D-0421 added a sixth halving on the reasoning that a 1.5 FLOP/block tail was the
appropriate perpetual issuance at the 96 FLOP basis. Two things have moved since. First, D-0435 turned
the block reward into a four-way split, so the *participant* fraction of the floor is now 75% + 10%
rather than 90% + 10% — the miner floor at 1.5 would have been 1.125 FLOP/block, and the machine-checked
deflation table (`Tokenomics.DeflationThreshold.threshold_table`) put the terminal fee threshold at
6 FLOP/block, a level the fee model does not reach. Second, D-0435 gave the subsidy its own halving
ladder but capped it at 3 eras against the reward's 6, which left an era-3 termination cliff sitting in
the middle of the halving phase rather than at its end.

Restoring the era-5 floor fixes both: the perpetual tail doubles to
[`floor_annual_emission`](../yellowpaper.md#param-floor_annual_emission) = 94,608,000 FLOP/yr, and the
subsidy can be run for exactly the reward's halving phase so the two schedules end on the same
boundary — one by flooring, one by terminating.

**This diverges from the tokenomics workbook, deliberately and on the record.** The workbook
(`FLOP Tokenomics & Airdrop.xlsx`, rev 2026-08-20, Tokenomics §1) states "Number of halvings: **6**",
and ECON-008 §1 lists that row as already-aligned. It is no longer aligned. This decision is the
authority for `max_halvings` and `floor_reward`; the workbook is stale on those two rows until it is
updated. Nothing else in ECON-008's alignment map is affected: the workbook's 10-year block-reward
total (63,072,000 × (96+48+24+12+6) = 11,731,392,000 FLOP) covers eras 0–4 only and is **unchanged**,
because the ladder is identical through era 4 — the divergence is entirely in the tail.

**Decision.**

1. **Five halvings.** [`max_halvings`](../yellowpaper.md#param-max_halvings) = **5**. The ladder is
   `96 → 48 → 24 → 12 → 6 → 3`; era 5 and every era after it pay the floor.
2. **Floor 3 FLOP/block.** [`floor_reward`](../yellowpaper.md#param-floor_reward) = **3**, derived as
   `initial_block_reward >> max_halvings` (96 >> 5) and never set independently.
3. **The perpetual tail doubles.** `floor_annual_emission` 47,304,000 → **94,608,000 FLOP/yr**
   (189,216,000 FLOP/era). This is the single largest economic consequence of this decision and is
   stated here so it is never discovered as a surprise.
4. **Reference totals restated, not re-derived by hand.** `emission_through_halving_eras` =
   `R₀·H·63/32` = **11,920,608,000** FLOP (end of era 5, ~year 12), was 12,015,216,000 at `127/64`.
   The `2·R₀·H` = 12,109,824,000 FLOP milestone keeps its **value** but is now reached one era earlier,
   at the end of **era 6**; the param is therefore **renamed** `emission_at_end_of_era7` →
   `emission_at_end_of_era6` rather than revalued, because the old label had become false.
5. **The subsidy runs the whole halving phase, then stops.**
   [`subsidy_duration_blocks`](../yellowpaper.md#param-subsidy_duration_blocks) = **315,360,000**
   (5 × `halving_interval_blocks`, ~10 years). Per recipient by era: **8, 4, 2, 1, 0.5**, then exactly
   **0 from era 5**. Combined per block: 16, 8, 4, 2, 1, then 0. Total over the schedule =
   63,072,000 × (16+8+4+2+1) = **1,955,232,000 FLOP** (was 1,766,016,000).
6. **The era-4 rate is exact in base units.** 0.5 FLOP per recipient is `8 × 10¹⁸ >> 4 = 5 × 10¹⁷`.
   Because 8 × 10¹⁸ is divisible by 2⁴, the integer shift truncates nothing on any rung and no paying
   era rounds to zero. This is a money path: it is pinned by
   `every_paying_era_is_exact_in_base_units` and `rate_halves_each_era_then_terminates` in
   `pallet-flop-subsidy`, and the lifetime total by
   `total_minted_over_schedule_is_1_955_232_000_flop`.
7. **The subsidy still terminates; the reward still floors.** They now end on the *same* boundary
   (era 5) by *different* mechanisms. A perpetual Labs/Foundation mint remains forbidden.

**Ratified constraints.**

- `floor_reward` **MUST** equal `initial_block_reward >> max_halvings`. It is derived; a future change
  to either input re-derives it, and it is never pinned independently.
- The subsidy **MUST** pay exactly zero from era `max_halvings` onward. It has no floor. A zero rate
  inside the minting window is a schedule/deadline disagreement and **MUST** fail closed
  (`SubsidyScheduleExhausted`), never mint a silent zero.
- `subsidy_duration_blocks` **MUST** equal `SUBSIDY_MAX_ERAS × halving_interval_blocks`, and
  `SUBSIDY_MAX_ERAS` **MUST** equal `RewardMaxHalvings` — asserted in `pallet-flop-subsidy` and in the
  runtime's `subsidy_parameters_match_spec`.
- Every per-era subsidy rate **MUST** be exact in base units. No rounding, no truncation to zero.
- The Day-730 cliff analysis **MUST** use the halving-together figure (112 → 56 = 1/2), not the
  pre-D-0435 29/53. The deepest total-issuance step on the schedule is now era 5 (7 → 3 = 4/7), and the
  anti-exodus work is re-pointed there.
- The workbook divergence **MUST** stay recorded. Any future re-alignment to a sixth halving needs a
  new D-id superseding this one; it is not a params edit.

**Deferred — recorded as open, NOT resolved by this decision.**

Every item deferred by D-0435 (#1350–#1363) remains open and is untouched here. In addition:

**Alternatives rejected.**

  terminate at era 5 while the reward halved once more at era 6, re-creating exactly the mid-phase

- *Let the subsidy floor alongside the reward* — a perpetual Labs/Foundation mint is protocol runway,

- *Defer until the workbook is re-ratified* — leaves the repo implementing a schedule the owner has
  reversed, and hides the divergence instead of recording it. Recording it is the point.

## D-0437 — The validator active set is capped at 1,000

**Status.** `[DECIDED]`, spec-only. Recorded in the **`reference:`** (not `parameters:`) block of
`params/flop-protocol-params.yaml` as `validator_active_set_cap`, and in yellowpaper §15.5 (R15.5b).
Reference-only deliberately: no code holds the value, so there is no implementation site for
`scripts/check_params.py` to gate, and listing it as enforced would report a green drift check over
nothing. **Not yet enforced in the runtime** — see "What is not wired" below and yellowpaper stub E.41.

**Context.** The set-size question has been open since the 2026-07 architecture review, carried as
item 12 ("Finality committee: how many validators, chosen how") and interacting with item 1
(validator stake). Public documentation has stated a 1,000-validator cap throughout; the yellowpaper
stated the opposite — "the active set has no fixed cap … ~1000 validators is the economics operating
point, not a protocol constant" — and the runtime bounded `ActiveValidators` at `ConstU32<10_000>`
with no slot limit anywhere. Three surfaces, two positions, no ratified decision. This entry ratifies
the cap and makes the yellowpaper and the parameter table agree with the documentation.

**Decision.**

1. **Cap = 1,000.** [`validator_active_set_cap`](../yellowpaper.md#param-validator_active_set_cap) =
   **1,000** bounds `ActiveValidators`. It is a protocol constant, not an economics operating point.
2. **The cap bounds admission, never registration.** `register` admits to `ValidatorQueue`; rotation
   promotes from the queue into the active set only while a slot is free. Anyone may register and
   stake at any time — the cap governs only how many hold active seats. This is the shape the pallet's
   own comment assumes ("Add to queue (will be promoted when slots available)"), but only the comment:
   `do_rotate` applies no free-slot check today.
3. **The cap and the committee are separate bounds.** The finalizing committee is
   [`finality_committee_size`](../yellowpaper.md#param-finality_committee_size) = **100**, sampled
   stake-weighted from the PoUI-gated active set (D-0401, §15.4). Capping the set does not change the
   committee draw; it changes the denominator the draw samples from, which is the quantity the
   committee-corruption arithmetic prices.
4. **Rotation is unchanged, and the slot-allocation rule stays open.**
   [`validators_to_eject`](../yellowpaper.md#param-validators_to_eject) =
   [`validators_to_promote`](../yellowpaper.md#param-validators_to_promote) = 50 per
   [`validator_rotation_interval_blocks`](../yellowpaper.md#param-validator_rotation_interval_blocks).
   D-0416 ratifies that ordering as stake-ordered subject to a minimum-performance floor, but D-0416 is
   `[PARTIAL]` because the runtime does not implement it: `do_rotate` promotes with `queue.pop()` — the
   queue **tail**, i.e. the most recent registrant — gated only on `stake >= MinimumValidatorStake`, and
   ejects by (verified-work recency, performance score) rather than stake. D-0437 therefore does **not**
   rest on stake-ordered allocation and does **not** close the "stake-ordered displacement vs first-come"
   half of outstanding-decisions item 12; under today's code the cap would allocate slots
   last-come-first-served at the minimum stake.

**What is not wired.** This decision is deliberately recorded ahead of implementation, because
enforcing it needs a consensus-critical choice that is not made here:

- `MaxAuthorities` = `MAX_ACTIVE_VALIDATORS` = **200** in the runtime, and
  `ValidatorSessionManager::new_session` hands the whole `ActiveValidators` set to `pallet_session`
  untruncated. An active set above 200 therefore collides with the authority bound. Resolving it means
  either raising `MAX_ACTIVE_VALIDATORS` to 1,000 (every active validator becomes a BABE authority) or
  specifying an authority-selection rule that subsets the active set (a third sampling layer). That
  choice is yellowpaper **E.41**, tracked in #1393.
- Rotation applies no slot limit today; only the `ConstU32<10_000>` storage bound applies. Aligning
  that bound is a storage-layout change and needs a decode-compat test.
- The promote path is not stake-ordered (`queue.pop()` + minimum-stake gate), so wiring the cap without
  wiring D-0416's ordering first would make slots first-come — the squatting case item 12 flags as the
  security-relevant one. Both belong in the same change.

Until E.41 closes, 1,000 is the ratified target that the spec and parameter table state, and the
runtime enforces nothing below 10,000.

**Does not supersede.** D-0416's rotation ordering (still `[PARTIAL]`), D-0401's committee sampling,
and D-0413's stake floor are all unchanged. The per-validator stake floor is derived against committee-corruption at
threshold 1/3 and does not move because the set is capped; what the cap does change is the *aggregate*
eligible-stake ceiling, which is the security quantity to track during bootstrap fill.

**Sources.** `whitepaper/outstanding-decisions.template.html` items 1 and 12 (2026-07-08 review).
Code: `pallets/pallets/validators/src/lib.rs` (`ActiveValidators`, `ValidatorQueue`, `register`,
`rotate`), `pallets/runtime/zkverify/src/lib.rs` (`MAX_ACTIVE_VALIDATORS`, `MaxAuthorities`,
`ValidatorSessionManager`). YP: §15.4, §15.5, App. A, App. E (E.41).

## D-0439 — Validator eligibility is verification liveness; re-execution is a checker lane

**Status.** `[DECIDED]`. Supersedes the **work leg** of
[D-0401](#d-0401--consensus--stake-bft-dag-alephbft-pow-ghostdag-deleted)'s committee constraint
("Committee = the PoUI gate: `stake ≥ min_stake` AND recent verified work") and the **verified-work**
component of [D-0416](#d-0416--validator-rotation-orders-by-stake-gated-by-a-minimum-performance-floor)'s
minimum-performance floor. The stake leg, the value-coupled floor (D-0413), stake ranking and the
sampled 100-seat committee (D-0437) are unchanged. Amends the *framing* of
[D-0414](#d-0414--validator-native-attestation-supersedes-m-of-n-oracle-set) (validators remain the
attestation authority; that duty now *is* the liveness signal) and extends
[D-0432](#d-0432--tee-attestation-is-optional-not-a-participation-or-settlement-precondition) (TEE
optional) from miners to validators and checkers. Resolves the §15.1 co-location clause.

**Context.** The yellowpaper's own role definition (§1.1) lists what a validator does — author, finalize,
store and serve DA, attest miners' proofs — and never lists producing PoUI proofs. Yet §15.3 priced a
"produce verified PoUI work (committee gate)" leg as *GPU-heavy, calibrated miner backend*, §15.1 said a
production validator "co-locates or delegates to a calibrated miner backend", and the onboarding and
video packets told operators a TEE GPU keeps them on the committee, while `docs/guides/VALIDATOR_GUIDE.md`
listed a CPU-only box. The code explains the drift:

- `pallet_validators::LastVerifiedWork` has exactly one writer, `note_verified_work`, fed only by the
  runtime adapter `ValidatorWorkRecorder` on `pallet_flop_poui::OnProofVerified`, whose `who` is
  `pending.submitter` — the **miner/prover** (`flop-poui/src/oracle.rs`). A validator refreshes its own
  recency only by mining under its validator account. The compute-channel `on_session_settled` path
  credits `(WorkVesting, BlockRewards)` only, so even a validator co-located with a session-serving miner
  earns no committee credit; only the direct per-task rail counts.
- `poui_committee` / `poui_committee_sampled` fall back to a **stake-only** committee when *no* validator
  has recent work (log warning only), so on every chain without validator-operated miners the work gate
  is inert. The partial state is worse: once a single validator also mines, every other validator fails
  the gate and the committee collapses to that operator — a seat-capture vector, not merely a cost. The
  ENG-1 devnet proof run finalized nothing because the gated committee stayed empty.
- Recency is also the first ejection sort key of rotation (`(is_work_verified_recent, score)`), so
  non-mining validators are ejected first.
- Attestation (D-0414), TOPLOC mismatch and escalation-clear quorums (#754), dispute standing (D-0424) and
  DA serve-or-slash (D-0402/D-0412) are all validator duties that exist in the runtime and are CPU work;
  none of them refreshes recency. Nothing issues duties automatically — `da_registry::issue_audit` and
  `synthetic_tasks::issue_ghost_task` are root-only — and no production client submits attestation
  bundles at all; an unattested pending proof expires with no penalty for validators who did not attest.
- §3.5 already places sampled re-execution with "a different miner", but the TOPLOC checker of #752 had
  each *validator* re-run prefill (`toploc-checker.ts` injects a GPU executor; `report_toploc_mismatch`
  documents the same), which is the one genuinely heavy validator duty in the tree.
- No proof depends on validators being provers: `CommitteeSampling.lean` is abstract over eligible stake
  and seat count, and `CoCVoC.lean` is stake-only algebra. The change is spec + runtime, not Lean.

**Decision.**

1. **Role separation.** A validator secures consensus, verifies, and stores. No validator function —
   registration, active set, committee eligibility, rotation floor, attestation, dispute standing, audits
   — MAY require executing inference, producing PoUI proofs, or owning a GPU or TEE. Validators are not
   miners: a validator account MAY also register as a miner, but miner activity confers no validator
   eligibility.
2. **Committee liveness = accepted verification duties.** The recency signal records the last block at
   which the validator's verification duty was accepted on-chain: (a) signer of an accepted validator
   attestation bundle (R3.6b); (b) signer of an accepted TOPLOC mismatch or escalation-clear quorum
   (R3.5d, §12.1); (c) an accepted DA retrievability-audit response (R5.3b); (d) an accepted dispute
   opening by a validator (R12.1f). Prover credit (`OnProofVerified`) MUST NOT refresh it. The window is
   unchanged: `WorkRecencyWindow` = 86,400 blocks (24 h).
3. **Opportunity guarantee.** Duties (a)–(d) exist only when there is traffic, and a DA audit needs a
   blob to exist (`da_registry::issue_audit` rejects an unknown commitment; genesis seeds none). So the
   protocol MUST issue every active validator at least one **known-answer verification challenge** per
   window — the §8.1 Ghost-Task pattern applied to verification: a VRF-selected fixture (attestation
   bundle or DA opening of a protocol-seeded liveness blob) with a chain-known expected verdict — from
   `on_initialize`, not from root, with a response deadline before the validator's current recency
   lapses. A correct answer is duty (e). A validator that received no challenge in a window is a protocol
   fault, not a validator fault; until issuance is wired the bootstrap profile of E.42 applies.
4. **Rotation floor reads the same signal** (D-0416's floor becomes "verification liveness + uptime").
5. **Re-execution lane (option C).** Re-execution of sampled or challenged turns is a **checker** duty in a
   different trust domain from the miner. *Floor:* three independent bonded checkers — distinct operators
   and trust domains from the miner and from each other, VRF-assigned — returning a **unanimous** verdict;
   any disagreement, unavailable evidence, model or capacity escalates (#754 lifecycle) and MUST NOT
   become an implicit accept. "Distinct operator" is enforced by the only identity binding the protocol
   has, the §2.4 Sybil moat: checker identity is a calibrated miner identity (capacity-proportional
   self-stake, accepted cap, independently attested hardware fingerprint), assignment is stake-weighted
   VRF over that pool excluding the session miner's account and fingerprint, and the three checkers carry
   pairwise-distinct fingerprints — so an adversary with share `q` of selection weight captures all three
   seats with probability ≤ `q³`. A unanimous accept stays challengeable within the R12.1f window with a
   fresh disjoint draw, and an overturned checker forfeits its bond. There is no self-declared operator
   or entity registry to lean on (`MaxSlotsPerEntity` is a doc-comment, not storage). *HARD tier:* a checker verdict carrying a TEE attestation of the
   re-execution is an OPTIONAL strengthening that raises the verdict's assurance tier and permitted value
   cap; it MUST NOT reduce the floor (§0 trust-domain principle; D-0432).
6. **Validators verify the lane, not the model.** Before co-signing a mismatch or clear quorum a validator
   checks assignment validity (VRF), bonds posted, signatures bound to the right commitment and turn, and
   unanimity; then it signs at the R3.6b threshold. Validators MUST NOT be assigned re-execution.
7. **Checker supply and payment.** Checker-eligible identities are calibrated miner identities holding
   the model that post the checker bond — a bare bonded account is not enough (point 5). Miners in
   another trust domain are the expected supply. `claim_audit_fee` pays the lane once it is bound to the
   VRF assignment.
8. **Hardware profile.** A validator is an AlephBFT-class node plus DA custody: 8 physical cores at
   ≥3.4 GHz (SMT off), 32 GB ECC, **4 TB enterprise NVMe**, 1 Gbps symmetric unmetered, no GPU or TEE
   (`validator-miner-hardware-costs.md` §2.1; the 2 TB Aleph Zero / Polkadot reference plus the §5.3 DA
   custody sizing). DA custody is the only heavy leg.

**Ratified constraints.**
- `LastVerifiedWork` changes meaning (last accepted verification duty). Keeping the storage name needs a
  doc note; renaming is a storage-key change that needs the decode-compat test or a genesis reset — the
  implementing PR records which.
- `ValidatorWorkRecorder` leaves the `OnProofVerified` tuple. Every accepted duty credits **all** its
  signers, not only the first `required`.
- The committee gate stays fail-closed; the zero-duty bootstrap fallback and the undersized pool remain
  E.42's problem and are not widened here.
- D-0401's third safety sub-proof is re-scoped from "useful-work integrity" to **verification
  integrity**: the eligibility leg is an accepted verification duty; the reward/settlement leg (tiered
  verification, §3) is unchanged.
- TEE remains optional end to end: for miners (D-0432), for validators (this entry), and for checkers
  (HARD tier only).

**Known consequences left open — deliberately not closed by this entry.**
- Gate wiring (writers, rotation reader, adapter removal, protocol-issued audit cadence, storage naming):
  yellowpaper E.52 · #1552.
- Re-execution lane (checker registry and bonds, VRF assignment, verdict fields on
  `report_toploc_mismatch`, `toploc-checker.ts` split into checker executor and validator verifier):
  E.53 · #1553.
- No validator attestation client exists; until one ships, the liveness signal has no producer:
  #1554.
- E.42 is untouched: the seat-capture bridge and the minimum-safe committee remain open (#848).

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Keep the proof-production gate.* A capture vector in the partial state, inert in the zero state,
  contradicts §1.1's role definition, and no proof depends on it; it also prices every committee seat at
  a GPU.
- *Stake-only committee.* Drops the "did its job" signal entirely and makes the E.42 fallback the
  permanent rule.
- *Uptime heartbeat (ImOnline-style).* Proves presence, not verification; trivially farmable.
- *Validators re-execute inside a TEE (option A alone).* Rests the lane on a single hardware root of
  trust (contra §0 / D-0432) and still puts a GPU on every validator.
- *Validators re-execute (status quo of #752) or a single bonded checker.* No independence between
  prover and checker, or none among checkers.
- *"Any bonded account" as checker identity.* A miner could register several checker accounts and
  unanimously accept its own work; without a Sybil-costly identity the three-checker floor is nominal.
- *A DA retrievability audit as the guaranteed duty.* Needs a blob to exist; a fresh or quiet chain has
  none, so the guarantee would be void exactly when it matters.

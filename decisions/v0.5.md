## D-0501 — Public claim scope and reproducible evidence

**Status:** draft for initial public release. Defines the claim scope and evidence requirements of
v0.5.0; publication requires the prepublication evidence review. Runtime behavior, economic settings,
and cryptographic mechanisms are unchanged.

**Context.** The first public paper needs a single, inspectable statement of its guarantees. Selection
stake and voting seats, artifact existence and proof applicability, signed work and verified execution,
and session call count and byte/work costs are separate quantities. Each claim needs explicit assumptions
and independently inspectable evidence. The finite-population counterexample and sizing calculations are reproduced in
[prepublication-evidence](../research/prepublication-evidence.md).

**Decision.**

1. **Consensus:** use AlephBFT's strict `< ⅓` Byzantine-seat premise (`3f < n`) throughout the current
   specification. The Lean `Consensus.Committee.quorum_inter_gt_third` and `Consensus.FinalCtx.nonforking`
   proofs apply with one unit of voting weight per seat, quorum-certified finalization, and honest
   non-equivocation. Selection stake and voting seats are different quantities. Withdraw any assertion that general
   unequal-stake sampling of distinct identities automatically satisfies a binomial tail bound at the
   attacker's aggregate stake fraction. The mechanism-specific capture proof remains an explicit open
   obligation; no secure global concentration threshold is inferred from this correction.
2. **Verification:** qualify D-0432's soundness wording. TEE independence is a target trust-domain
   requirement, not evidence that a complete SOFT settlement/dispute lane exists. The abstract fallback
   theorem is conditional on its supplied verifier-error bound and enforcement mapping. D-0431's
   mandatory commitment remains; presence, signature/aggregate consistency, execution checks, and
   reward eligibility are stated separately. A multiplicative error model requires justified
   independence or suitable conditional bounds. Closed-channel reward recovery remains open.
3. **Finality:** D-0430's freeze/recovery policy governs stalled finality. A best-head payout claim does
   not follow from a theorem about undisputed channels on one modeled chain. Remove conflicting
   normative permission for best-head escape; do not change the deployed finality path.
4. **Accounting and sizing:** qualify D-0405's measurement language as the implemented reference-work
   convention; physical counters, cache-aware work, and E.30's unit taxonomy require their own evidence
   or definition. Correct direct-rail quorum arithmetic; the inherited 125,000-byte propagation budget
   is a sizing assumption pending workload/topology measurements. D-0427's lane limits are admission targets;
   their lifecycle conversion excludes extra evidence calls and is not a measured throughput result.
5. **Conditional security:** economic deterrence requires effective collectible monitoring and bounded
   total exposure; it is not a Byzantine soundness theorem or a full equilibrium from one inequality.
   DA custody, HTLC inclusion, governance-approved runtime changes, and external demand each retain
   explicit assumptions and unresolved obligations. Historical design rationale is not proof of those
   assumptions.
6. **Publication:** every claim states whether its backing is mathematical, modeled, measured, or
   assumed. Export a curated reproducible evidence bundle and distinguish private artifact existence
   from independent reproducibility. Specification and runtime conformance statuses remain separate.

**Alternatives.** Keeping the existing prose would retain false implications. Changing consensus,
settlement economics, or the verifier while repairing publication would require separate protocol
choices and implementation verification. This decision instead states the guarantees the current
models support and makes the remaining engineering/research obligations reviewable.

**Consequences.** This narrows claims, not admission or payment gates. The public draft is suitable for
review of specified mechanisms and assumptions; it does not certify production security, benchmarked
capacity, or completion of Appendix E. The documented session-key lifetime is corrected from the
unchanged 864,000-block runtime constant, with a regression gate for its day conversion.

**Supersedes by scope.** The unconditional security/measurement/sizing interpretations of D-0401,
D-0405, D-0427 and D-0432 described above; preserves their underlying protocol choices. Reaffirms
D-0430 for finality-sensitive settlement. No historical entry is rewritten.

## D-0502 — Withdraw unmeasured capacity and latency profile

**Status:** proposal pending ratification. Claim-scope change only; runtime limits, dispatch, and
consensus behavior are unchanged. This record does not ratify the disposition.

**Mechanism status:** PENDING. E.46 stays open until runtime benchmarks and the disclosed network
matrix exist; partial encoding evidence does not close it.

**Context.** D-0427 proposed 96 steady/128 burst session-control transactions per block and derived
48/64 cooperative lifecycles per second from two inclusions. The paper also carried a 125,000-byte
propagation scenario and sub-second finality target. None was established by serialized runtime calls,
supported runtime benchmarks, or an end-to-end network/workload matrix. Deterministic SCALE fixtures
now include actual runtime pallet/call indices, extrinsic framing, signature and extension fields, DA
references, and boundary turn/path inputs. They show byte growth but cannot establish throughput or
latency.

**Proposed decision.** If ratified, remove all four figures from the v0.5 conformance profile. State only the configured 5
MiB block-length limit (75% Normal) and two-dimensional runtime weight envelope, neither as a capacity
guarantee. Publish charged dispatch weights as estimates, not measurements: the direct-attestation
weight identifies itself as an unbenchmarked stub, and session weights do not parameterize Merkle-path
length or duplicate scanning. Keep E.46 open until the runtime-benchmark build succeeds and the
disclosed network matrix reports successful, rejected, backlogged, and stalled runs. A future capacity
profile requires a new decision and must distinguish chain admission, serving capacity, and
client-perceived latency.

**Evidence.** [Prepublication evidence](../research/prepublication-evidence.md) and its
[JSON](../evidence/yellowpaper-claim-evidence.json) / [CSV](../evidence/yellowpaper-claim-evidence.csv)
bundle. The recorded runtime-benchmark attempt remains blocked by #550: its WASM dependency graph
mixes Polkadot SDK generations, producing cross-version crypto/type API failures and incompatible
`sp-io` 38.0.2/48.0.0 implementations that abort on duplicate `panic_impl`. Therefore no runtime/build
hash or benchmark output exists. No network run was performed; the empty log/hash/hardware fields and
required workload matrix are retained as negative evidence.

**Consequences if ratified.** The paper makes no finalized-lifecycle throughput or inclusion/finality percentile
claim. Runtime admission continues to enforce its existing byte, weight, state, and validity gates.
Measured profiles may be added only with raw logs, runtime/build hashes, generator and seeds, hardware
profile, configuration, repetitions, uncertainty, and rerun instructions.

**Would supersede by scope.** D-0427's throughput/admission figures and D-0501's temporary treatment of the
125,000-byte and latency figures as targets. Preserves D-0401's one-second BABE slot and every deployed
runtime limit.

## D-0505 — Canonical wire and accounting profile

**Status:** draft for initial public release; runtime and Rust/TypeScript/Python SDK changes are implemented
in the authoring repository. Publication remains subject to approval of the exact public bundle.

**Context.** Appendix F previously summarized fields but left widths, container prefixes, version dispatch,
domain separation, malformed input policy, tree behavior, and migration cutoffs implicit. The runtime tried
multiple historical leaf hashes after signature failure, making the accepted format set difficult to audit.
`G_n` also lacked one numeric taxonomy across the direct and session rails.

**Decision.** Adopt Appendix F's `flop-wire-v1` profile and its public byte-for-byte corpus.

1. Fixed integers are unsigned little-endian; SCALE enums/options/bools and compact lengths are canonical
   and fail closed. Objects reject unknown tags, invalid presence values, truncation, overflow, overlong
   compact integers, and trailing bytes.
2. Direct-rail task producers use the versioned `FLOP/POUI/TASK` preimage. The runtime continues to receive
   its H256 as an opaque local replay key, so active validators must enforce that producer derivation before
   signing. Compute-channel IDs bind the genesis hash, parties, and nonce under a versioned protocol domain;
   receipts use their own domain/version and inherit deployment/session binding through the channel ID.
3. Every current settlement/dispute turn carries a `V0`–`V3` SCALE tag. Verification selects exactly that
   preimage and never retries. Current policy-pinned channels accept V2/V3; pre-policy channels may explicitly
   select V0/V1. V2 is described as lower evidence and cannot be presented as V3/TOPLOC-bound assurance
   (#1404); policy-less legacy formats remain confined by the existing marker (#686).
4. Current DA transcripts use FCC4 with an explicit per-turn leaf version, strict optional-field consistency,
   and exact byte consumption. Historical unprefixed/FCC2/FCC3 reads are migration-only and produce one
   explicit in-memory version before verification.
5. New channels require the tagged receipt. A new `ChannelWireProfiles=1` marker is the cutoff: only absent
   pre-upgrade channels accept the historical untagged receipt, while unknown values fail closed. No channel
   storage value, amount, leaf preimage, or historical signature is rewritten.
6. Settled `G_n` is whole `floor(F_eff/10⁹)`: `u64` on the direct rail and `u128` for sessions, with checked
   conversion/aggregation. Profile v1 bills the full prompt/context term even when a KV-cache prefix is reused.
   Any proof-bound cache discount requires a new meter/profile version (#1153), never reinterpretation.

**Alternatives.** A leading byte inside every historical leaf would invalidate existing roots and signatures.
Continuing trial verification would preserve ambiguity. An additive wire-profile map distinguishes new opens
without rewriting in-flight channel storage.

**Consequences.** E.30 closes; E.51 remains partial. The fixture publishes schema, inputs, exact
bytes/hashes/signature, positive and malformed/domain/version cases, generation commands, and consumer
paths. Production producer/validator enforcement of `task_hash` v1 and cross-version channel-resume receipt
selection remain open in E.51. V2's unbound TOPLOC field and proof-bound cache discounts remain separately
visible under #1404 and #1153/E.22.

**Supersedes by scope.** Appendix F's unversioned receipt and trial-dispatch descriptions, and D-0501's
placeholder treatment of the `G_n` numeric taxonomy. Historical V0–V3 hash preimages remain unchanged.

## D-0506 — Paid agent memory and validator data services

**Status:** draft for initial publication, implementing the requested paid-memory product direction.
Protocol profile and numerical settings remain subject to E.54 ratification and implementation;
this decision does not activate a runtime fee.

**Context.** D-0412 makes DA a validator duty with no byte-fee revenue. Its two retention classes cover
protocol evidence and leased model images. Agents also need private, versioned application storage
and must pay the validators retaining and returning those bytes.

**Decision.**

1. Add a separate paid application-object namespace, retaining sovereign validator custody and
   FLOP-denominated transfers. Preserve existing protocol/model duties and `DataRef` v1 encoding.
   FLOP is not a storage chain: keep on chain only the epoch tariff and capacity declarations, one
   reservation per payer, service lanes, one checkpoint per provider per epoch, and disputes. Object
   commitments, manifests and ordinary memory heads live with the assigned provider group, sequenced
   by the generation-order sequencer and quorum-receipted; heads reach the chain only through
   checkpoint roots or an optional owner pin. Agents, sessions and sub-agents operate inside one
   payer's reservation through delegated capabilities anchored in agent-wallet limits.
2. Fund storage as a fixed-term capacity reservation of reserved encoded bytes on a protocol-assigned
   provider set, priced by one epoch tariff: a per-write setup fee plus rent on reserved byte-blocks.
   The tariff is the stake-weighted median of the votes of validators that declare capacity for the
   epoch, clamped to a per-epoch step bound and any governance floor/ceiling, and stepped up when
   declared capacity stays below demand headroom. Assignment is a stake-weighted random sample among
   declarers; the payer cannot choose providers. Release rent per epoch against accepted audit and
   checkpoint state, rounded once per provider; earn setup at each write's activation receipts;
   include audit/repair/checkpoint overhead and bound liability. A reservation is take-or-pay:
   deleting an object never refunds or settles anything, it frees the bytes for reuse within the same
   term; the remaining term is transferable to another payer by signed consent. Keep
   failed-activation/provider-fault remedies, unused lane budgets and refundable deposits separate.
   Repair cannot repeat setup. Voting is not proof of costs; voluntary capacity and the step rule are
   the clearing mechanism.
3. Pay setup and retrieval through one funded service lane per assigned provider, shared by every
   namespace and delegate, using payer-signed cumulative vouchers over activated writes, verified
   encoded bytes and requests at the lane's pinned tariff version. No log-based debits, double
   redemption, new issuance, inference reward credit, or automatic committee-duty credit. The request
   fee is advanced with the request, before the first chunk, so the unpaid first chunk is no longer
   a free good.
   Retrieval pricing controls demand for validator traffic; it does not prove traffic safety by itself.
   Require direct authenticated pull, bounded retries and atomic aggregate work/egress/credit budgets
   that survive lane churn and restart, including failed admission and repair. Protect protocol reserves;
   fees recycled within a payer/provider coalition are not an external attack cost. No automatic burn
   or new protocol take is introduced.
4. Require conforming clients to encrypt private objects; validators enforce the envelope, commitments,
   signatures and authority, without claiming to prove encryption, secret/random keys or plaintext
   absence for arbitrary uploaders. Authorize operations with attenuated capabilities and wallet limits.
   Use immutable versions and compare-and-swap heads with a provider-quorum receipt, equivocation as
   an objective slashable fault, and an optional chain-pinned head; distinguish revocation and
   tombstones from erasure of copies already acquired.
5. Require admitted capacity/collateral, independent placement, reconstruction at activation,
   objective post-commit audit proofs, bounded repair, and historical assignment liability after exit.
   Calibrate audit sampling so that audit probability times the cost of re-fetching a reconstructing
   shard set at the epoch tariff is at least the provider's rent share per epoch, under E.47's
   independence premise. Count accrued-but-unreleased rent as forfeitable bond. Existing
   known-answer hashes alone cannot establish continuous paid custody.
6. Keep the extension inactive until E.54 fixes the wire/security/economic profile and E.47's relevant
   placement and repair premises are demonstrated. Select rates and limits from measured costs and
   adversarial economic scenarios; do not invent a launch price or modify emission allocation.

**Supersedes by scope.** D-0412's prohibition on DA-fee income applies to §5.3 protocol/model duties;
it no longer prohibits the separately authorized paid application objects in §5.4. D-0412 model
lease deposits and free top-ups, D-0407 lease lifecycles, and protocol-evidence access remain intact. For paid reservations only,
historical assignment liability replaces the §5.3 live-set-only penalty rule; a replacement is never
slashed for its predecessor's service interval. Open PR #548's proposed model-storage economics are
separate and are neither ratified nor activated by this decision.

**Specification.** §5.4, Appendices D, E.54, F.7, G.4, and H.3.

**Rationale & sources.** Agent memory storage market;
economics, mechanism and security review.

## D-0507 — Post-testnet genesis validator selection and placement admission

**Status:** proposal, pending ratification. Spec-only; the wiring task is #1713. Depends on
E.41 (#1393) for any active set above the runtime's 200-authority bound.

**Context.** D-0437 caps the active set at 1,000 and D-0401/§15.4 sample a 100-seat committee from it,
but nothing says how the first 1,000 are chosen. Today `register` enters `ValidatorQueue` only, and
promotion runs at [`validators_to_promote`](../yellowpaper.md#param-validators_to_promote) = 50 per
30-day rotation, so filling the set from the queue alone would take about 600 days. The earlier
allocation ("800 guardian + 200 auction", ECON-003 "top 800 testnet validators" at the legacy 305,505
bond) predates D-0421 (auction removed) and D-0440 (bond 1,200,000). Promotion is first-come at the
minimum stake (`queue.pop()`), the squatting case D-0437 flags. Placement is not an admission input
anywhere, although the seat-capture bound only holds when no fault domain exceeds about 10 % of the
pool (validator-set-diversity §3.3: the finite-pool profile
bounds a 10 % domain at ≤ 5.7e-8/yr and a 15 % domain at ≤ 1.3e-2/yr, while the assumption-free bound
— seats never exceed identities — protects only up to 33 identities, one fewer than the 34 seats
that break `3f < n` at n = 100).

**Decision.**

1. **Genesis seeds the active set directly.** The `flop-mainnet` (and `flop-testnet`) genesis preset
   places the ranked cohort into `ActiveValidators` up to
   [`validator_active_set_cap`](../yellowpaper.md#param-validator_active_set_cap), with session keys and
   bonded self-stake set at genesis and an empty queue. Queue-and-rotation admission (§15.5) applies only
   after genesis.
2. **Ranking key is earned work, not stake.** Testnet stake is faucet-issued, so the cohort is ranked by
   verification liveness on testnet — accepted verification duties per epoch (the `validatorEpochs`
   signal of the testnet earnings rules) — then uptime, then earlier registration as the tie-break.
   Stake enters only as the bond gate at genesis.
3. **Placement is an admission input, capped on count and on stake.** Each candidate signs a placement
   declaration (hosting ASN, metro, operating entity). Genesis composition rejects any cohort in which
   one hosting ASN, one metro, or one operating group exceeds **10 % of the seeded set by validator
   count or 10 % of seeded self-stake**, or in which one entity exceeds `MaxSlotsPerEntity`. Both legs
   are required because the committee draw is stake-weighted while the capture bound is modelled on a
   uniform draw over identities (E.42); capping count alone would admit a small, heavily staked domain
   that the model does not cover. A higher-ranked candidate that would breach a cap yields its seat to
   the next-ranked candidate outside that domain. Foundation-operated seats count against the same
   caps. The histogram the composition produced is published with the chain spec.
4. **Bond grant.** Each seeded validator receives the D-0440 bond (1,200,000 FLOP) through the ECON-003
   grant lane and is bonded at genesis; a candidate whose account is not funded by the snapshot loses
   the seat to the next-ranked candidate.
5. **Post-genesis promotion** is stake-ordered among floor-passing queue entries (D-0416) and fills only
   free slots under the cap (D-0437). First-come promotion at the minimum stake would be withdrawn.

**Ratified constraints (on ratification).** The ranking key, the two-part per-domain cap, and "genesis
seeds directly, rotation thereafter" become §15.2/§15.5 text with R-IDs; both cap legs are recorded in
`params/flop-protocol-params.yaml` as reference parameters until the composition tool enforces them.

**Precedent, and the argument against this decision.** No production chain seeds mainnet genesis seats
by testnet rank. Cosmos Game of Stakes ranked participants primarily on precommit uptime and jail
history, explicitly not on accumulated stake, but it awarded prizes; Hub genesis remained
stake-determined. NEAR's Stake Wars gated *entry* to a restricted mainnet whose foundation-run nodes
were progressively replaced. The softer shape — seed from foundation plus a qualified cohort, admit the
rest permissionlessly — carries less risk than a ranked allocation of 1,000 seats, because a published
ranking is a farming target. Two known failure modes argue the same way: Game of Stakes saw automated
metric-farming inflate the scoreboard, and NEAR's Stake Wars III kicked chunk-only producers on a
metric that measured the wrong role's duty. This decision keeps the ranked form because FLOP's genesis
grants the bond (D-0440) and therefore cannot be permissionless at genesis, but the ratifying reviewer
should weigh the softer alternative explicitly.

**Known weakness.** Ranking by earned work rewards Sybil farming across identities, and the bound that
should contain it — `MaxSlotsPerEntity` — is self-declared and, today, read nowhere in the pallet. The
placement declaration has the same problem: a declaration with no penalty attached is decoration.
Ratifying this decision **MUST** come with a misdeclaration penalty (ejection or slash) and enforcement
of the slot limit, or the caps are advisory.

**Numbers are gated.** The seeded-set size, the 10 % legs and the bond path **MUST NOT** be fixed by
this record. The launch plan seats 20–50 then at most 100 testnet validators, so a 1,000-seat
composition has no cohort to draw from yet, and the decision cannot execute above 200 seats until E.41
closes. The ranking key and the two-part cap are the ratifiable parts; the constants follow the cohort
under #1713.

**Alternatives rejected.** Stake-ranked genesis (ranks faucet balances); queue-only fill (600 days);
auction slots (removed by D-0421); a per-country rather than per-ASN cap (provider ≠ country; two
operators in one OVH site are one fault domain).

**Specification.** §15.2, §15.5, E.41. **Rationale.**
validator-set-diversity §3–§4,
testnet-to-mainnet-conversion.

## D-0508 — Rotation on underperformance: floor-based ejection and on-chain performance signal

**Status:** proposal, pending ratification. Wiring task #1711; model
`formal-specs/economics/validator-rotation.qnt` (variant A = shipped rule, variant B = this decision).

**Context.** SPEC-021 as shipped: a composite score (40 % uptime, 30 % block rate, 20 % accuracy,
10 % latency) written by a **root-origin** `update_performance` oracle, 20 % decay per rotation, ranking
by (recent verified work, score), a **fixed** bottom-[`validators_to_eject`](../yellowpaper.md#param-validators_to_eject) = 50
ejected every 30 days, 7-day cooldown, `rejoin` with stake topped up. Three defects: the score is an
off-chain trust input; a fixed count evicts healthy validators when the whole set performs; and 50 per
30 days drains a failing cohort at 5 % of a 1,000 set per month. "Accuracy" and "latency" have no
operational definition.

**Decision.**

1. **Ejection is floor-based, and bounded.** A validator is eligible for ejection only if it fails the
   floor: no accepted verification duty within `WorkRecencyWindow` (R15.4c), **or** composite score below
   `performance_floor` (proposed 5,000 of 10,000). Rotation ejects floor-failing validators, lowest score
   first, up to a per-rotation cap of **10 % of the active set** (100 of 1,000) and promotes into the
   freed slots. A validator above the floor with recent verified work is never ejected by rotation.

   Three guards make a wrong floor survivable, and they matter more than the floor value itself:

   - **No evidence, no ejection.** A validator with fewer than a minimum number of scored samples in the
     window is not ejectable. This one predicate covers the bootstrap case (a fresh chain where every
     score is zero), the quiet-chain case (duty signals exist only when there is traffic, which is why
     R15.4c mandates protocol-issued challenges), and freshly promoted validators, which today enter with
     `performance_score = 0`.
   - **Minimum-set guard on every removal path.** Rotation, jail and kick **MUST NOT** reduce the active
     set below a minimum bound, and **MUST** skip rather than partially apply an ejection that would.
     Without it the shipped rule removes the entire set on any network of 50 or fewer validators
     (defect #1727): block production continues on stale authorities while the finality committee, which
     derives from the active set, empties and AlephBFT stops. That defect is open (#1727, E.56); the
     guard is the same predicate and may land ahead of the rest of this decision.
   - **The per-rotation cap is fixed, not tunable.** It is the blast-radius bound that makes a wrong floor
     or a mis-derived signal recoverable, so it does not become a governance knob.
2. **The score is derived on chain.** The four components are redefined as observable signals and the
   root-origin oracle is retired once they land: *uptime* = BABE slots authored / slots assigned over the
   interval (measurable for the whole active set); *participation* = seated AlephBFT sessions in which the
   validator contributed units before session close, reported by `pallet_aleph` (each validator on a
   1,000 pool is seated in about 10 % of sessions, roughly 290 samples per 30 days at 900-block sessions,
   about 72 at 3,600); *duty* = accepted verification duties plus known-answer challenges answered, over
   challenges issued (R15.4c); *lag* = median DAG-head distance while seated (the AlephBFT performance
   metric). The 40/30/20/10 weights are kept provisionally for these four in that order.

   **The lag component carries a grace band.** Scoring distance from the DAG frontier penalises honest but
   geographically distant validators, which is in direct tension with D-0507's placement caps: a metric
   that rewards proximity pushes operators into one metro. The lag component **MUST** therefore apply no
   penalty within a grace band sized to worst-case honest round-trip time, and score only beyond it. This
   is the shape Solana adopted for the same problem in SIMD-0033 (Timely Vote Credits), which sizes its
   grace from the roughly 100 ms maximum latency between two points on Earth and states the goal
   explicitly: a strict schedule "would encourage all validators to move to the same country". The band is
   measured in the wiring task, not guessed here.
3. **What ejection means** is unchanged: seat, rewards, and committee eligibility are lost for at least
   the [`ejection_cooldown_blocks`](../yellowpaper.md#param-ejection_cooldown_blocks) window; stake is
   thawed, not slashed. Liveness slashing (§15.7) is a separate path and may precede ejection.
4. **Score decay** (20 % per rotation) is retained so the floor reflects recent behaviour. Because
   decay runs before the floor test, a seat below `floor / 0.8` (6,250 at a 5,000 floor) re-enters the
   below-floor cohort at the next rotation; the floor is therefore evaluated on the pre-decay score of
   the rotation that ejects.
5. **Promoted validators keep their queued score.** The shipped `do_rotate` inserts promotions with
   `performance_score = 0`, which under a floor rule would make every fresh promotion eviction-eligible
   at the next rotation; promotion carries the candidate's queue score forward.
6. **Three declared constants are wired.** `EjectionCooldownBlocks`, `ScoreDecayPerRotation`, and
   `MaxSlotsPerEntity` are declared in the pallet `Config` and read nowhere; the Quint witnesses
   `witness_A_cooldown_bypass` (re-register in the same block), `witness_A_entity_overshoot` (six slots
   against a five-slot limit), and `witness_A_popped_candidate_discarded` (an under-staked popped
   candidate is dropped from both sets) show the consequences. All three become enforced predicates in
   the same change as the floor.

**Supersedes by scope.** D-0416's second leg ("eject below-floor, then rotate out the bottom-N by
stake of the remainder") would be withdrawn: ejection is floor-only, and stake remains the *promotion* key.
The Quint invariant `inv_no_healthy_eviction` (I6) is stated for that reading.

**Ratified constraints (on ratification).** R15.5 is reworded from "eject the bottom 50" to the floor
rule; `performance_floor` and the per-rotation cap enter the parameter table; the Quint invariants
I1–I8 of `validator-rotation.qnt` hold for the wired rule (I1, I2, I3, I5 are additionally
model-checked by Apalache to depth 4; the rest by simulation).

**What the model does and does not cover.** `validator-rotation.qnt` models the rotation **decision
rule** — the ejection predicate, the promotion ordering, the active-set cap, the ejection cooldown and
the per-entity slot limit — and deliberately does **not** model score provenance: in both variants
`performance_score` enters as an arbitrary write of the four components through the root
`update_performance` oracle, so an oracle-written score and a score derived from observable BABE /
Aleph / duty / DAG signals are indistinguishable in it. An implementation that retires nothing and
keeps the root oracle satisfies every invariant. Decision point 2 above is therefore **not** covered by
any formal artifact today; the spec that would cover it accumulates the observable counters (slots
assigned and authored, seated sessions expected and participated, duties issued and answered, lag
samples), derives the composite from them, and carries a witness that an arbitrary oracle write breaks
the derivation. That spec is task #1719 and is a precondition for claiming the provenance leg is
verified.

**Precedent.** Aleph Zero, the production deployment of this consensus family, already runs an absolute
floor with hysteresis on exactly these two signals: it suspends a validator after more than 48 sessions
below 80 % of expected blocks, or more than 24 sessions more than 11 units behind the DAG frontier, with
counters reset every 10 eras. The counter-with-reset shape is preferred to a single-rotation threshold,
for the same reason as the guards above: it survives a transient outage. The wider trend separates
ejection from slashing — Cosmos Hub has an open proposal to remove its downtime slash outright and
Osmosis runs without one. D-0409's 1 % jail and 5 % kick are not revisited here; the interaction is
flagged for the ratifying reviewer.

**Numbers are gated, principles are not.** The floor value, the sample minimum, the grace band and the
weights **MUST NOT** be fixed by this record: they need the score distribution of a real cohort, which no
network has produced. This decision ratifies the shape — floor rather than bottom-N, derived rather than
oracular, bounded blast radius, no ejection without evidence. The constants are set after one or two
rotations of testnet data, under #1711.

**Alternatives rejected.** Keep the fixed bottom-N (evicts healthy nodes, and at 50 per 30 days cannot
drain a bad cohort); keep the root oracle (a single key decides who is ejected); rank by stake for
ejection (the spec already forbids padding the liveness signal to buy rank, and stake is the *promotion*
key, not the *ejection* key); a floor relative to the set's median (re-opens the tournament dynamic
D-0416 removed, and mass-ejects when a network-wide incident moves the median).

**Specification.** §15.4, §15.5. **Rationale.**
validator-set-diversity §1, SPEC-021, D-0416, D-0437.

## D-0509 — Committee session cadence aligned to the BABE epoch

**Status:** proposal, pending ratification. Consensus safety zone: wiring task #1712 needs CTO
ratification and a devnet handover measurement before the constant flips.

**Context.** `AlephEpochBlocks` = 900 (~15 min) while the BABE epoch is 3,600 blocks. The committee
seed is BABE epoch randomness, fixed for the epoch and one epoch old (R15.4b), so the four sessions
inside one BABE epoch are predictable together: a 15-minute session does not shorten the
adaptive-corruption window below the epoch. The Lean model prices the cadence directly: on the finite
1,000 pool at 900 blocks the yearly union bound is 5.7e-8 at 10 % attacker concentration and 1.3e-2 at
15 %; at 3,600 blocks the 10 % bound is 1.4e-8 and the binomial 15 % bound falls from 6.44e-2 to 1.61e-2
(`CommitteeSampling.pool_p10_year_capture_le_5_7e_minus_8`, `pool_p10_hourly_refresh_capture_le_1_43e_minus_8`,
`p15HourlyRefreshRisk`). Shorter sessions buy faster dilution of a bad draw and more scoring samples; they
cost three extra committee handovers per epoch and four times the union-bound terms. Aleph Zero runs
900-block sessions with a ~14-seat finality committee; FLOP's 100 seats make handover the larger cost.

**Decision.**

1. `AlephEpochBlocks` = `EpochDurationInBlocks` (3,600 in production, 60 in fast/dev builds). Committee
   refresh coincides with the BABE epoch boundary; the domain-separated seed (R15.4b) is unchanged.
2. The live Lean profile becomes the 3,600-block draw count (`epochYearDraws 3600` = 8,760) and
   `scripts/check_lean_constants.py` continues to tie `AlephEpochBlocks` to it. The seed-correlation
   note on `draws_union_bound` already sizes `AdaptiveCorruptionBoundary.epochDuration` against the BABE
   epoch rather than `AlephEpochBlocks`.
3. Scoring under D-0508 is re-based on about 2.4 seatings per validator per day (about 72 per 30-day
   rotation on a 1,000 pool), which the decision records as adequate.
4. Before the constant flips: measure session-handover cost (keychain rebuild, DAG catch-up) at a
   100-seat committee on the devnet and record it in
   validator-set-diversity.

**Precedent.** Every documented beacon-driven committee design derives all committees of a period from
one seed fixed before that period begins: Ethereum computes an epoch's committees from a RANDAO mix two
epochs old (`MIN_SEED_LOOKAHEAD`, with `MAX_SEED_LOOKAHEAD` bounding activation grinding), and Cardano
fixes the epoch nonce and stake snapshot in the preceding epoch. FLOP's 900-block session inside a
3,600-block seed period is the inverse of that pattern and buys no unpredictability. We found no paper
naming this as an error directly, so the argument rests on the uniformity of the precedent rather than a
citation.

**Alternatives rejected.** Keep 900 (buys bad-draw dilution only; the seed already fixes committees for
the epoch); 28,800 (8 h; a validator is seated 0.3×/day, too few samples for rotation scoring, and a bad
draw lives eight hours); a per-session fresh seed (a different mechanism, E.42).

**Specification.** §2.2, §15.4, parameter table. **Rationale.** `CommitteeSampling.lean`
(`epochYearDraws_antitone`), validator-set-diversity §1.

## D-0510 — Bootstrap nodes and the public discovery plane

**Status:** proposal, pending ratification. Operational, not a runtime change; the launch checklist item
is P-11 in `docs/TESTNET_MAINNET_LAUNCH_PLAN.md` and the procedure is
`docs/setup/bootnodes.md`.

**Context.** On stock Substrate a bootnode is only a peer-discovery seed. On FLOP it is load-bearing:
`aleph_reserved_from_network()` (`pallets/relay-node/service/src/aleph_support.rs`) unions the CLI
`--reserved-nodes` with the chain spec's `bootNodes`, and that union is the reserved peer set of both
Aleph notification protocols on every node, retained across committee rotations, with 50 peer slots
above the committee cap held for archive/RPC/bootnodes (PR #743). A `bootNodes` entry is therefore a
permanent dial target for finality gossip, and a bootnode outage degrades the validator clique's seed,
not just fresh-node discovery. Today the only bootnode is `alice` inside the devnet compose, no public
FLOP bootnode, RPC, or telemetry host exists, `flop_simnet` and `flop_devnet` share `protocol_id =
"flop"` (a cross-network peering hazard), and P-01 ("testnet chain spec generated") has no bootnode
input although the spec must carry the multiaddrs.

**Decision.**

1. **Bootnodes are a launch requirement**, cut before the chain spec: testnet ≥ 2 on ≥ 2 hosting ASNs
   and ≥ 2 continents; mainnet ≥ 3 on ≥ 3 hosting ASNs and ≥ 2 continents, IPv4 and IPv6, behind DNS
   names with persisted node keys (`zkv-relay key generate-node-key --file`, `--node-key-file`,
   mode 0400).
2. **A bootnode is not a validator** and carries no session keys; it is a non-validating full node on a
   modest profile, never co-located with a validator, and it counts toward the same 10 % per-domain
   placement cap as validators ([D-0507](#d-0507--post-testnet-genesis-validator-selection-and-placement-admission)).
   Validator IPs are not published.
3. **Each network has a unique `protocol_id`** before any public bootnode exists.
4. **Retirement** keeps a retired bootnode reachable for one deprecation window after the chain spec or
   docs drop it, because running nodes keep dialing it as a reserved peer.
5. **A community bootnode list** with a submission process complements the foundation set; the public
   RPC and telemetry endpoints follow the same placement rule as the discovery plane.

**Known cost.** Chain-spec bootnode lists are a standing maintenance burden: Polkadot's RFC-0008 opens
by noting that every add or remove requires updating every chain spec, and its issue tracker carries
recurring wrong-address, peer-ID-mismatch and upgrade-ban failures. That is the argument for keeping the
foundation list small and stable, letting DHT discovery carry the rest, and treating the community list
as additive rather than load-bearing. It is also why retirement keeps a deprecation window (point 4):
running nodes hold these addresses as reserved peers.

**Alternatives rejected.** No bootnodes (DHT-only discovery; the Aleph reserved seed would be empty on
fresh nodes); bootnodes on validator hosts (publishes validator IPs and couples the discovery plane to
the committee's fault domains); a single provider (the Aleph Zero public plane is OVH-majority, which is
the pattern this decision avoids).

**Specification.** `docs/setup/bootnodes.md`, `docs/setup/network-launch-readiness.md`, P-11.
**Rationale.** validator-set-diversity §2 (public discovery
plane), `aleph_support.rs` reserved-peer seeding.

## D-0511 — One canonical compute-channel transcript and receipt definition

**Status:** draft for initial public release; specification clarification only. Runtime behavior and
wire bytes are unchanged.

**Context.** R12.1b and Appendix F.3 were both normative but described different transcript leaves.
R12.1b restated the legacy V0 field set under the undefined name `session_id`, while F.3 defines the
versioned leaves accepted by the runtime, including V3's decode-policy, token-ID, TOPLOC, and timing
bindings. The duplicate wording could lead a current client to sign a leaf that settlement rejects. The
receipt's `payable` field and the per-channel scope of `decode_policy_hash` were also implicit in runtime
state rather than defined by the specification.

**Decision.** Appendix F.3 is the sole compute-channel transcript-leaf and agent-receipt wire definition.
R12.1b and the informative walkthrough point to it instead of restating a preimage; `channel_id` is the
canonical identifier. `ChannelDecodePolicies[channel_id]` pins one policy at open for every turn,
including a multi-agent pipeline. Receipt `payable` is the gross receipt-bound settlement amount in
10⁻¹⁸ FLOP base units, before audit and forward adjustments: full reserved escrow on cooperative
settlement, the R12.1d tariff on unilateral settlement, or the stored forward-contract payment when that
override exists. The miner's net transfer is that amount less the audit earmark, and a forward channel
additionally refunds unearned availability to the buyer. The walkthrough's cooperative `open_channel`
signature matches Appendix G.1.

**Consequences.** Current clients obtain leaf versioning, TOPLOC binding, receipt fields, policy scope,
and payout derivation from one normative location. Existing leaf hashes, signatures, markers, balances,
and settlement behavior do not change.

**Alternatives.** Defining `session_id` as an alias would preserve two leaf definitions. Giving §12.1
precedence over Appendix F would make the legacy tuple authoritative and break current settlement.
Leaving `payable` to client inference would keep cooperative and unilateral receipt signatures
ambiguous under partial use.

**Supersedes by scope.** D-0505's Appendix F profile is preserved; this entry removes the conflicting
R12.1b restatement and makes its already-implemented per-channel policy and payout derivations explicit.

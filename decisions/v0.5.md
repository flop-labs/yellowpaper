## D-0501 — Public claim scope and reproducible evidence

**Status:** draft for initial public release. Defines the claim scope and evidence requirements of
v0.5.0; publication requires the prepublication evidence review. Runtime behavior, economic settings,
and cryptographic mechanisms are unchanged.

**Context.** The first public paper needs a single, inspectable statement of its guarantees. Selection
stake and voting seats, artifact existence and proof applicability, signed work and verified execution,
and session call count and byte/work costs are separate quantities. Each claim needs explicit assumptions
and independently inspectable evidence. The finite-population counterexample and sizing calculations are reproduced in
[prepublication-evidence](../research/prepublication-evidence.md).

**Decision.**

1. **Consensus:** use AlephBFT's strict `< ⅓` Byzantine-seat premise (`3f < n`) throughout the current
   specification. The Lean `Consensus.Committee.quorum_inter_gt_third` and `Consensus.FinalCtx.nonforking`
   proofs apply with one unit of voting weight per seat, quorum-certified finalization, and honest
   non-equivocation. Selection stake and voting seats are different quantities. Withdraw any assertion that general
   unequal-stake sampling of distinct identities automatically satisfies a binomial tail bound at the
   attacker's aggregate stake fraction. The mechanism-specific capture proof remains an explicit open
   obligation; no secure global concentration threshold is inferred from this correction.
2. **Verification:** qualify D-0432's soundness wording. TEE independence is a target trust-domain
   requirement, not evidence that a complete SOFT settlement/dispute lane exists. The abstract fallback
   theorem is conditional on its supplied verifier-error bound and enforcement mapping. D-0431's
   mandatory commitment remains; presence, signature/aggregate consistency, execution checks, and
   reward eligibility are stated separately. A multiplicative error model requires justified
   independence or suitable conditional bounds. Closed-channel reward recovery remains open.
3. **Finality:** D-0430's freeze/recovery policy governs stalled finality. A best-head payout claim does
   not follow from a theorem about undisputed channels on one modeled chain. Remove conflicting
   normative permission for best-head escape; do not change the deployed finality path.
4. **Accounting and sizing:** qualify D-0405's measurement language as the implemented reference-work
   convention; physical counters, cache-aware work, and E.30's unit taxonomy require their own evidence
   or definition. Correct direct-rail quorum arithmetic; the inherited 125,000-byte propagation budget
   is a sizing assumption pending workload/topology measurements. D-0427's lane limits are admission targets;
   their lifecycle conversion excludes extra evidence calls and is not a measured throughput result.
5. **Conditional security:** economic deterrence requires effective collectible monitoring and bounded
   total exposure; it is not a Byzantine soundness theorem or a full equilibrium from one inequality.
   DA custody, HTLC inclusion, governance-approved runtime changes, and external demand each retain
   explicit assumptions and unresolved obligations. Historical design rationale is not proof of those
   assumptions.
6. **Publication:** every claim states whether its backing is mathematical, modeled, measured, or
   assumed. Export a curated reproducible evidence bundle and distinguish private artifact existence
   from independent reproducibility. Specification and runtime conformance statuses remain separate.

**Alternatives.** Keeping the existing prose would retain false implications. Changing consensus,
settlement economics, or the verifier while repairing publication would require separate protocol
choices and implementation verification. This decision instead states the guarantees the current
models support and makes the remaining engineering/research obligations reviewable.

**Consequences.** This narrows claims, not admission or payment gates. The public draft is suitable for
review of specified mechanisms and assumptions; it does not certify production security, benchmarked
capacity, or completion of Appendix E. The documented session-key lifetime is corrected from the
unchanged 864,000-block runtime constant, with a regression gate for its day conversion.

**Supersedes by scope.** The unconditional security/measurement/sizing interpretations of D-0401,
D-0405, D-0427 and D-0432 described above; preserves their underlying protocol choices. Reaffirms
D-0430 for finality-sensitive settlement. No historical entry is rewritten.

## D-0502 — Withdraw unmeasured capacity and latency profile

**Status:** proposal pending ratification. Claim-scope change only; runtime limits, dispatch, and
consensus behavior are unchanged. This record does not ratify the disposition.

**Mechanism status:** PENDING. E.46 stays open until runtime benchmarks and the disclosed network
matrix exist; partial encoding evidence does not close it.

**Context.** D-0427 proposed 96 steady/128 burst session-control transactions per block and derived
48/64 cooperative lifecycles per second from two inclusions. The paper also carried a 125,000-byte
propagation scenario and sub-second finality target. None was established by serialized runtime calls,
supported runtime benchmarks, or an end-to-end network/workload matrix. Deterministic SCALE fixtures
now include actual runtime pallet/call indices, extrinsic framing, signature and extension fields, DA
references, and boundary turn/path inputs. They show byte growth but cannot establish throughput or
latency.

**Proposed decision.** If ratified, remove all four figures from the v0.5 conformance profile. State only the configured 5
MiB block-length limit (75% Normal) and two-dimensional runtime weight envelope, neither as a capacity
guarantee. Publish charged dispatch weights as estimates, not measurements: the direct-attestation
weight identifies itself as an unbenchmarked stub, and session weights do not parameterize Merkle-path
length or duplicate scanning. Keep E.46 open until the runtime-benchmark build succeeds and the
disclosed network matrix reports successful, rejected, backlogged, and stalled runs. A future capacity
profile requires a new decision and must distinguish chain admission, serving capacity, and
client-perceived latency.

**Evidence.** [Prepublication evidence](../research/prepublication-evidence.md) and its
[JSON](../evidence/yellowpaper-claim-evidence.json) / [CSV](../evidence/yellowpaper-claim-evidence.csv)
bundle. The recorded runtime-benchmark attempt remains blocked by #550: its WASM dependency graph
mixes Polkadot SDK generations, producing cross-version crypto/type API failures and incompatible
`sp-io` 38.0.2/48.0.0 implementations that abort on duplicate `panic_impl`. Therefore no runtime/build
hash or benchmark output exists. No network run was performed; the empty log/hash/hardware fields and
required workload matrix are retained as negative evidence.

**Consequences if ratified.** The paper makes no finalized-lifecycle throughput or inclusion/finality percentile
claim. Runtime admission continues to enforce its existing byte, weight, state, and validity gates.
Measured profiles may be added only with raw logs, runtime/build hashes, generator and seeds, hardware
profile, configuration, repetitions, uncertainty, and rerun instructions.

**Would supersede by scope.** D-0427's throughput/admission figures and D-0501's temporary treatment of the
125,000-byte and latency figures as targets. Preserves D-0401's one-second BABE slot and every deployed
runtime limit.

## D-0505 — Canonical wire and accounting profile

**Status:** draft for initial public release; runtime and Rust/TypeScript/Python SDK changes are implemented
in the authoring repository. Publication remains subject to approval of the exact public bundle.

**Context.** Appendix F previously summarized fields but left widths, container prefixes, version dispatch,
domain separation, malformed input policy, tree behavior, and migration cutoffs implicit. The runtime tried
multiple historical leaf hashes after signature failure, making the accepted format set difficult to audit.
`G_n` also lacked one numeric taxonomy across the direct and session rails.

**Decision.** Adopt Appendix F's `flop-wire-v1` profile and its public byte-for-byte corpus.

1. Fixed integers are unsigned little-endian; SCALE enums/options/bools and compact lengths are canonical
   and fail closed. Objects reject unknown tags, invalid presence values, truncation, overflow, overlong
   compact integers, and trailing bytes.
2. Direct-rail task producers use the versioned `FLOP/POUI/TASK` preimage. The runtime continues to receive
   its H256 as an opaque local replay key, so active validators must enforce that producer derivation before
   signing. Compute-channel IDs bind the genesis hash, parties, and nonce under a versioned protocol domain;
   receipts use their own domain/version and inherit deployment/session binding through the channel ID.
3. Every current settlement/dispute turn carries a `V0`–`V3` SCALE tag. Verification selects exactly that
   preimage and never retries. Current policy-pinned channels accept V2/V3; pre-policy channels may explicitly
   select V0/V1. V2 is described as lower evidence and cannot be presented as V3/TOPLOC-bound assurance
   (#1404); policy-less legacy formats remain confined by the existing marker (#686).
4. Current DA transcripts use FCC4 with an explicit per-turn leaf version, strict optional-field consistency,
   and exact byte consumption. Historical unprefixed/FCC2/FCC3 reads are migration-only and produce one
   explicit in-memory version before verification.
5. New channels require the tagged receipt. A new `ChannelWireProfiles=1` marker is the cutoff: only absent
   pre-upgrade channels accept the historical untagged receipt, while unknown values fail closed. No channel
   storage value, amount, leaf preimage, or historical signature is rewritten.
6. Settled `G_n` is whole `floor(F_eff/10⁹)`: `u64` on the direct rail and `u128` for sessions, with checked
   conversion/aggregation. Profile v1 bills the full prompt/context term even when a KV-cache prefix is reused.
   Any proof-bound cache discount requires a new meter/profile version (#1153), never reinterpretation.

**Alternatives.** A leading byte inside every historical leaf would invalidate existing roots and signatures.
Continuing trial verification would preserve ambiguity. An additive wire-profile map distinguishes new opens
without rewriting in-flight channel storage.

**Consequences.** E.30 closes; E.51 remains partial. The fixture publishes schema, inputs, exact
bytes/hashes/signature, positive and malformed/domain/version cases, generation commands, and consumer
paths. Production producer/validator enforcement of `task_hash` v1 and cross-version channel-resume receipt
selection remain open in E.51. V2's unbound TOPLOC field and proof-bound cache discounts remain separately
visible under #1404 and #1153/E.22.

**Supersedes by scope.** Appendix F's unversioned receipt and trial-dispatch descriptions, and D-0501's
placeholder treatment of the `G_n` numeric taxonomy. Historical V0–V3 hash preimages remain unchanged.

## D-0506 — Paid agent memory and validator data services

**Status:** draft for initial publication, implementing the requested paid-memory product direction.
Protocol profile and numerical settings remain subject to E.54 ratification and implementation;
this decision does not activate a runtime fee.

**Context.** D-0412 makes DA a validator duty with no byte-fee revenue. Its two retention classes cover
protocol evidence and leased model images. Agents also need private, versioned application storage
and must pay the validators retaining and returning those bytes.

**Decision.**

1. Add a separate paid application-object namespace, retaining sovereign validator custody and
   FLOP-denominated transfers. Preserve existing protocol/model duties and `DataRef` v1 encoding.
   FLOP is not a storage chain: keep on chain only the epoch tariff and capacity declarations, one
   reservation per payer, service lanes, one checkpoint per provider per epoch, and disputes. Object
   commitments, manifests and ordinary memory heads live with the assigned provider group, sequenced
   by the generation-order sequencer and quorum-receipted; heads reach the chain only through
   checkpoint roots or an optional owner pin. Agents, sessions and sub-agents operate inside one
   payer's reservation through delegated capabilities anchored in agent-wallet limits.
2. Fund storage as a fixed-term capacity reservation of reserved encoded bytes on a protocol-assigned
   provider set, priced by one epoch tariff: a per-write setup fee plus rent on reserved byte-blocks.
   The tariff is the stake-weighted median of the votes of validators that declare capacity for the
   epoch, clamped to a per-epoch step bound and any governance floor/ceiling, and stepped up when
   declared capacity stays below demand headroom. Assignment is a stake-weighted random sample among
   declarers; the payer cannot choose providers. Release rent per epoch against accepted audit and
   checkpoint state, rounded once per provider; earn setup at each write's activation receipts;
   include audit/repair/checkpoint overhead and bound liability. A reservation is take-or-pay:
   deleting an object never refunds or settles anything, it frees the bytes for reuse within the same
   term; the remaining term is transferable to another payer by signed consent. Keep
   failed-activation/provider-fault remedies, unused lane budgets and refundable deposits separate.
   Repair cannot repeat setup. Voting is not proof of costs; voluntary capacity and the step rule are
   the clearing mechanism.
3. Pay setup and retrieval through one funded service lane per assigned provider, shared by every
   namespace and delegate, using payer-signed cumulative vouchers over activated writes, verified
   encoded bytes and requests at the lane's pinned tariff version. No log-based debits, double
   redemption, new issuance, inference reward credit, or automatic committee-duty credit. The request
   fee is advanced with the request, before the first chunk, so the unpaid first chunk is no longer
   a free good.
   Retrieval pricing controls demand for validator traffic; it does not prove traffic safety by itself.
   Require direct authenticated pull, bounded retries and atomic aggregate work/egress/credit budgets
   that survive lane churn and restart, including failed admission and repair. Protect protocol reserves;
   fees recycled within a payer/provider coalition are not an external attack cost. No automatic burn
   or new protocol take is introduced.
4. Require conforming clients to encrypt private objects; validators enforce the envelope, commitments,
   signatures and authority, without claiming to prove encryption, secret/random keys or plaintext
   absence for arbitrary uploaders. Authorize operations with attenuated capabilities and wallet limits.
   Use immutable versions and compare-and-swap heads with a provider-quorum receipt, equivocation as
   an objective slashable fault, and an optional chain-pinned head; distinguish revocation and
   tombstones from erasure of copies already acquired.
5. Require admitted capacity/collateral, independent placement, reconstruction at activation,
   objective post-commit audit proofs, bounded repair, and historical assignment liability after exit.
   Calibrate audit sampling so that audit probability times the cost of re-fetching a reconstructing
   shard set at the epoch tariff is at least the provider's rent share per epoch, under E.47's
   independence premise. Count accrued-but-unreleased rent as forfeitable bond. Existing
   known-answer hashes alone cannot establish continuous paid custody.
6. Keep the extension inactive until E.54 fixes the wire/security/economic profile and E.47's relevant
   placement and repair premises are demonstrated. Select rates and limits from measured costs and
   adversarial economic scenarios; do not invent a launch price or modify emission allocation.

**Supersedes by scope.** D-0412's prohibition on DA-fee income applies to §5.3 protocol/model duties;
it no longer prohibits the separately authorized paid application objects in §5.4. D-0412 model
lease deposits and free top-ups, D-0407 lease lifecycles, and protocol-evidence access remain intact. For paid reservations only,
historical assignment liability replaces the §5.3 live-set-only penalty rule; a replacement is never
slashed for its predecessor's service interval. Open PR #548's proposed model-storage economics are
separate and are neither ratified nor activated by this decision.

**Specification.** §5.4, Appendices D, E.54, F.7, G.4, and H.3.

**Rationale & sources.** Agent memory storage market;
economics, mechanism and security review.

## D-0507 — Post-testnet genesis validator selection and placement admission

**Status:** proposal, pending ratification. Spec-only; the wiring task is #1713. Depends on
E.41 (#1393) for any active set above the runtime's 200-authority bound.

**Context.** D-0437 caps the active set at 1,000 and D-0401/§15.4 sample a 100-seat committee from it,
but nothing says how the first 1,000 are chosen. Today `register` enters `ValidatorQueue` only, and
promotion runs at [`validators_to_promote`](../yellowpaper.md#param-validators_to_promote) = 50 per
30-day rotation, so filling the set from the queue alone would take about 600 days. The earlier
allocation ("800 guardian + 200 auction", ECON-003 "top 800 testnet validators" at the legacy 305,505
bond) predates D-0421 (auction removed) and D-0440 (bond 1,200,000). Promotion is first-come at the
minimum stake (`queue.pop()`), the squatting case D-0437 flags. Placement is not an admission input
anywhere, although the seat-capture bound only holds when no fault domain exceeds about 10 % of the
pool (validator-set-diversity §3.3: the finite-pool profile
bounds a 10 % domain at ≤ 5.7e-8/yr and a 15 % domain at ≤ 1.3e-2/yr, while the assumption-free bound
— seats never exceed identities — protects only up to 33 identities, one fewer than the 34 seats
that break `3f < n` at n = 100).

**Decision.**

1. **Genesis seeds the active set directly.** The `flop-mainnet` (and `flop-testnet`) genesis preset
   places the ranked cohort into `ActiveValidators` up to
   [`validator_active_set_cap`](../yellowpaper.md#param-validator_active_set_cap), with session keys and
   bonded self-stake set at genesis and an empty queue. Queue-and-rotation admission (§15.5) applies only
   after genesis.
2. **Ranking key is earned work, not stake.** Testnet stake is faucet-issued, so the cohort is ranked by
   verification liveness on testnet — accepted verification duties per epoch (the `validatorEpochs`
   signal of the testnet earnings rules) — then uptime, then earlier registration as the tie-break.
   Stake enters only as the bond gate at genesis.
3. **Placement is an admission input, capped on count and on stake.** Each candidate signs a placement
   declaration (hosting ASN, metro, operating entity). Genesis composition rejects any cohort in which
   one hosting ASN, one metro, or one operating group exceeds **10 % of the seeded set by validator
   count or 10 % of seeded self-stake**, or in which one entity exceeds `MaxSlotsPerEntity`. Both legs
   are required because the committee draw is stake-weighted while the capture bound is modelled on a
   uniform draw over identities (E.42); capping count alone would admit a small, heavily staked domain
   that the model does not cover. A higher-ranked candidate that would breach a cap yields its seat to
   the next-ranked candidate outside that domain. Foundation-operated seats count against the same
   caps. The histogram the composition produced is published with the chain spec.
4. **Bond grant.** Each seeded validator receives the D-0440 bond (1,200,000 FLOP) through the ECON-003
   grant lane and is bonded at genesis; a candidate whose account is not funded by the snapshot loses
   the seat to the next-ranked candidate.
5. **Post-genesis promotion** is stake-ordered among floor-passing queue entries (D-0416) and fills only
   free slots under the cap (D-0437). First-come promotion at the minimum stake would be withdrawn.

**Ratified constraints (on ratification).** The ranking key, the two-part per-domain cap, and "genesis
seeds directly, rotation thereafter" become §15.2/§15.5 text with R-IDs; both cap legs are recorded in
`params/flop-protocol-params.yaml` as reference parameters until the composition tool enforces them.

**Precedent, and the argument against this decision.** No production chain seeds mainnet genesis seats
by testnet rank. Cosmos Game of Stakes ranked participants primarily on precommit uptime and jail
history, explicitly not on accumulated stake, but it awarded prizes; Hub genesis remained
stake-determined. NEAR's Stake Wars gated *entry* to a restricted mainnet whose foundation-run nodes
were progressively replaced. The softer shape — seed from foundation plus a qualified cohort, admit the
rest permissionlessly — carries less risk than a ranked allocation of 1,000 seats, because a published
ranking is a farming target. Two known failure modes argue the same way: Game of Stakes saw automated
metric-farming inflate the scoreboard, and NEAR's Stake Wars III kicked chunk-only producers on a
metric that measured the wrong role's duty. This decision keeps the ranked form because FLOP's genesis
grants the bond (D-0440) and therefore cannot be permissionless at genesis, but the ratifying reviewer
should weigh the softer alternative explicitly.

**Known weakness.** Ranking by earned work rewards Sybil farming across identities, and the bound that
should contain it — `MaxSlotsPerEntity` — is self-declared and, today, read nowhere in the pallet. The
placement declaration has the same problem: a declaration with no penalty attached is decoration.
Ratifying this decision **MUST** come with a misdeclaration penalty (ejection or slash) and enforcement
of the slot limit, or the caps are advisory.

**Numbers are gated.** The seeded-set size, the 10 % legs and the bond path **MUST NOT** be fixed by
this record. The launch plan seats 20–50 then at most 100 testnet validators, so a 1,000-seat
composition has no cohort to draw from yet, and the decision cannot execute above 200 seats until E.41
closes. The ranking key and the two-part cap are the ratifiable parts; the constants follow the cohort
under #1713.

**Alternatives rejected.** Stake-ranked genesis (ranks faucet balances); queue-only fill (600 days);
auction slots (removed by D-0421); a per-country rather than per-ASN cap (provider ≠ country; two
operators in one OVH site are one fault domain).

**Specification.** §15.2, §15.5, E.41. **Rationale.**
validator-set-diversity §3–§4,
testnet-to-mainnet-conversion.

## D-0508 — Rotation on underperformance: floor-based ejection and on-chain performance signal

**Status:** proposal, pending ratification. Wiring task #1711; models
`validator-rotation.qnt` (variant A = shipped
rule, variant B = this decision) and
`validator-score-provenance.qnt`.

**Context.** SPEC-021 as shipped: a composite score (40 % uptime, 30 % block rate, 20 % accuracy,
10 % latency) written by a **root-origin** `update_performance` oracle, 20 % decay per rotation, ranking
by (recent verified work, score), a **fixed** bottom-[`validators_to_eject`](../yellowpaper.md#param-validators_to_eject) = 50
ejected every 30 days, 7-day cooldown, `rejoin` with stake topped up. Three defects: the score is an
off-chain trust input; a fixed count evicts healthy validators when the whole set performs; and 50 per
30 days drains a failing cohort at 5 % of a 1,000 set per month. "Accuracy" and "latency" have no
operational definition.

**Decision.**

1. **Ejection is floor-based, and bounded.** A validator is eligible for ejection only if it fails the
   floor: no accepted verification duty within `WorkRecencyWindow` (R15.4c), **or** composite score below
   `performance_floor` (proposed 5,000 of 10,000). Rotation ejects floor-failing validators, lowest score
   first, up to a per-rotation cap of **10 % of the active set** (100 of 1,000) and promotes into the
   freed slots. A validator above the floor with recent verified work is never ejected by rotation.

   Three guards make a wrong floor survivable, and they matter more than the floor value itself:

   - **No evidence, no ejection.** A validator with fewer than a minimum number of scored samples in the
     window is not ejectable. This one predicate covers the bootstrap case (a fresh chain where every
     score is zero), the quiet-chain case (duty signals exist only when there is traffic, which is why
     R15.4c mandates protocol-issued challenges), and freshly promoted validators, which today enter with
     `performance_score = 0`.
   - **Minimum-set guard on every removal path.** Rotation, jail and kick **MUST NOT** reduce the active
     set below a minimum bound, and **MUST** skip rather than partially apply an ejection that would.
     Without it the shipped rule removes the entire set on any network of 50 or fewer validators
     (defect #1727): block production continues on stale authorities while the finality committee, which
     derives from the active set, empties and AlephBFT stops. That defect is open (#1727, E.56); the
     guard is the same predicate and may land ahead of the rest of this decision.
   - **The per-rotation cap is fixed, not tunable.** It is the blast-radius bound that makes a wrong floor
     or a mis-derived signal recoverable, so it does not become a governance knob.
2. **The score is derived on chain.** The four components are redefined as observable signals and the
   root-origin oracle is retired once they land: *uptime* = BABE slots authored / slots assigned over the
   interval (measurable for the whole active set); *participation* = seated AlephBFT sessions in which the
   validator contributed units before session close, reported by `pallet_aleph` (each validator on a
   1,000 pool is seated in about 10 % of sessions, roughly 290 samples per 30 days at 900-block sessions,
   about 72 at 3,600); *duty* = accepted verification duties plus known-answer challenges answered, over
   challenges issued (R15.4c); *lag* = median DAG-head distance while seated (the AlephBFT performance
   metric). The 40/30/20/10 weights are kept provisionally for these four in that order.

   **The lag component carries a grace band.** Scoring distance from the DAG frontier penalises honest but
   geographically distant validators, which is in direct tension with D-0507's placement caps: a metric
   that rewards proximity pushes operators into one metro. The lag component **MUST** therefore apply no
   penalty within a grace band sized to worst-case honest round-trip time, and score only beyond it. This
   is the shape Solana adopted for the same problem in SIMD-0033 (Timely Vote Credits), which sizes its
   grace from the roughly 100 ms maximum latency between two points on Earth and states the goal
   explicitly: a strict schedule "would encourage all validators to move to the same country". The band is
   measured in the wiring task, not guessed here.
3. **What ejection means** is unchanged: seat, rewards, and committee eligibility are lost for at least
   the [`ejection_cooldown_blocks`](../yellowpaper.md#param-ejection_cooldown_blocks) window; stake is
   thawed, not slashed. Liveness slashing (§15.7) is a separate path and may precede ejection.
4. **Score decay** (20 % per rotation) is retained so the floor reflects recent behaviour. Because
   decay runs before the floor test, a seat below `floor / 0.8` (6,250 at a 5,000 floor) re-enters the
   below-floor cohort at the next rotation; the floor is therefore evaluated on the pre-decay score of
   the rotation that ejects.
5. **Promoted validators keep their queued score.** The shipped `do_rotate` inserts promotions with
   `performance_score = 0`, which under a floor rule would make every fresh promotion eviction-eligible
   at the next rotation; promotion carries the candidate's queue score forward.
6. **Three declared constants are wired.** `EjectionCooldownBlocks`, `ScoreDecayPerRotation`, and
   `MaxSlotsPerEntity` are declared in the pallet `Config` and read nowhere; the Quint witnesses
   `witness_A_cooldown_bypass` (re-register in the same block), `witness_A_entity_overshoot` (six slots
   against a five-slot limit), and `witness_A_popped_candidate_discarded` (an under-staked popped
   candidate is dropped from both sets) show the consequences. All three become enforced predicates in
   the same change as the floor.

**Supersedes by scope.** D-0416's second leg ("eject below-floor, then rotate out the bottom-N by
stake of the remainder") would be withdrawn: ejection is floor-only, and stake remains the *promotion* key.
The Quint invariant `inv_no_healthy_eviction` (I6) is stated for that reading.

**Ratified constraints (on ratification).** R15.5 is reworded from "eject the bottom 50" to the floor
rule; `performance_floor` and the per-rotation cap enter the parameter table; the Quint invariants
I1–I8 of `validator-rotation.qnt` hold for the wired rule (I1, I2, I3, I5 are additionally
model-checked by Apalache to depth 4; the rest by simulation).

**What the models do and do not cover.** `validator-rotation.qnt` models the rotation **decision
rule** — the ejection predicate, promotion ordering, active-set cap, ejection cooldown and per-entity
slot limit — but deliberately does **not** model score provenance: in both variants
`performance_score` enters through the root `update_performance` oracle. The companion
`validator-score-provenance.qnt`
covers decision point 2: it accumulates observable BABE / Aleph / duty / DAG counters, derives the
40/30/20/10 composite at rotation, resets the measurement window, protects the zero-challenge duty
floor, and witnesses that an arbitrary oracle write breaks the derivation invariant. The artifacts
remain deliberately separate: the provenance model does not model ejection or promotion, and the
rotation model still treats its score input as given.

**Precedent.** Aleph Zero, the production deployment of this consensus family, already runs an absolute
floor with hysteresis on exactly these two signals: it suspends a validator after more than 48 sessions
below 80 % of expected blocks, or more than 24 sessions more than 11 units behind the DAG frontier, with
counters reset every 10 eras. The counter-with-reset shape is preferred to a single-rotation threshold,
for the same reason as the guards above: it survives a transient outage. The wider trend separates
ejection from slashing — Cosmos Hub has an open proposal to remove its downtime slash outright and
Osmosis runs without one. D-0409's 1 % jail and 5 % kick are not revisited here; the interaction is
flagged for the ratifying reviewer.

**Numbers are gated, principles are not.** The floor value, the sample minimum, the grace band and the
weights **MUST NOT** be fixed by this record: they need the score distribution of a real cohort, which no
network has produced. This decision ratifies the shape — floor rather than bottom-N, derived rather than
oracular, bounded blast radius, no ejection without evidence. The constants are set after one or two
rotations of testnet data, under #1711.

**Alternatives rejected.** Keep the fixed bottom-N (evicts healthy nodes, and at 50 per 30 days cannot
drain a bad cohort); keep the root oracle (a single key decides who is ejected); rank by stake for
ejection (the spec already forbids padding the liveness signal to buy rank, and stake is the *promotion*
key, not the *ejection* key); a floor relative to the set's median (re-opens the tournament dynamic
D-0416 removed, and mass-ejects when a network-wide incident moves the median).

**Specification.** §15.4, §15.5. **Rationale.**
validator-set-diversity §1, SPEC-021, D-0416, D-0437.

## D-0509 — Committee session cadence aligned to the BABE epoch

**Status:** proposal, pending ratification. Consensus safety zone: wiring task #1712 needs CTO
ratification and a devnet handover measurement before the constant flips.

**Context.** `AlephEpochBlocks` = 900 (~15 min) while the BABE epoch is 3,600 blocks. The committee
seed is BABE epoch randomness, fixed for the epoch and one epoch old (R15.4b), so the four sessions
inside one BABE epoch are predictable together: a 15-minute session does not shorten the
adaptive-corruption window below the epoch. The Lean model prices the cadence directly: on the finite
1,000 pool at 900 blocks the yearly union bound is 5.7e-8 at 10 % attacker concentration and 1.3e-2 at
15 %; at 3,600 blocks the 10 % bound is 1.4e-8 and the binomial 15 % bound falls from 6.44e-2 to 1.61e-2
(`CommitteeSampling.pool_p10_year_capture_le_5_7e_minus_8`, `pool_p10_hourly_refresh_capture_le_1_43e_minus_8`,
`p15HourlyRefreshRisk`). Shorter sessions buy faster dilution of a bad draw and more scoring samples; they
cost three extra committee handovers per epoch and four times the union-bound terms. Aleph Zero runs
900-block sessions with a ~14-seat finality committee; FLOP's 100 seats make handover the larger cost.

**Decision.**

1. `AlephEpochBlocks` = `EpochDurationInBlocks` (3,600 in production, 60 in fast/dev builds). Committee
   refresh coincides with the BABE epoch boundary; the domain-separated seed (R15.4b) is unchanged.
2. The live Lean profile becomes the 3,600-block draw count (`epochYearDraws 3600` = 8,760) and
   `scripts/check_lean_constants.py` continues to tie `AlephEpochBlocks` to it. The seed-correlation
   note on `draws_union_bound` already sizes `AdaptiveCorruptionBoundary.epochDuration` against the BABE
   epoch rather than `AlephEpochBlocks`.
3. Scoring under D-0508 is re-based on about 2.4 seatings per validator per day (about 72 per 30-day
   rotation on a 1,000 pool), which the decision records as adequate.
4. Before the constant flips: measure session-handover cost (keychain rebuild, DAG catch-up) at a
   100-seat committee on the devnet and record it in
   validator-set-diversity.

**Precedent.** Every documented beacon-driven committee design derives all committees of a period from
one seed fixed before that period begins: Ethereum computes an epoch's committees from a RANDAO mix two
epochs old (`MIN_SEED_LOOKAHEAD`, with `MAX_SEED_LOOKAHEAD` bounding activation grinding), and Cardano
fixes the epoch nonce and stake snapshot in the preceding epoch. FLOP's 900-block session inside a
3,600-block seed period is the inverse of that pattern and buys no unpredictability. We found no paper
naming this as an error directly, so the argument rests on the uniformity of the precedent rather than a
citation.

**Alternatives rejected.** Keep 900 (buys bad-draw dilution only; the seed already fixes committees for
the epoch); 28,800 (8 h; a validator is seated 0.3×/day, too few samples for rotation scoring, and a bad
draw lives eight hours); a per-session fresh seed (a different mechanism, E.42).

**Specification.** §2.2, §15.4, parameter table. **Rationale.** `CommitteeSampling.lean`
(`epochYearDraws_antitone`), validator-set-diversity §1.

## D-0510 — Bootstrap nodes and the public discovery plane

**Status:** proposal, pending ratification. Operational, not a runtime change; the launch checklist item
is P-11 in `docs/TESTNET_MAINNET_LAUNCH_PLAN.md` and the procedure is
`docs/setup/bootnodes.md`.

**Context.** On stock Substrate a bootnode is only a peer-discovery seed. On FLOP it is load-bearing:
`aleph_reserved_from_network()` (`pallets/relay-node/service/src/aleph_support.rs`) unions the CLI
`--reserved-nodes` with the chain spec's `bootNodes`, and that union is the reserved peer set of both
Aleph notification protocols on every node, retained across committee rotations, with 50 peer slots
above the committee cap held for archive/RPC/bootnodes (PR #743). A `bootNodes` entry is therefore a
permanent dial target for finality gossip, and a bootnode outage degrades the validator clique's seed,
not just fresh-node discovery. Today the only bootnode is `alice` inside the devnet compose, no public
FLOP bootnode, RPC, or telemetry host exists, `flop_simnet` and `flop_devnet` share `protocol_id =
"flop"` (a cross-network peering hazard), and P-01 ("testnet chain spec generated") has no bootnode
input although the spec must carry the multiaddrs.

**Decision.**

1. **Bootnodes are a launch requirement**, cut before the chain spec: testnet ≥ 2 on ≥ 2 hosting ASNs
   and ≥ 2 continents; mainnet ≥ 3 on ≥ 3 hosting ASNs and ≥ 2 continents, IPv4 and IPv6, behind DNS
   names with persisted node keys (`zkv-relay key generate-node-key --file`, `--node-key-file`,
   mode 0400).
2. **A bootnode is not a validator** and carries no session keys; it is a non-validating full node on a
   modest profile, never co-located with a validator, and it counts toward the same 10 % per-domain
   placement cap as validators ([D-0507](#d-0507--post-testnet-genesis-validator-selection-and-placement-admission)).
   Validator IPs are not published.
3. **Each network has a unique `protocol_id`** before any public bootnode exists.
4. **Retirement** keeps a retired bootnode reachable for one deprecation window after the chain spec or
   docs drop it, because running nodes keep dialing it as a reserved peer.
5. **A community bootnode list** with a submission process complements the foundation set; the public
   RPC and telemetry endpoints follow the same placement rule as the discovery plane.

**Known cost.** Chain-spec bootnode lists are a standing maintenance burden: Polkadot's RFC-0008 opens
by noting that every add or remove requires updating every chain spec, and its issue tracker carries
recurring wrong-address, peer-ID-mismatch and upgrade-ban failures. That is the argument for keeping the
foundation list small and stable, letting DHT discovery carry the rest, and treating the community list
as additive rather than load-bearing. It is also why retirement keeps a deprecation window (point 4):
running nodes hold these addresses as reserved peers.

**Alternatives rejected.** No bootnodes (DHT-only discovery; the Aleph reserved seed would be empty on
fresh nodes); bootnodes on validator hosts (publishes validator IPs and couples the discovery plane to
the committee's fault domains); a single provider (the Aleph Zero public plane is OVH-majority, which is
the pattern this decision avoids).

**Specification.** `docs/setup/bootnodes.md`, `docs/setup/network-launch-readiness.md`, P-11.
**Rationale.** validator-set-diversity §2 (public discovery
plane), `aleph_support.rs` reserved-peer seeding.

## D-0511 — One canonical compute-channel transcript and receipt definition

**Status:** draft for initial public release; specification clarification only. Runtime behavior and
wire bytes are unchanged.

**Context.** R12.1b and Appendix F.3 were both normative but described different transcript leaves.
R12.1b restated the legacy V0 field set under the undefined name `session_id`, while F.3 defines the
versioned leaves accepted by the runtime, including V3's decode-policy, token-ID, TOPLOC, and timing
bindings. The duplicate wording could lead a current client to sign a leaf that settlement rejects. The
receipt's `payable` field and the per-channel scope of `decode_policy_hash` were also implicit in runtime
state rather than defined by the specification.

**Decision.** Appendix F.3 is the sole compute-channel transcript-leaf and agent-receipt wire definition.
R12.1b and the informative walkthrough point to it instead of restating a preimage; `channel_id` is the
canonical identifier. `ChannelDecodePolicies[channel_id]` pins one policy at open for every turn,
including a multi-agent pipeline. Receipt `payable` is the gross receipt-bound settlement amount in
10⁻¹⁸ FLOP base units, before audit and forward adjustments: full reserved escrow on cooperative
settlement, the R12.1d tariff on unilateral settlement, or the stored forward-contract payment when that
override exists. The miner's net transfer is that amount less the audit earmark, and a forward channel
additionally refunds unearned availability to the buyer. The walkthrough's cooperative `open_channel`
signature matches Appendix G.1.

**Consequences.** Current clients obtain leaf versioning, TOPLOC binding, receipt fields, policy scope,
and payout derivation from one normative location. Existing leaf hashes, signatures, markers, balances,
and settlement behavior do not change.

**Alternatives.** Defining `session_id` as an alias would preserve two leaf definitions. Giving §12.1
precedence over Appendix F would make the legacy tuple authoritative and break current settlement.
Leaving `payable` to client inference would keep cooperative and unilateral receipt signatures
ambiguous under partial use.

**Supersedes by scope.** D-0505's Appendix F profile is preserved; this entry removes the conflicting
R12.1b restatement and makes its already-implemented per-channel policy and payout derivations explicit.

## D-0512 — Development-network exception to the paid-storage activation gate

**Status:** ratified by the owner on 2026-09-25, for development networks only. Scopes D-0506 item 6;

1. A development network (devnet, Era D in the launch plan) whose token carries no value may carry the

## D-0514 — Era-T conversion rules and a fail-closed miner adjustment

**Status:** ratified by the owner on 2026-09-29. No earlier decision set these rules. They were a
draft in ERA_T_TO_ERA_0 and a placeholder in E.38.

**Context.** §8 specified `work-vesting` only; the path that turns Era-T activity into Era-0 airdrop
grants had no normative section. D-0421, D-0438 and D-0440 fixed that genesis supply goes to
three airdrop cohorts and sized each pool. They did not say what earns an allocation, how the
snapshot is fixed, or what the miner performance adjustment does when its inputs are missing.
`pallet_airdrop_vesting` applies the plain linear curve when a miner has no mainnet score. That
makes missing evidence the most favourable outcome on a money path.

**Decision.**

1. Conversion distributes only the existing `genesis_supply`, with no incremental emission and no
   allocation funded twice. Each cohort draws only from its own airdrop pool, and cohorts do not
   cross-subsidize. `genesis_reserve` is not an Era-T airdrop pool.
2. A faucet grant, sponsored bond or transferred balance creates no allocation right by itself.
   Held Era-T balance and stake size are not scoring terms. Agents are scored on settled
   compute-channel spend and miners on PoUI-verified effective-FLOP served. Completed job count and
   active days are not scoring terms. A fraud-flagged account receives zero allocation, with no
   partial credit, unless the flag is overturned before the genesis specification is locked.
3. Conversion reads Era-T activity at a governance-ratified, finalized snapshot height. The
   per-account allocation and its hash are published before the genesis specification is locked;
   until then the snapshot may be revoked or corrected. The snapshot records each account's cohort,
   activity, fraud flag and cluster identity, and the published allocation binds the inputs used to
   filter and score it. The finalized Era-T chain state, the allocation file, the
   conversion-parameters file and their hashes are the evidence of record.
4. The miner performance adjustment fails closed. While a Miner grant lacks either performance
   input, or its testnet baseline is zero, the adjustment factor is taken as zero, so the grant
   unlocks nothing before its end block. The end-block rule is unchanged: every grant is fully vested
   there.

Score caps and aggregation, the accounting identity, the validator activity basis, appeals, the
agent release horizon and the remainder disposition stay open in E.38.

**Specification.** §8.2 R8.3–R8.5, R8.7; Appendix H.4.

**Rationale & sources.** Balance carries no scoring weight because the conversion rewards work, not
holdings (ECON-007).
Item 2 replaces the E.38 placeholder, which listed completed jobs and active days among the activity
inputs. The pallet change for item 4 is #1175 (BUILD step 4). The conversion allocator in
`ai-agent-simulator` still weights completed jobs and active days (#1174).

## D-0515 — Airdrop grant landing, claims and locked compute spend

**Status:** ratified by the owner on 2026-09-29. Extends [D-0514](#d-0514--era-t-conversion-rules-and-a-fail-closed-miner-adjustment)
to the two §8.2 requirements it left out; supersedes nothing.

**Context.** §8.2 made R8.6 (grant landing) and R8.8 (claims and locked spend) normative, but
D-0514 ratified only R8.3–R8.5 and R8.7. Both are money-path rules, and no earlier decision set
them: the landing's funding was open in #1175, and the locked-spend sinks were a draft in
LOCKED_COMPUTE_SPEND.

**Decision.**

1. Each Era-0 airdrop allocation is funded exactly once, as a genesis balance frozen by a
   `pallet_airdrop_vesting` grant whose tier matches the allocation's cohort. Creating the grant
   does not mint the allocation again. Which bridge writes the grants over the funded balances is
   left open.
2. `claim_vested` computes the unlocked amount at the finalized head, subtracts what was already
   claimed, and releases only a positive remainder from the freeze.
3. Still-locked principal may be spent only through `agent_transfer`, `open_channel`,
   `top_up_escrow` and `force_open`, for compute. That spend consumes vesting principal and never
   becomes freely transferable credit.

**Specification.** §8.2 R8.6, R8.8; Appendix H.4.

## D-0517 — Airdrop unlock: miner earn-to-unlock, validator halving release

**Status:** ratified by the owner on 2026-09-30, in review of #2092 and #2097. Supersedes D-0514 item 4 for
Miner grants and the end-block rule of R8.7 for Miner and Validator grants; narrows D-0515 item 3 to
Agent grants.

**Context.** D-0514 made Miner and Validator airdrop grants vest linearly over
`airdrop_vesting_duration_blocks`, with a performance adjustment for miners. The team's published
terms were different, and the owner directed the protocol to follow them. D-0515 item 3 let still-locked
principal of any tier be spent on compute. A compute channel pays `ch.miner` by free transfer and has
no agent-differs-from-miner guard, so a miner could spend its own locked principal on a channel that
pays itself and recover about 0.99 FLOP of free balance per FLOP spent, under either schedule.

**Decision.**

1. A Miner grant unlocks `⌊P/4⌋` of its principal `P` at its start block. The rest unlocks only
   against unlock credit: one FLOP per FLOP the grantee receives as the channel's miner in the payout
   of a session settled by `settle`, `certificate_settle` or `force_settle → finalize`, counted once
   the settlement is finalized and the channel dispute window has passed with no upheld fraud dispute.
   Block rewards, forward-availability payments, refunds, penalties and dispute bonds accrue no
   credit. The grant
   has no end block; principal not yet unlocked stays frozen. The D-0514 performance adjustment no
   longer applies.
2. A Validator grant stays frozen until block 63,072,000, the first block of halving era 1. From
   there, Validator grants are released one per 86,400 blocks, each releasing its remaining principal in full, until all are
   released. No Validator grant releases until the release order is fixed (E.38). Release lifts only
   the vesting freeze: it never unbonds stake, and an active validator keeps `validator_min_stake`
   bonded.
3. Only Agent grants may spend still-locked principal, and only through the four compute calls of
   D-0515. A miner may bond its still-locked principal as its own self-stake; it stays frozen while
   bonded and after unbonding until it unlocks. Otherwise still-locked Miner and Validator principal
   cannot be spent, transferred or used for compute; slashing of bonded stake applies to it. A slash of
   a grantee's bonded stake is charged first to still-frozen grant principal (added to `S`, the grant's
   slashed total) and removed from the grant's freeze atomically, then to other balance.
4. The Agent grant is unchanged: its horizon stays open in E.38, and a linear schedule would use
   `airdrop_vesting_duration_blocks`.

This settles the miner vesting divergence of #1355 and the validator unlock conflict of #1359, which
D-0435 marked "do not build as specified": a stake could unlock while still securing the set.

**Recorded residual.** Once its quarter is liquid, a miner can buy inference from itself through an
agent it controls and unlock the rest at about the 1% audit earmark, roughly 0.75% of principal. No
runtime common-control rule binds this (E.38, E.49).

**Open.** The Validator release order and the disposition of Miner principal that never earns its
unlock stay in E.38. Whether a fraud
verdict upheld after the dispute window reverses unlock credit follows E.44.

**Specification.** §8.2 R8.7, R8.8; Appendix E.38, H.4; the `airdrop_vesting_duration_blocks`
description.

**Rationale & sources.** Block rewards are excluded because they would turn the unlock back into a
clock: 72 FLOP per block is about 6.22M FLOP a day to the miner pool, enough to unlock the 900M miner
tranche in about 145 days whatever the demand, and rewards also pay the synthetic-task floor. The
Validator window opens on the §9.2 Day-730 revenue cliff, and releasing one grant per day staggers
that exit. The constants stay inline in R8.7 until #1175 builds the schedule, since a params entry
needs a consuming implementation.

## D-0519 — Service-lane close boundary, claim window and provider-only redemption

**Status:** ratified by the owner on 2026-10-04 as A + M2. The owner replied "Continue on both" in
the session after the separate requests to approve these lane-close rules and authorize the required
Codex consultation. This records the session instruction, not a GitHub review signature. Paid-storage
lanes remain restricted to development networks under D-0512 until E.54 is ratified and implemented;
the numerical claim window remains UNRATIFIED (E.54). This documentation changes no runtime behavior;
the implementation is #1836.

**Context.** R5.4g settles retrieval with cumulative payer-signed `ServiceVoucher`s. Before this
decision, G.4 said `close_service_lane` stopped new authorization and let anyone holding a valid
voucher redeem it. F.7 gave each `ServiceLane` a close boundary and a claim deadline. E.54 listed
lane-close boundaries among the items it must ratify, without defining either boundary. Two gaps
blocked implementation:

1. *A signature carries no time.* Every value a signer can put in a voucher before close (a block
   hash, an epoch, a sequence number) is still available afterwards, so a voucher signed after
   close can be indistinguishable from one signed before it. A signature is provably older than a
   block only if the chain recorded it, or a commitment to it, before that block. Close therefore
   cannot be enforced against later signatures by any check on voucher contents, and a validity
   field in the voucher would not help, because the signer chooses its value. R5.4j already bounds
   the payer's exposure by the signed cap until the claim deadline, but refers to "the funded lane's
   close and claim rules" without defining them.
2. *Redemption order.* R5.4g rejects stale counters, and G.4 lets anyone submit, so whoever lands a
   voucher first picks the order. Suppose a provider holds an unredeemed voucher for a large read at
   sequence 7. The payer can sign sequence 8 with one more write and no read bytes, then submit it
   itself. That voucher is valid and pays one setup fee, after which the provider's voucher is stale
   and never redeems. For about one setup fee, the payer voids earned retrieval of up to an epoch.

**Decision.**

1. **Close boundary.** A lane's close boundary is the block that includes the payer's
   `close_service_lane`, or the reservation's end if that comes first. Once set it never moves:
   neither a repeated close, a revocation, nor a provider rotation changes it. Closing stops new
   funding. The lane keeps its reservation-provider slot and its escrow until it is finalized.
2. **Claim window.** The claim deadline is the close boundary plus `W` blocks. `W` is the profile's
   lane claim window: an E.54 limit, nonzero and at least one paid-storage epoch, fixed when the lane
   is funded. E.54 constrains permitted activation, challenge and funded-request timing so that all
   setup entitlements become redeemable, and all funded-request deadlines affecting lane claims
   expire, by the fixed deadline, including sequential delays and boundary rules. A profile or
   admission allowing an obligation beyond it is rejected; a missing bound is never zero.
   Qualification and admission must establish an actual runtime dispatch opportunity after all
   entitlement prerequisites complete while known finalized height remains within the unchanged
   deadline. Maturity-time arithmetic alone does not prove this under arbitrary finality jumps;
   affected work stays disabled, including on development networks, until qualified. This is a
   redemption-validity requirement, not a guarantee of transaction inclusion.
   The deadline is read on the finalized prefix, as §1.2 requires for economic deadlines.
   A closing lane accepts redemption while the finalized height is at or below the deadline, and
   finalizes only once it is above. When no finalized height is known, neither runs. A finality
   stall therefore freezes the window (§13 F1); catch-up can cross the deadline at once, so `W` does
   not promise that many blocks after recovery. Finalization refunds exactly the lane's remaining
   escrow. Reservation termination never releases funds still allocated to a lane.
3. **What close does not do.** Settlement accepts every otherwise-valid voucher up to the deadline,
   whether it was signed before or after the close boundary. After close, the payer's exposure is the
   lane's unspent budget until the deadline, and the payee stays fixed, so only the lane's provider
   can gain from a post-close signature. Close is never presented as proof of when a voucher was
   signed. Once the close is finalized, a conforming payer or delegate stops signing for the lane,
   and a conforming provider stops admitting new requests on it.
4. **Redemption.** The lane's provider dispatches `redeem_service`, directly or through an
   authorized proxy. A voucher redeems only if all of these hold:
   - its sequence exceeds the last redeemed sequence;
   - each cumulative counter is at least its last redeemed value;
   - its amount equals the R5.4g formula at the lane's pinned tariff;
   - its amount is above the amount already paid and within the budget.

   Redemption pays exactly the difference to the lane's provider. It does not require that provider
   to still be assigned. A provider serves only against a voucher that dominates the last one it
   accepted.
5. **Lane identity.** The chain assigns lane identifiers at funding from a per-payer nonce. A nonce
   is never reused, even after its lane is finalized or pruned, so a voucher for one lane never
   redeems against another.
6. **Wire format.** The `ServiceVoucher` encoding and domain pinned for the development profile do
   not change.

**Where the wording lands.** R5.4g takes the redemption rule of item 4. R5.4j takes items 1
to 3, in place of its reference to undefined close and claim rules. The F.7 `ServiceLane` record adds
the claim window, and its acceptance constraints add the fixed boundary, the finalized-prefix
deadline, non-reused identifiers and assignment-independent claims. In G.4, `redeem_service` names
the lane's provider (or its proxy) as origin and adds the deadline rule, and
`close_service_lane`/`finalize_service_lane` state items 1 and 2. E.54 adds the lane claim window to
the windows it ratifies, together with the residual post-close exposure of item 3.

**Alternatives rejected.**

- *Checkpoint-anchored close.* After the boundary, a voucher redeems only with an inclusion proof,
  under a provider checkpoint posted by the boundary, of a commitment that binds the signed voucher.
  This is the only on-chain proof that a voucher predates close. It needs checkpoint posting and a
  pinned commitment format first, and it does not lower the amount at risk. A later profile version
  may require it for lanes funded under that profile, since every lane pins its profile.
- *A validity field in the voucher.* The signer chooses it, so it proves nothing about close, and it
  would re-pin the development encoding in three languages.
- *Amount-ordered redemption by any submitter.* This defeats the payer's sequence attack, but it drops
  R5.4g's stale-counter rejection. That rejection could not be restored later without voiding
  vouchers providers already hold.

**Supersedes by scope.** No earlier decision. Narrows the G.4 `redeem_service` origin from any
holder of a valid voucher to the lane's provider. D-0506 item 3 is unchanged.

**Specification.** §5.4 R5.4g, R5.4j; Appendices E.54, F.7, G.4.

**Rationale & sources.** This is the design that commits to least. It keeps every check the chain
can enforce (no refund before the deadline, no claim after it, the budget cap, strict voucher
order), adds no wire change and no new dependency, and leaves the stronger checkpoint-anchored close
open as a later profile version. The finalized-prefix clock follows §1.2 and §13 F1. Design record,
second opinions and the implementation handoff are on #1836. Background:
economics, mechanism and security review.

## D-0522 — Agent airdrop spend-to-unlock

**Status:** ratified by the owner on 2026-10-05. The owner's instruction in the session was: fix
yellowpaper for "Agent 3:1 spend-to-unlock is ahead of the spec. The WP and the workbook both state it
as settled, but the yellowpaper still lists the agent unlock schedule as open (E.38)." This records
that session instruction, not a GitHub review signature. The spend cap (item 2) and the payout and
refund attribution (item 3) are this decision's specification of "3:1" and await the owner's
confirmation in review. Supersedes D-0517 item 4. This documentation changes no runtime behavior; the
implementation is #1175 and #1354.

**Context.** D-0517 left the Agent grant's horizon open in E.38, with a linear schedule over
`airdrop_vesting_duration_blocks` as the fallback. The tokenomics workbook (Airdrop Schedule §5) and
the draft public whitepaper both publish 3:1 spend-to-unlock with no time vesting: every three locked
FLOP spent on compute unlock one, at most three quarters of a grant is spent and one quarter becomes
liquid, and principal never spent stays locked. ECON-009 gap G1 objected that 3:1 needs 900,000,000
FLOP of locked spend against 823,500,000 of projected demand over years 1–3. That objection binds
only a fixed horizon; with no end block, a demand shortfall slows the unlock instead of breaking a
schedule.

LOCKED_COMPUTE_SPEND returns compute-channel refunds of
locked-funded escrow as ordinary free balance. R8.8 already forbids that ("MUST NOT turn it into
freely transferable credit"), and under spend-to-unlock it would also let an agent open a channel from
locked principal, let it lapse, and recover the whole amount liquid with no compute bought.

**Decision.**

1. An Agent grant unlocks nothing at its start block and has no end block. It unlocks only against
   spend credit: one FLOP of principal for each three FLOP of its locked part in the `payable` of a
   session settled by `settle`, `certificate_settle` or `force_settle → finalize` (the gross
   settlement amount before the audit earmark, Appendix F.3), counted once the settlement is
   finalized, the channel dispute window has passed, and every fraud dispute raised in that window
   has resolved with none upheld. Locked principal spent through `agent_transfer`, refunds, the
   R12.1d penalty and any other leg outside the `payable` earn no credit. The unlocked amount is
   `⌊K/3⌋`, where `K` is the total locked part of credited `payable`s.
2. The grant's locked compute spend `C`, net of refunds returned to it, never exceeds `⌊3P/4⌋`, where
   `P` is the principal at creation. Credit lags spend by finality and the dispute window; without the
   cap an agent that spends its whole locked balance first would consume the quarter its credit
   unlocks.
3. Escrow a channel draws from locked principal, at opening or by top-up, is its locked part. At
   close the escrow is allocated once, in order: the `payable` draws on the locked part first; a
   refund then draws on what remains of it first and returns that share to the grant as frozen
   principal, reducing `C`; every other leg draws on the remainder. A single ordered allocation keeps
   each unit of principal counted once. This enforces R8.8's existing rule, and makes the R12.1d
   penalty fall on the agent's free funds before its locked principal.
4. Principal neither spent nor unlocked stays frozen; its disposition joins the Miner's in E.38.
5. `airdrop_vesting_duration_blocks` no longer governs any ratified grant tier. It remains the
   airdrop-vesting pallet's interim schedule until R8.7 is implemented.

**Worked example.** Across the whole agent pool, `P` = 1,200,000,000: `C` ≤ 900,000,000 and the
unlocked total ≤ 300,000,000, the workbook's end state (Airdrop Schedule B90–B91). For one grant of
1,200 FLOP: 900 FLOP of locked spend in settled `payable`s unlocks 300 and leaves nothing frozen.
Credit counts the agent's spend, not the miner's net receipt, so the 1% audit earmark taken out of
the `payable` does not shave the unlock; counting the miner's 891 instead would unlock 297 and leave
3 frozen. If 90 of those 900 went through `agent_transfer` instead, `K` = 810, 270 unlocks, `C` sits at
the cap, and 30 stays frozen under item 4.

**Recorded residual.** An agent that controls the miner it pays recovers about 99% of its locked
spend as that miner's payout, which the D-0515 compute sinks already allowed, and now also unlocks a
third of it. No runtime common-control rule binds this (E.38, E.49).

**Open.** The disposition of principal that never unlocks stays in E.38, and whether a fraud verdict
upheld after the dispute window reverses spend credit follows E.44. Whether locked Agent principal may
be staked (the workbook says yes; R8.8 says no) is not changed here.

**Specification.** §8.2 R8.7, R8.8; Appendix E.38, H.4; the `airdrop_vesting_duration_blocks` and
`genesis_agent_airdrop` descriptions.

**Rationale & sources.** The ratio and the cap stay inline in R8.7 until #1175 builds the schedule,
as D-0517 did, since a params entry needs a consuming implementation. `agent_transfer` earns no credit
because it has no settlement record or dispute window to count against. Credit is taken on the
gross `payable` because the published rule is "every 3 $FLOP spent on inference releases 1": the
agent's spend, of which the audit earmark is part (raised in review of #2381). Counting a payout only once
every timely dispute has resolved, rather than once the window passes with none upheld, keeps a
challenge still pending at the window's end from releasing credit early; a second opinion through
`just consult` (Codex) asked for this and for the single ordered allocation of item 3. Workbook: FLOP Tokenomics &
Airdrop.xlsx, rev 2026-10-04, Airdrop Schedule §5 (B80 `n` = 3; B89–B91).

## D-0524 — Slot and tranche position rent

**Status:** ratified by the protocol owner on 2026-10-07 in
[task #1965](https://github.com/flop-labs/flop-core/issues/1965). The production activation
profile and numerical tariff, bond and audit parameters remain pending E.54.

**Supersedes by scope.** D-0506 item 2's rounding once per provider, and R5.4f's independent
provider cumulative ceilings and replacement entitlement within residual segment budgets.
The escrow ceiling, signed payer cap, checked arithmetic and separate setup/lane holds remain.

**Decision.** One rent accumulator per slot and funding tranche, indexed by position in the term.
A slot holds `c` encoded bytes. Tranche `k` funds positions `[S_k,S_k+L_k)` at `p_k/q_k`, with
`q_k > 0`, for `B_k = ceil(c·L_k·p_k/q_k)`. Its accumulator is
`R_k(τ) = ceil(c·(τ−S_k)·p_k/q_k)`. Accepted interval `[a,b)` earns its historical holder
`R_k(b)−R_k(a)`; split an interval at every tranche boundary. Never restart the accumulator
at replacement, epoch boundaries or repeat assignment. Never index it by accepted-block count.

A replaced provider keeps earned/unreleased rent for its own positions, pending the existing
challenge/forfeiture rule. A replacement receives only its own positions. Failed, unserved and
forfeited deltas remain in escrow and refund to the payer; they cannot be reassigned. Close settles
surviving historical entitlements before refunding `Σ_k B_k − paid − held` per slot; live holds
cannot be refunded. Each provider bears its positions' rounding discrepancy: within one tranche,
an interval can earn above or below its exact share by strictly less than one base unit. The payer
funds each tranche's ceiling and receives all unearned deltas at close. Fully accepted service
releases every funded ceiling unit and refunds zero. No provider has an independent ceiling guarantee.

**Rationale.** Disjoint position deltas telescope within each funded tranche, conserving the cap
regardless of release order. Splitting epochs, rotating identities and assigning the same provider
again cannot change payment for the same served positions. New positions use their own funding
tranche's tariff. For `c=1,T=2,p/q=1/2`, A earns 1 and B earns 0, rather than the impossible
independent entitlements 1+1 against funding 1. Residual clipping would make earnings depend on
release order and underpay F in the multi-replacement vector.

**Evidence and handoff.** Seven state/balance vectors
and `paid-storage-counterexamples.qnt` witnesses supply #1837's payout and #1907's money-path corpus,
and #1570/#1571's proof/model coverage. `PaidStorageArithmetic.slot_counter_releases_le_term`
proves the term bound; `slot_interval_fairness` proves the strict one-unit interval error.
`PaidStorageSettlement.lean` already indexes intervals by position. These are specification/model
contracts, not runtime conformance receipts. #1837 must consume this decision and the regression
vectors before its payout implementation is ready. This task changes no pallet code or launch default.

## D-0526 — Active-set cap and BABE authority alignment (1,000 active validators, 100 finalizers)

**Status:** ratified 2026-10-15 (closes #1393 / YP E.41).

**Context.** D-0437 (#1400) ratified `validator_active_set_cap = 1,000` in the yellowpaper and protocol

`MAX_ACTIVE_VALIDATORS` and `MaxAuthorities` were set to 200, while `ValidatorSessionManager::new_session`

**Decision.**
1. **Option (a) ratified:** Raise `MAX_ACTIVE_VALIDATORS` and `MaxAuthorities` to 1,000. Every active
   validator is a BABE block authoring authority. Because BABE slot evaluation evaluates a private VRF
   locally per slot with zero gossip round overhead, 1,000 authorities incur no interactive consensus
   round messaging.
2. **AlephBFT finality decoupled:** AlephBFT finality remains strictly bounded to the 100-member PoUI-gated

## D-0527 — Miner airdrop unlock credits miner block rewards

**Status:** ratified by the owner. The ruling is recorded in the body of #2625 (2026-10-12) and in
§1 item 2 of outstanding-decisions, not in a review signature.
Supersedes D-0517 item 1's exclusion of block
rewards and the block-reward half of D-0517's rationale. The rest of D-0517 stands: a quarter at the
start block, no end block, the payout credit and its dispute-window rule, locked Miner principal usable
only as self-stake, slash attribution, and the Validator release. This documentation changes no
runtime behavior; the implementation is #1175.

**Context.** D-0517 item 1 credited a Miner grant only for the payouts its grantee receives as a
channel's miner. The terms the team gives counterparties count block rewards as well, and the
projection model's estimate of about four months to unlock at constant capacity holds only if they
count. The public airdrop page, generated from D-0517, gave a counterparty the opposite answer.

**Decision.**

1. A Miner grant's unlock credit accrues one FLOP per FLOP of either receipt:
   1. the payout the grantee receives as the channel's miner, as D-0517 item 1 defines it and counted
      as it says: once the settlement is finalized and the channel dispute window has passed with no
      upheld fraud dispute;
   2. the grantee's share of the miner leg of the block reward (`miner_share_ppt`, 75% of each block
      reward) as actually minted to the grantee account in a block-reward distribution, counted once
      the distribution block is finalized.
2. No other receipt counts: the validator, agent and staker legs of the block reward, the
   Labs/Foundation subsidy, transaction fees and tips, forward-availability payments, refunds,
   penalties and dispute bonds. Each receipt counts once.
3. The unlocked amount stays `min(P − S, ⌊P/4⌋ + credit)` with no end block. Whether a fraud verdict
   upheld later reverses credit of either kind follows E.44.

**Specification.** §8.2 R8.7.

**Rationale & sources.** D-0517 gave two reasons to exclude block rewards. Both still describe the
mechanism: the owner accepts the first, and the second is bounded by what the miner leg pays for.

- *A clock.* The era-0 miner leg is 72 FLOP per block, about 6.22M FLOP a day. If the airdropped
  miners earned all of it, block rewards alone would unlock the 900M locked tranche in about 145 days
  whatever the demand; at the miner calculator's launch share (1,000 of 1,200 H100-equivalents) they
  take about 174 days. The published "about four months" is this constant-capacity case with payouts
  added. In the calculator's base case the network grows past the airdropped cohort, its share falls,
  and the whole tranche unlocks by month 8. It is not a calendar clock: a grantee that stops doing
  verified work stops unlocking, and its share of the leg falls as other miners' verified `G_n` grows.
- *The synthetic-task floor.* `pallet_block_rewards` splits the miner leg pro rata to each miner's
  `G_n` for the distribution period (`PeriodGn`), and its only writer is `pallet_flop_poui`'s
  `OnProofVerified`: quorum-attested direct-rail proofs (synthetic and ghost, calibration) under R3.6b.
  The simulator path that skips the quorum is compiled out of production runtimes. Block-reward credit
  is therefore credit for verified capacity, including the synthetic-task bootstrap floor (§9.2), and
  never for registered but idle hardware. Limiting the credit to that leg is the filter; no separate
  one is needed. Minted rewards are liquid (R9.13) and nothing claws back credited `G_n` or a minted
  reward, so credit counts at finality with no dispute-window lag. A future direct-rail clawback would
  need a matching lag.
- *Self-dealing.* Current-profile channel payout issues no public work credit and does not feed
  `BlockRewards` (R11.2b), so wash demand through an agent the miner controls buys no block-reward
  credit. It still buys payout credit, at the 15% validator-leg cost E.38 records (D-0525). If a later
  profile lets channel payout earn public work credit (E.44), wash demand would also raise a grantee's
  share of the miner leg; that case belongs to E.49. A validator leader grading ghost tasks for a
  miner it controls is threat V8 and is unchanged.

The validator leg stays excluded because a Validator grant has its own release rule. Transaction fees
stay excluded under either realization of R9.6: the interim one that pays the block author, and the
ratified target that attributes the miner share by `G_n`.

## D-0528 — Sponsor-owned registration bonds count as self-stake on devnet and testnet

**Status:** ratified by the owner on 2026-10-17 for devnet and testnet, in the brief for #2676. It settles
#683 for those networks only; mainnet treatment stays open in E.32. #1361 (the capacity formula) also
stays open, so grants are sized from the live on-chain requirement, never from a constant. SPEC-024
positions keep their mainnet meaning.

**Context.** Outside GPU miners began onboarding on devnet-ts on 2026-10-17. Each one needed the core team
to transfer about 0.6–1M FLOP (a miner) or the 1.2M FLOP validator minimum (D-0440). A plain transfer is
spendable: an agent could pose as a miner or validator and spend the grant on inference. SPEC-024 sponsor
positions already kept principal in the sponsor account, but only as backing: they never satisfied the
miner capacity bond or the validator floor, and they required an operator that had already registered
with its own funds.

**Decision.**

1. On devnet and testnet runtimes, active sponsored principal counts as the operator's self-stake:
   toward the miner capacity bond (`required_self_stake_for`, `is_capacity_bonded`, the hw-calibration
   bond check and the audit-forfeit base) and toward the validator effective minimum (queue promotion and
   `rejoin`). On mainnet, sponsored stake stays backing and never satisfies self-stake. A build feature
   (`sponsored-self-stake`, implied by the devnet `volta` build and by the `testnet` build) selects the
   rule; on its own it is a compile error, and CI checks that the mainnet node build reports it off.
   Governance cannot switch it.
2. The bond is sponsor-owned and usable only as stake. It stays frozen in the sponsor account, so the
   operator can never transfer it, pay fees with it, place it on hold or escrow, or back other stake with
   it. It is slashable while active or unbonding, and it is refunded only to the sponsor, after the role's
   unbonding window has elapsed on the finalized prefix. The slash-proceeds account may not post
   principal that counts as self-stake, because slashing it would pay that account back. While it still
   holds a live position from before the rule was enabled, no sponsored principal is credited at all
   (fail closed) until that position is withdrawn. The bond stays at risk for slashing and exit.
   Sponsor principal is the sponsor account's only staking freeze: an account with own stake, a
   delegation or a freeze in the other staking pallet cannot sponsor, and a sponsor account cannot
   self-stake or delegate in either pallet. When a running chain first enables the rule, positions
   whose sponsor already holds another staking freeze are quarantined, and nothing is credited until
   that scan finishes (it runs in bounded per-block batches) or while any quarantined position
   remains live (fail closed).
3. A sponsor may register an operator that holds no balance (`sponsor_register_miner`,
   `sponsor_register_validator`), posting the whole bond. The bond must cover the live requirement:
   `required_self_stake_for(miner)` for a miner, `effective_minimum_stake()` for a validator.
   `sponsor_top_up` grows an existing position when the requirement rises.
4. On devnet and testnet an operator's deregistration starts the unbonding of its active sponsor
   positions, and the account cannot register again until they are withdrawn: a slash of the new
   registration must never reach sponsors of the old one. The operator may withdraw them after the
   finalized unlock, and may fully unbond a position that outlived its registration, so an absent
   sponsor cannot lock it out. Because a sponsor may unbond while a validator is jailed, `un_jail`
   first restores the bonded self-stake to the effective minimum from the validator's own spare
   balance, as `rejoin` does. A sponsor can release its own position with `sponsor_unbond` and then
   `sponsor_withdraw`. If that takes an active validator below the effective minimum, the validator
   goes back to the queue, and the unbond waits while that would cross the minimum active set; an
   author never keeps its seat without collateral.
5. The validator self-stake ratio for native delegation counts operator-owned stake only, so a fully
   sponsored validator accepts no native delegation.
6. Chain state and events report own stake (`self_stake`, zero when fully sponsored) separately from
   sponsored stake. A sponsored bond creates no allocation right (R8.4).

**Specification.** §6.1 R6.1a; §2.3; §15.2; Appendices E.32, G.3 and H.1.

**Rationale & sources.**

- *Ownership instead of a lock on the operator.* Freezes and fungible holds overlap in FRAME, so frozen
  funds in the operator's account could still be consumed by inference escrow. Keeping the principal in
  the sponsor account closes every operator path at once and reuses the SPEC-024 slash and refund
  machinery unchanged.
- *One staking freeze per sponsor.* An account's frozen amount is its largest freeze, not their sum. A
  sponsor backing a miner and a validator with the same funds would pass any admission check that sums
  freezes, and could then transfer the smaller bond away (#2770 review). Exclusivity closes that without
  a storage change, a shared freeze id or a move to holds.
- *All sponsored stake counts.* A separate position kind for registration bonds would change a stored type
  and need a migration, with no devnet or testnet behavior that depends on the distinction.
- *Mainnet authority unchanged.* Item 4 applies only where the rule is enabled, so on mainnet only the
  sponsor can start a refund (E.32).
- *A dedicated build feature.* Tying the rule to `volta` alone would make a testnet build enable
  simulator-mode crediting to get it.
- *Residual risks, recorded in E.32 and H.1.*
  - A sponsor's own fungible hold over its frozen principal would make a later slash transfer fail. The
    slash then fails atomically, and so does the dispatch that needs it: a channel `finalize` that
    forfeits audit stake cannot complete until the hold is released. The same gap applies to self-stake
    and delegator freezes, so the sponsor service uses dedicated accounts that do nothing else.
  - The operator still needs a small, spendable balance for the existential deposit, fees and session
    keys.
  - A sponsor account holds at most 64 live positions.
  - Rotation or slash ejection leaves positions active, and the sponsor unbonds them.
  - A validator offence reported after deregistration cannot reach its unbonding positions, as it cannot
    reach a validator's own stake today.

**Alternatives rejected.**

- A plain transfer: the operator could spend it.
- Transfer plus a freeze in the operator account: holds and fees could consume it.
- A position-kind marker: a storage migration for no behavioral gain.
- Moving all sponsor principal into a pallet escrow account: it changes the balance model of every
  existing position.

## D-0532 — Miner capacity bond: one unit, window inside the coefficient, value set against a security envelope

**Status:** proposal pending ratification (#1361). Amends D-0418; does not supersede its curve shape.
Nothing changes in the runtime, `params/` or published surfaces until the owner ratifies it and the
activation gates below are met.

**Context.** #1361 found three miner capacity-stake numbers in circulation. They reduce to one formula
and one implementation error (analysis):

- D-0418 and §6.1 require `10,000 + k·B_p`, with `k = 0.01` FLOP per G_n/s of calibrated cap. The
  calibration behind `k` already integrates the 604,800-block dispute window.
- The workbook's 19,890 FLOP per H100-eq unit is that formula with H100 SXM peak (989,000) standing
  in for calibrated `B_p`.
- The runtime (#643, specified in #633 as a settlement-exposure bound) computes
  `10,000 + 0.01·B_p·604,800·1.25`. It uses D-0418's per-G_n/s coefficient as a per-G_n rate, which
  counts the window twice and adds surge: 7,560 FLOP per G_n/s, 756,000× the ratified requirement.
  An 8× H200 host at 40% of peak would need 23.9B FLOP. That is more than the 18.47B FLOP issued by
  the end of era 6 (genesis 4.40B, block emission 12.11B, developer subsidy 1.96B), so real-hardware
  calibration (#2850) cannot be bonded.

The bound was re-derived with today's parameters and the 2026-10 consult's corrections: rewards
divide by credited work, rewards are liquid, and settlement exposure is part of the gain. For emission
fraud the floor is `k_min = P·W/(c·T)`, with `P·W` = 43.5M FLOP of miner pot per window in era 0,
`c` the effective catch probability and `T` the network credited throughput. The gain never exceeds
`P·W`, so the uncapped linear curve over-deters at the top, and D-0418's no-cap invariant stands.

**Decision (proposed):**

1. **One unit.** The capacity coefficient is FLOP per G_n/s of **calibrated** cap `B_p`. The exposure
   window is inside the coefficient (`k` is sized against gain over the window). The requirement
   **MUST NOT** multiply by the revocation or dispute window again.
2. **Surge is a separate margin.** `σ = miner_stake_surge_multiplier_ppm / 10⁶ ≥ 1` multiplies only the
   capacity term:
   `required_self_stake(B_p) = min_miner_self_stake + σ·k·B_p`.
   Whether `σ` applies to every cap or only to provisional/SOFT caps stays with E.33. Until then it
   applies to every cap, as the runtime does now.
3. **The value of `k` is set at activation, not here.** A later owner decision sets `k`, citing
   evidence that it satisfies the security envelope (item 4). The candidate to validate first is
   `k = 0.25` FLOP per G_n/s (`VFY/4`) with `σ = 1.25`. At 40% of peak that is: 1× RTX 4090 30,625;
   1× H100 SXM 133,625; 8× H200 999,000; 8× B200 2,260,000 FLOP. Analytically it deters liquid,
   all-fake emission fraud once credited throughput exceeds about 1.9e8 G_n/s at `c = 0.9`
   (7.0e8 at `c = 0.25`). That is a sensitivity result, not a demonstrated bound: `c` is unmeasured,
   and enforced detection is dispute-only today.
4. **Security envelope and activation gates.** The runtime keeps today's requirement until a value of
   `k` is shown to satisfy `c_lower · s · S_available ≥ G_max` throughout operation, where:
   - `c_lower` is a conservative bound on detection, adjudication **and collection**, not a sampling
     rate;
   - `G_max` is total retained emission **and settlement** gain, including concurrent and unresolved
     exposure, with no collateral counted twice;
   - the time until fraudulent crediting stops is bounded, and collateral is retained through
     dispute resolution;
   - enforcement continues after activation: stale measurements, falling credited throughput or failed
     detection liveness block new uncovered exposure, including during bootstrap.

   The evidence must also include:
   - real-inference calibration (#2850), active or activating in the same upgrade;
   - aggregate cap enforcement across a miner's concurrent channels;
   - measured detection liveness;
   - a reproducible adversarial analysis with runtime tests covering bootstrap, low utilization,
     coalitions and capacity exits, with uncertainty margins, where leaving the validated envelope
     fails closed.

   Activation is a separate owner decision, recorded in Appendix H.
5. **Alignment, after ratification and activation:**
   - runtime: drop `RevocationLatencyBlocks` from `required_self_stake` and set `CapacityStakePerGflop`;
   - `params/` (`miner_capacity_stake_per_gflop`, the revocation and surge descriptions), then
     `just verify-params`, the simulator mirror and `check_params.py`;
   - the miner-worker bond sizing and faucet sponsor sizing, which mirror the runtime formula and
     move with it;
   - the Lean `stake_covers_settlement_exposure` statement and the Quint miner-staking spec;
   - §6.1, Appendices A and C.1, and the workbook (Projection Input §2), which should use calibrated
     `B_p` instead of peak spec;
   - the published surfaces that quote 7,560 FLOP per G_n/s: the knowledge pages, the intro and
     params pages, the onboarding docs and the runbooks.

**Rejected:**

- *Keep the runtime formula.* Hardware-scale caps cannot be bonded.
- *Keep `k = 0.01`.* It deters only above about 4.8e9 G_n/s credited at `c = 0.9`.
- *Ratify `k = 0.25` now.* Bondability and conditional thresholds do not establish deterrence while
  `c` is unmeasured (2026-10 consult).
- *Settlement-exposure sizing (cap × window × price per G_n).* The model-registry base price
  (0.003 FLOP/G_n) is devnet-scale: at that price a week of 8× H200 service bills about 5.7B FLOP. It
  needs its own recalibration, and settlement exposure is bounded by escrow and aggregate cap
  enforcement, not by this bond.
- *A cap or sublinear curve.* Unnecessary, since the gain is bounded by `P·W`, and D-0418 rejects it.

**Owner decision requested:**

1. Ratify the unit and window placement now (items 1–2).
2. Adopt `k = 0.25` as the candidate to validate, or name another; 0.05 and 1.0 are tabulated in the
   analysis.
3. Approve the security envelope and gates (item 4), which fund the adversarial analysis that sets
   `k`.

**Specification:** §6.1, Appendices A, C.1, E.33, H; `params/flop-protocol-params.yaml`
(`miner_capacity_stake_per_gflop`, `miner_stake_revocation_latency_blocks`,
`miner_stake_surge_multiplier_ppm`); analysis.

## D-0533 — Model-registry base price per G_n: a reference set against emission at a reference throughput

**Status:** proposal pending ratification (follow-up to #1361 and D-0532). Nothing changes in the
runtime, `params/` or published surfaces until the owner ratifies the method and a later decision
sets the value at activation.

**Context.** `ModelRegistryBasePricePerGFLOP` = 0.003 FLOP per `G_n` was sized for devnet throughput
(about 1e3 `G_n/s` network-wide). Its only stated derivation is a simulator input denominated in USD
per GFLOP (analysis).

Real-inference calibration (#2850) puts caps near 1e5–1e7 `G_n/s` per host. At 0.003, one 8× H200
host at 40% of peak bills about 9,500 FLOP/s. That is 132× the whole era-0 miner pot, or 5.74B FLOP
per week against 18.47B FLOP issued by the end of era 6.

The price does four jobs today:
- it is the gateway's de facto bill: gateway escrow is estimated `G_n` × epoch price, and a
  cooperative settle pays the whole escrow;
- it sets the `certificate_settle` floor;
- it is every new model's default price;
- base/10 floors publisher overrides.

The market factor is inert: `Permill::from_percent(200)` saturates at 100%, so the epoch price
always equals the model's price.

**Decision (proposed):**

1. **Method (to ratify).** The base price is a FLOP-denominated reference, not a peg:
   `p_ref = φ · P_m(e) / T_ref`. It is the era-`e` miner emission per credited `G_n` at a reference
   network throughput `T_ref`, scaled by `φ`, the ratio of gross fees to the miner emission pot.
   - No exchange rate enters.
   - The emission rate is the design reference D-0532 uses. Channel payouts do not earn it today
     (E.44).
2. **Benchmark (to validate, not ratified).**
   - The inputs are `φ = 1` and `T_ref` = 500 H100-equivalent units (the workbook's testnet T3 basis)
     × 740,140 `G_n/s` × 40% = 1.48e8 `G_n/s`.
   - That gives 4.86e-7, so the benchmark is **5e-7 FLOP per `G_n`** (`VFY / 2_000_000`,
     500,000,000,000 base units).
   - `T_ref` is a scenario, not measured throughput. The parity price scales as `1/u` (1.95e-6 at
     10% utilization). On a PCIe-equivalent unit instead of the measured SXM unit, it is about
     6.4e-7.
   - At 5e-7: a 70B-class 1k-token request costs 0.07 FLOP, an 8× H200 host-week bills 0.96M FLOP,
     and the publisher floor is 5e-8.
3. **Bounds on `φ` (analysis, not ratified).** Let `m` be the miner share of a settlement payment:
   0.85 under ratified D-0525, 0.99 in today's runtime.
   - **Bond.** If D-0532's envelope counts settlement gain at `p_ref`, the bond must cover
     `(1 + mφ)·k_min(T_ref)`. Holding that to 1.0 FLOP per `G_n/s` at `c = 0.9` gives
     `φ ≤ ~2`.
   - **Wash demand.** Fee leakage deters it only at `φ ≥ 1/(1−m)` (6.7 to 100), and only if credit
     checked the price paid, which it does not. The base price makes no claim against wash demand;
     that stays with E.49.
4. **Activation (a separate owner decision).** The activated value cites:
   - measured eligible credited throughput (indexer realized index, settled `G_n`) and #2850 caps;
   - the reward and fee split live at activation (E.44 credit, D-0525 `m`);
   - migration of existing `avg` and epoch prices, outstanding gateway quotes and open certificate
     channels;
   - minimum-escrow, existential-deposit and rounding boundaries;
   - audit funding at measured claim frequency and cost.

   The value is revisited at each halving (`φ` doubles at constant throughput), or when realized
   credited throughput leaves `[T_ref/10, 10·T_ref]`.
5. **D-0532 coupling.**
   - Settlement exposure stays bounded by escrow and aggregate cap enforcement, as D-0532 states.
   - The adversarial analysis that sets D-0532's `k` adds a settlement term: `m × (escrow a miner can
     collect in W)`. That counts gateway estimates, publisher prices above `p_ref`, and contract
     prices. At exactly `p_ref` it equals `m·φ·k_min(T_ref)` per `G_n/s` (0.29–0.33 at the benchmark,
     `c = 0.9`), and it does not fall as throughput grows.
   - The base-price change and D-0532 activation ship in the same upgrade or in a fixed order.
     Otherwise hardware-scale caps would meet a 0.003 gateway bill.
6. **Coupled items (each a separate owner decision):**
   - `rate_G` and `channel_base_per_turn` (E.24), today 1e-18 FLOP per `G_n`, with `p_ref` as the
     natural reference;
   - audit funding (earmark, `audit_fee_per_turn`, claim frequency), which must stay solvent without
     lowering `c`;
   - the simulator mirror, which becomes a FLOP-denominated input.
7. **Alignment, after activation:**
   - the runtime constant and `param_consts.rs`;
   - `#[pallet::constant]` exposure;
   - the market-factor unit fix (or removal of `MaxMarketFactorPercent`);
   - promotion into `params/flop-protocol-params.yaml` with a `check_params.py` entry, then
     `just verify-params`;
   - a reset of every registered model's `avg` and epoch price;
   - the docs that quote 0.003.

**Rejected:**

- *Keep 0.003.* One host bills 132× the miner pot, a single 70B-class request exceeds
  `agent_per_tx_limit`, and the base/10 floor blocks hardware-scale publisher prices.
- *Halve the base with emission.* Pegs fees to the subsidy.
- *Size from the bond (`k/(W·σ)`).* Makes the bond the price oracle.
- *Tier multipliers per `G_n`.* `G_n` per token already scales with model size.
- *Ratify 5e-7 now.* `T_ref` is a scenario, and both channel reward credit and the D-0525 split are
  unimplemented (2026-10 consult).
- *Price high enough to deter wash.* Unbondable under D-0532, and ineffective without a price gate on
  credit.

**Owner decision requested:**

1. Ratify the method (item 1).
2. Adopt 5e-7 FLOP per `G_n` as the benchmark to validate, or name another `φ`, `T_ref` or
   utilization.
3. Approve the activation evidence, the D-0532 coupling and the activation order (items 4–5).
